1. The scapegoat is a man in a file

A scapegoat, in the old sense, is the one sent out carrying what the community will not carry. This essay does not find that Australia did that to him. It finds that the public record, as he has published it, is thick with his name and thin with an examination of the thing he asked to have examined.

The person in the files is Dr Richard William McLean. The name he publishes under is Barran Dodger. The archive’s itemised reading of the offices, dated 28 October 2026, begins with a rule that this essay adopts: “A reply is not an investigation. A referral is not help.” Only links that open are used (The responses, 2026).

On 27 March 2023 a letter on Federal Court paper, read in that itemised account as the work of Scott Tredwell, General Counsel, not a judge, recorded that he was satisfied McLean was or had been an employee of the Department of Social Services. The same reading says the letter named three categories — perverting the course of justice, maladministration, and danger to health or safety — assumed the conduct was disclosable for the purpose of the assessment, and said that no further action would be taken. Employment was acknowledged. The disclosure was not actioned. The PDF is the assessment of 27 March 2023, and a second file of the same date is the final assessment (The responses, 2026). Naming a category is not a finding that the category was proved. Refusing to go further is not a finding that the disclosure was false. Both limits are in the archive’s own lexicon: a non-allocation “proves the decision not to investigate. It does not prove the disclosure was false, and it does not prove the office committed a crime” (The lexicon, 2026).

That is the shape of the scapegoat in the documents. He is identified. The harm he named is written down as a category. The investigation he asked for is the thing the letter does not do.

4. The reckoning is the publication of the record

If there is a reckoning that does not require a believer, it is this: the letters are no longer only in the offices that wrote them. They are in a public repository under his account, and on this site, with dates and names attached. The offices had the letterhead. The archive has the copy. A copy is not a conviction. A copy is a way for a third person to read what was written without asking the office for permission to see its own sentence.

5. What is not in the equation

He is not removed from the story. He is the author of the letters’ occasion and of the gospel. What is removed is the machine’s loyalty. The government is not believed because it is the government. He is not believed because he has suffered, or because he has written a psalm. A document is believed, for what it is, when it can be opened. An allegation remains an allegation until a competent body, or a reader who has done the work, says otherwise. This page is not that body.

The witness remains, in his phrase. The record remains, in the files. The evidence must be examined, which is a task this essay starts and does not finish.
