PART IV — THE QUESTIONS OF INTENTION

  • I want the intentions behind the treatment I describe to be examined, rather than having each incident considered in isolation.

The central issue is whether the decisions, actions and omissions affecting me were unrelated events, mistakes, negligence, or procedurally justified decisions—or whether the evidence establishes deliberate retaliation, suppression, exclusion, or coordinated conduct.

The possible purposes requiring examination include the following.

1. Exclusion

  • Was the conduct intended, or knowingly allowed, to drive me out of my home, employment, community, or ordinary social life?

2. Discrediting

  • Were actions or statements used to undermine my reputation, credibility, professional standing, or the credibility of my disclosures?

3. Silencing

  • Were my attempts to report alleged wrongdoing, make public-interest disclosures, or seek accountability discouraged or obstructed?

4. Denial of protection

  • Were opportunities to provide protection disregarded, and were the risks I reported adequately considered?

5. Loss of independence

  • Did decisions concerning employment, property, finances, housing, disability support, or personal autonomy undermine my ability to live independently?

6. Isolation

  • Did the cumulative effect of the decisions and omissions leave me without the social, professional, legal, or institutional support necessary to challenge my circumstances?

7. Administrative exhaustion

  • Was I left repeatedly pursuing different agencies, submitting complaints, seeking records, and trying to obtain answers without a satisfactory resolution?

8. Avoidance of accountability

  • Were decisions, actions, omissions, or their consequences inadequately scrutinised or explained?

9. Fragmentation of the record

  • Did the separation of my circumstances across multiple institutions prevent the full history from being properly examined?

10. Continuation of detriment

  • Were known adverse circumstances allowed to continue without appropriate review, intervention, or remedy?

The overarching question is whether the cumulative effect of these matters was to marginalise me, undermine my credibility and independence, obstruct my disclosures, and leave me without effective protection or redress.

Intent must be established through evidence. Relevant material may include communications, instructions, decision records, contemporaneous notes, stated reasons, procedural departures, information-sharing records, and evidence showing what particular decision-makers knew at the relevant time.

  • I seek an independent assessment that identifies both supporting and contrary evidence, rather than assuming that every adverse outcome proves deliberate coordination.

PART V — DOCUMENTARY RECORD AND EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

The purpose of this master statement is to organise the allegations and consequences I want examined against the documentary record.

My Barrandodger repository contains material relating to government correspondence, disclosure decisions, oversight complaints, disability support, housing, guardianship, financial administration, health-related matters, legal assistance, and other institutional dealings.

The existence of a document does not necessarily establish every allegation associated with it. Each record must be examined for what it actually says, who created it, when it was created, what decision it records, what evidence it relies on, and what remains unresolved.

For every significant allegation, the record should identify:

Claim ID: A unique reference number.

Allegation: The precise conduct, decision, omission, or consequence being raised.

Person or institution: The person, agency, service provider, or organisation concerned.

Date or period: When the alleged event occurred or the relevant decision was made.

Source: The original document, email, letter, recording, webpage, or other evidence.

Precise location: The page, paragraph, timestamp, correspondence entry, or other identifiable passage.

Exact wording: A direct quotation where appropriate, or a clearly identified summary.

  • What the record establishes: The facts that can reasonably be drawn from the document.
  • What the record does not establish: Any conclusion that would go beyond the document.

Related records: Other documents that corroborate, contradict, qualify, or contextualise the matter.

Response: Any explanation, determination, denial, or response from the institution concerned.

Outstanding issue: The question that remains unresolved.

Loss or consequence: The harm or loss claimed and the evidence relevant to it.

Review or remedy: The investigation, correction, assistance, compensation, or other outcome sought.

Original evidence must remain preserved. Website presentation may change, but the underlying records must not be altered.

Where a document is incomplete, inaccessible, redacted, or unavailable, that limitation should be recorded rather than filled with inference. Where an allegation is disputed, the dispute should be stated.
