Forensic Probability & Verification Framework

Added to the Barran Dodger Archive to increase transparency, explicitness of assumptions, and independent examinability of claims of improbability and institutional pattern.

Purpose

This page supplies a consistent methodology so that statements about “nearly zero probability of survival,” systemic targeting, and related inferences can be assessed by independent readers. It does not convert any claim into a proven fact. It makes the archive more complete as a finding aid.

1. Survival Probability Claim — Explicit Form

The archive records the interpretation that survival under the described conditions over more than 35 years is “nearly zero.” For this interpretation to be examinable:

  • Define the outcome: what events would have constituted non-survival or ordinary expected outcome.
  • List each alleged adverse event with linked primary documents (official letters, medical records, court filings).
  • State baseline rates from comparable populations or historical data, if any are used.
  • Declare independence or dependence assumptions between events.

Under the present record the claim remains an interpretation resting on first-person testimony plus selected documents. Alternative models (ordinary administrative failure, independent misfortune, or partial success of remedies) remain open until quantified evidence is supplied and weighted.

2. Document Verification Protocol

  • Re-compute SHA-256 hashes of downloaded files and compare with published values.
  • Check OpenTimestamps or Bitcoin anchoring receipts where present.
  • Examine Git commit history for addition dates and subsequent changes.
  • Note provenance statements attached to each item.

Discrepancies should be reported so the public record can be corrected.

3. Claim Classification Standard

  • Documented event — supported by contemporaneous official correspondence, court records or medical records linked in the archive.
  • Testimony — first-person account not independently corroborated in the public record.
  • Interpretation / inference — conclusions (including probability assessments and motive attributions) drawn from the above.
  • Spiritual / prophetic statement — expressions of faith, not offered as empirical claims.

4. Legal and Intellectual Effect

This framework:

  • Preserves the distinction between evidence and conclusion.
  • Makes assumptions explicit so they can be challenged or refined.
  • Supports independent examination by researchers, journalists, lawyers or oversight bodies.
  • Reduces the risk of selective reading by supplying a consistent methodology.

The archive remains a finding aid. Readers must form their own conclusions. Nothing on this page constitutes legal advice or a determination of fact.
