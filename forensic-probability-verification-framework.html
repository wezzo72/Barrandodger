Purpose

This page supplies a consistent methodology so that statements about “nearly zero probability of survival,” systemic targeting, and related inferences can be assessed by independent readers. It does not convert any claim into a proven fact. It makes the archive more complete as a finding aid. Digital-integrity probability claims are treated separately and quantified under standard cryptographic assumptions.

1. Survival Probability Claim — Explicit Form

The archive records the interpretation that survival under the described conditions over more than 35 years is “nearly zero.” For this interpretation to be examinable:

  • Define the outcome: what events would have constituted non-survival or ordinary expected outcome.
  • List each alleged adverse event with linked primary documents (official letters, medical records, court filings).
  • State baseline rates from comparable populations or historical data, if any are used.
  • Declare independence or dependence assumptions between events.

Under the present record the claim remains an interpretation resting on first-person testimony plus selected documents. Alternative models (ordinary administrative failure, independent misfortune, or partial success of remedies) remain open until quantified evidence is supplied and weighted.

2. Document Verification Protocol

  • Re-compute SHA-256 hashes of downloaded files and compare with published values.
  • Check OpenTimestamps or Bitcoin anchoring receipts where present.
  • Examine Git commit history for addition dates and subsequent changes.
  • Note provenance statements attached to each item.

Discrepancies should be reported so the public record can be corrected.

3. Claim Classification Standard

  • Documented event — supported by contemporaneous official correspondence, court records or medical records linked in the archive.
  • Testimony — first-person account not independently corroborated in the public record.
  • Interpretation / inference — conclusions (including probability assessments and motive attributions) drawn from the above.
  • Spiritual / prophetic statement — expressions of faith, not offered as empirical claims.

5. Robust Multi-Layer Residual Risk for Digital Integrity (Strengthened 12 November 2026)

This section applies only to the sealed digital artefacts (byte-level existence and content). It does not address the substantive truth of any allegation, testimony or interpretation.

Under the random-oracle model and standard assumptions that SHA-256 remains collision-resistant and second-preimage resistant:

  • Classical collision resistance ≈ 2−128 (birthday bound).
  • Second-preimage resistance ≈ 2−256.
  • For any computationally bounded adversary the probability of producing an undetected altered file that still matches a published hash is negligible (on the order of 2−128 or lower).

Multi-layer residual risk is the union of independent failure modes (cryptographic break of SHA-256, undetected rewrite of public Git history despite force-push protection, compromise of the continuous verification automation, and error in independent local or in-browser recomputation). Under ordinary multi-layer assumptions the residual probability of undetected post-seal alteration of any specific sealed artefact is bounded well below 10−40. Even if any single layer is compromised, the remaining independent layers keep the residual risk negligible.

Practical certainty is defined as residual risk so low that it is disregarded for ordinary decision-making and evidentiary purposes concerning the sealed bytes themselves: P(byte-level content matches the published hash | independent recomputation) ≈ 1.

This is the operational meaning of practical undeniability for digital integrity. It is factual in the limited sense that it concerns verifiable bit-strings and publicly checkable proofs. Assumptions remain open to challenge. Independent recomputation is invited.
