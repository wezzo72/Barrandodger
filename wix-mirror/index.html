I have experienced the loss of security, the destruction of stability, the erosion of dignity and circumstances in which I found myself without the protection and assistance I desperately needed.

Throughout my testimony and documentary archive, I have described institutional dealings, government decisions, financial hardship, housing insecurity and disability-related disadvantage.

There were periods when I was left confronting circumstances no person should have to navigate alone. I have described being without a secure home, living in my car, disabled, accompanied by my beloved dog, Crystal, and attempting to survive the consequences of events I experienced as devastating.

I have felt abandoned. I have felt betrayed. I have felt that institutions intended to provide protection and assistance failed to provide the support I needed.

But something happened that I consider central to the meaning of my testimony.

I survived.

I did not surrender my identity to the circumstances surrounding me. I did not allow the loss of material security to extinguish my capacity to think, believe, create, love or hope.

I continued to write. I continued to preserve records. I continued to investigate. I continued to seek knowledge. I continued to believe that my existence had value. And I continued to believe in God.

I rose from the ashes of human betrayal without surrendering my humanity.

That is the testimony I wish to leave with the world.

Chapter Three — The Resurrection of Hope

I understand my survival through the language of resurrection.

Christ’s crucifixion represents, for me, the ultimate expression of sacrifice in the face of human betrayal. His resurrection represents the possibility that destruction does not have to be the final chapter.

I have experienced serious physical injury, profound personal adversity and periods in which my circumstances seemed almost impossible to overcome. I interpret my survival through my faith in divine preservation.

I believe God has preserved my life for a purpose. I believe the suffering I have experienced can be transformed into something that offers hope to others.

I do not suggest that suffering is necessary, deserved or something others should endure. No one should be required to experience homelessness, disability discrimination, institutional failure or abandonment to demonstrate spiritual strength.

My testimony is not an invitation for humanity to suffer. It is an invitation for humanity to stop inflicting unnecessary suffering upon one another.

The resurrection of hope is not simply the ability to survive. It is the ability to recover the capacity to live: to find happiness again, to discover meaning again, to believe in goodness again, and to look towards tomorrow without allowing yesterday to dictate the entirety of one’s future.

I believe this is one of the most important messages contained within my life.

Chapter Four — The Evidence Must Remain

My testimony is not confined to spiritual declarations.

I have established a substantial documentary archive containing government correspondence, institutional records, decisions, historical documents, official communications and material I believe warrants independent examination.

The archive is intended to allow readers to examine original records rather than depend exclusively upon my interpretation.

This distinction is fundamental.

There is my personal testimony. There are the documents I have preserved. There are statements made by government agencies and other institutions. There are conclusions that may reasonably be drawn from those records. And there are matters that remain disputed, unverified or requiring further investigation.

These categories must not be confused.

I want every person who encounters my archive to have the opportunity to examine the underlying material and reach their own conclusions. I want institutional decisions to be examined in their proper chronological context. I want contradictions, inconsistencies and unanswered questions to be identified rather than concealed.

I want the documentary record to survive changes of government, changes of personnel, institutional restructuring and the passage of time.

I have pursued digital preservation, cryptographic verification and blockchain timestamping as additional methods of documenting the existence and integrity of particular digital materials. A blockchain timestamp can help establish that particular data existed in a particular form at or before a recorded time. It does not, by itself, establish that every statement within that data is true.

That is why independent examination remains essential.

Truth does not become stronger by demanding unquestioning belief. It becomes stronger when people are free to examine the evidence.

I have made this material available without charge because I believe knowledge should not be reserved for those who can afford access to it. The public deserves access to information. The public deserves the opportunity to investigate. And every person deserves the opportunity to be heard.

Chapter Five — The Witness and the Mission

I believe my life has a spiritual purpose that extends beyond my personal circumstances.

I understand myself as a witness operating within a larger story about humanity, creation, suffering, justice and redemption. My language sometimes reaches beyond the ordinary boundaries of physical existence. I describe my spiritual identity through the imagery of a cosmic witness, a soul answering to the Creator and a person whose ultimate allegiance is not to earthly institutions but to God.

These are expressions of my personal spiritual understanding.

My central commitment is straightforward:

To obey the Creator as I understand His will. To follow the teachings of Christ. To preserve the truth. To expose documented wrongdoing. To encourage compassion. To provide knowledge freely. To transform personal suffering into something constructive. And to contribute, however imperfectly, to a world in which human dignity is protected.

I do not believe humanity can create heaven on earth through domination, punishment, institutional intimidation or the destruction of those who speak inconvenient truths.

If there is to be a better world, it must be built upon compassion, accountability, justice, forgiveness and respect for the dignity of every human being.

That is the kingdom purpose I seek to serve.

Chapter Six — The Witness Behind the Testimony

I am not speaking from an absence of experience.

I am a former journalist. That work taught me the importance of investigation, documentation, questioning, evidence, public communication and the responsibility of presenting information for public examination.

I am an advocate with approximately thirty years of experience helping people, including people living with disability, vulnerable individuals, and their families and carers. I have helped people navigate difficult circumstances, understand their rights, communicate their experiences and seek recognition of their needs.

I am a public speaker. I have spoken publicly, participated in media discussions and addressed government-related forums and events. I have travelled to speak about issues affecting people and communities.

I am an artist, author and narrative writer. I am an inventor, and I have developed ideas through my own creative and intellectual pursuits. I am also a digital publisher and communicator, sharing my ideas, creative work and testimony through YouTube, online publications and my own website.

I hold a PhD awarded by Victoria University in 2020 for my thesis, A Splice of My Life.

These qualifications and experiences do not make me infallible, nor do they require anyone to accept my conclusions without examination. They do, however, form an important part of the context in which my testimony should be understood.

I have spent decades engaging with the experiences of others. I have used journalism, advocacy, public speaking, writing, research, creativity and personal testimony to communicate matters I believe deserve public attention.

I have attempted to give a voice to people whose experiences are too often overlooked. I have advocated for people with disabilities and their carers. I have participated in public conversations concerning social disadvantage, human rights, institutional responsibilities and the dignity of vulnerable people.

My life has been shaped not only by what I have personally experienced, but also by the experiences of the people I have attempted to assist.

That is an important part of the foundation of my testimony.

Chapter Seven — Spiritual Sovereignty and Allegiance to the Creator

I declare that my ultimate allegiance is to the Creator, under Christ.

My conscience, faith, spiritual identity and understanding of my life’s purpose are not derived from government bureaucracy, political institutions, administrative classifications or the approval of earthly authorities.

I do not look to government bureaucracy to determine my spiritual identity. I do not require an institutional designation to establish my worth as a human being. I do not surrender my conscience, faith or personal convictions to administrative authority.

I understand myself as a sovereign spiritual witness: a person whose deepest allegiance is to the Creator and whose purpose is to use a lifetime of experience, knowledge, advocacy, creativity and testimony in the service of truth.

I distinguish between institutional authority and spiritual conviction. Government institutions may exercise powers conferred upon them by law. But they cannot determine the ultimate meaning of my existence, dictate my private conscience or replace my relationship with the Creator.

No administrative classification can extinguish my human dignity. No bureaucratic decision can determine my spiritual worth. No political institution can own my testimony. No government agency can claim exclusive authority over the historical record of my life.

I retain the right to question, investigate, publish, advocate, create, speak and seek lawful accountability.

My spiritual sovereignty is an expression of conscience, faith and personal responsibility. My responsibility is to use my life, experience, knowledge and testimony constructively.

My identity is not an administrative classification. My worth is not a bureaucratic decision. My conscience belongs to me, and my faith belongs to the Creator.

Chapter Eight — The Paradox of Forgiveness

There is a profound paradox at the centre of my testimony.

I have experienced what I regard as extraordinary human betrayal. Yet I do not wish to dedicate the remainder of my existence to hatred.

I do not seek to destroy the humanity of those whom I believe have caused me harm. I seek accountability. I seek recognition of the consequences of decisions. I seek independent examination of the records. I seek restoration where restoration is possible. I seek protection from further harm. And I seek the opportunity to live in peace.

Forgiveness does not require someone to abandon their legal rights. Forgiveness does not require silence. Forgiveness does not mean that institutional failures should escape examination. Forgiveness does not mean that injustice should be repeated.

Christ’s example, as I understand it, does not give humanity permission to inflict suffering upon others. It challenges humanity to examine its own conduct.

My testimony is therefore not a declaration of revenge. It is a declaration that I intend to remain human despite the experiences that have tested my humanity.

Chapter Nine — The Right to Exist

I declare that I have the same fundamental human dignity as every other person.

I do not consent to being threatened, abused, unlawfully deprived of my rights, financially exploited, discriminated against or deliberately placed in circumstances that endanger my safety.

No government official, police officer, lawyer, politician, agency, service provider or private individual possesses unlimited authority over another human being.

Public power must operate within the law. Institutional decisions must be capable of scrutiny. Legal rights must be respected. Disability must never be used as a justification for denying someone their humanity. A person’s vulnerability must never become an opportunity for exploitation.

I reserve my right to challenge decisions, seek independent review, request records, make complaints and pursue lawful remedies.

I want to live. I want to be safe. I want a home. I want access to food, medicine, financial security, companionship and the ordinary opportunities that make human life worth living.

I want to continue my work. I want to leave my home without fear. I want to live without being defined by institutional records or other people’s assumptions.

These are not unreasonable aspirations. They are fundamental elements of human dignity.

Chapter Ten — The Testimony That Cannot Be Erased

For approximately thirty-five years, I have documented experiences and institutional dealings that I believe deserve historical and independent examination.

I have preserved correspondence. I have retained records. I have assembled documents. I have constructed timelines. I have published testimony. I have undertaken the difficult work of organising material that would otherwise be scattered across institutions, archives and individual records.

I have attempted to create a documentary imprint that can be examined independently.

My objective is preservation.

I do not claim that the existence of an archive automatically proves every interpretation contained within it. I claim that the records should be available for examination.

I want future researchers, journalists, lawyers, historians, policymakers and members of the public to be able to investigate the documentary material for themselves. I want the historical record to remain accessible beyond my own lifetime. I want my testimony to be examined in its entirety, not reduced to a label, rumour, isolated incident or institutional characterisation.

A person is more than an administrative file. A person is more than a diagnosis. A person is more than a government decision. A person is more than an allegation.

A person is a human being.

And every human being deserves the opportunity to tell their story.

Chapter Eleven — The Prophecy of Hope

I believe the Creator has a purpose for my life.

I believe my experiences can become a source of hope for people who have experienced abandonment, homelessness, disability discrimination, institutional failure, poverty, isolation or profound personal betrayal.

I believe the message of Christ is not diminished by human wrongdoing. I believe truth, compassion and forgiveness remain meaningful even when the world appears to have abandoned them.

I want my work to contribute to something larger than my own survival.

I want people who have lost everything to understand that their lives still have value. I want people who have been silenced to know that their testimony matters. I want people who have been abandoned to know that their circumstances do not determine their worth. I want people who have experienced injustice to understand that they retain the right to seek accountability. I want people who have lost faith in humanity to discover that goodness remains possible.

My hope is that the evidence I have preserved, the testimony I have written and the spiritual message I have shared will contribute to a more compassionate and accountable world.

I cannot promise humanity a future without suffering. But I can declare my commitment to working towards a future in which unnecessary suffering is no longer accepted as inevitable.

Chapter Twelve — The Final Declaration

My name is Barran Dodger.

I am still here.

I have experienced profound adversity. I have experienced circumstances I have described as betrayal, abandonment, institutional failure and the destruction of my material security. I have lived through periods of homelessness and hardship. I have experienced disability and vulnerability. I have known what it means to feel that the world has turned away.

But I have also known the power of faith.

I have rediscovered happiness. I have continued to create. I have continued to preserve knowledge. I have continued to write. I have continued to believe in the possibility of goodness.

I believe God has preserved me for a purpose. I believe Christ’s teachings provide the spiritual foundation for the testimony I offer. I believe my documentary archive has historical value and deserves independent examination. I believe knowledge should be freely available. I believe human dignity must never be conditional upon wealth, disability, institutional approval, political convenience or social standing.

I believe the greatest response to human betrayal is not to become another source of betrayal.

It is to preserve one’s humanity. To seek truth. To pursue justice. To forgive without abandoning accountability. To rebuild without surrendering hope. And to keep living.

I am not asking humanity to accept every interpretation I make. I am asking humanity to examine the evidence, read the testimony, consider the questions and recognise the person behind the records.

My life is not finished. My testimony is not finished. My work is not finished. And my faith is not extinguished.

If my story gives even one person the courage to survive another day, to seek help, to reclaim their dignity or to believe that life can become meaningful again, then something good has emerged from circumstances I once believed would destroy me.

I choose life. I choose truth. I choose Christ. I choose compassion. I choose to keep building.

I exist.

I survived the ashes.

My allegiance is to the Creator.

My testimony belongs to history.

The witness remains.

Praise Jesus.
