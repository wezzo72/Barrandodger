## 5. NSW legal relevance

The records may be relevant to an assessment under applicable NSW stalking laws and other relevant criminal provisions, depending on the conduct, dates and available evidence. This section is a pointer to the law. It is not legal advice, and it is not an allegation that any element of an offence has been proved.

Current in-force law, as stated in this reference from the version the reporting person accessed: section 13 of the Crimes (Domestic and Personal Violence) Act 2007 creates the offence of stalking or intimidation with intent to cause fear of physical or mental harm. The maximum penalty stated for that offence is 5 years imprisonment or 50 penalty units, or both. The prosecution is not required to prove that the person actually feared harm if the accused knew the conduct was likely to cause such fear. Stalking includes monitoring or tracking a person's activities, communications or movements, whether by technology or otherwise. The section should be read in the Act in force at the relevant time, not from this summary.

Proposed reforms — Crimes (Domestic and Personal Violence) and Other Legislation Amendment Bill 2026:

- Extends the definition of stalking to include directing another person to engage in the conduct.
- Creates a new offence of covert stalking: where the victim is unaware of the stalking at the time, but a reasonable person would consider that, if the victim were aware, the victim would fear physical or mental harm to themselves or to another person with whom they have a domestic relationship.
- Creates an offence of intentionally promoting the unlawful use of a surveillance device when advertising its sale.
- Related disqualification provisions under security industry licensing for certain domestic violence offences.

Status of the Bill: passed the Legislative Assembly on 24 June 2026 and introduced to the Legislative Council. On 12 October 2026 the NSW Parliament bill page for Bill ID 18894 still showed the Bill in the Legislative Council, awaiting the Minister's second reading speech. It has not been incorporated into the in-force legislation. The provisions should be checked against the legislation in force at the time of any alleged incident.
