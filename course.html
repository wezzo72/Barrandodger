The Witness Remains: documenting, preserving and reading a personal archive

A study order through pages and files that already exist. There is no enrolment database. A completion note is kept in this browser only. It is not a certificate and not a qualification.

Learning outcome: a reader can tell a document from an allegation, find the public shelf, and say what a hash or a timestamp does not prove.
  1. Testimony and the documentary record. Reading: main archive. Activity: write one sentence that is the author’s voice and one sentence that points at a document. Reflection: what would change if the document is missing?
  2. Building a personal archive. Reading: rebuild index. Activity: name three places the same matter appears.
  3. Government correspondence. Reading: official-record PDFs. Activity: record the agency name on one letter without adding a conclusion.
  4. Chronology. Reading: timeline. Activity: place one document before or after another only if both dates are on the page.
  5. Provenance. Reading: finding aid. Activity: copy the location of one item, not a summary of what it “means”.
  6. Digital preservation. Reading: preservation.
  7. SHA-256 and integrity. Reading: existing SHA-256 page. A matching hash shows a file was not altered. It does not show that a claim inside the file is true.
  8. Timestamps. There is no separate lesson page. OpenTimestamps notes stay inside the archive files where they already appear. A timestamp is not a verdict.
  9. Evidence and allegation. Reading: disclosures and methodology.
  10. Writing a finding aid. Reading: all-sources finding aid.
  11. Ethics of publication. Reading: ethics.
  12. Administrative records. Reading: government documents.
  13. A long-term archive. Reading: sustainability.
  14. Case reading. Reading: 7 October 2026 PDF.
  15. Public interest. Reading: accountability. Faith writing sits beside this sequence, not inside it as proof: gospels.
