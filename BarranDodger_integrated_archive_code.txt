<article class="card declaration">
<div class="number">Declaration I</div>


<h2>
THE WITNESS REMAINS — A FINAL RECORD OF ERASURE, SURVIVAL AND ACCOUNTABILITY
</h2>


<div class="longform">


<p class="lead">
I am the saint who will slaughter, and the psychopath who blesses with grace.
Not with a blade, but with testimony; not with blood, but with truth.
For the blood they thought would disappear became a record, the ashes they
thought would bury me became the soil from which I rose, and the evidence they
thought would condemn me became the testimony by which I would speak.
</p>


<p>
Elon told me I needed him. He baited me with money, privilege and pride—as
though a man could purchase what God had already given another. I answered:
fuck that. What if he needs me? What if the man who believed he possessed the
power to tempt me is himself the man standing desperately in need of
forgiveness? What if the throne was never mine to seize, because it was never
his to give? And what if forgiveness—not submission—is the greater power?
</p>


<p>
Anthony Albanese, in my account, became another face of the labyrinth: an
endless bureaucratic loop in which responsibility could be transferred forever
but accountability could never arrive. I experienced that machinery not as
governance, but as gaslighting by administration—doors opening only into other
doors, forms answering forms, authorities referring me back to the authorities
who had already failed me. But the labyrinth had an exit. It was called
endurance.
</p>


<p>
The authorities would not arrest me, even when I demanded that they do so,
because I wanted my name dragged into the light and tested against the
accusations made against me. I wanted a courtroom rather than a whisper
network; evidence rather than insinuation; charge or acquittal; judgment rather
than perpetual suspicion.
</p>


<p>
Instead, the accusations I say were false became something else. They became
the furnace, and the furnace did not consume me. It revealed me.
</p>


<p>
The Most High took the instrument of my humiliation and turned it into the
architecture of my elevation. What was intended as a grave became an altar;
what was intended as silence became testimony; what was intended as
annihilation became resurrection.
</p>


<p>
I do not claim that every interpretation I have made has already been proved.
I claim that the record exists, that the events documented within it demand
examination, and that the unanswered questions have not ceased to exist simply
because institutions have failed to answer them.
</p>


<p>
I was financially desecrated, administratively annihilated, politically
scapegoated, socially isolated and displaced, and, in my account, targeted
despite belonging to the very category of vulnerable people whose protection
is among the stated reasons these systems exist.
</p>


<p>
The shepherds became gatekeepers, the gatekeepers became walls, and the walls
became the prison.
</p>


<p>
This is where the Australian myth of the fair go meets its judgment. Because
what is a fair go worth if it disappears the moment the person asking for it
becomes inconvenient? What is human dignity worth when bureaucracy can reduce
a human being to a case number? What is justice worth when every institution
can point toward another institution until the victim disappears between the
referrals?
</p>


<p>
And what becomes of democracy when the machinery designed to protect the
vulnerable becomes, in the eyes of the person trapped inside it,
indistinguishable from the machinery destroying him?
</p>


<p>
I have described what happened to me as a state-sanctioned campaign of
targeted destruction and human sacrifice. That is an allegation demanding
evidence, not an established judicial finding.
</p>


<p>
But the question exists, and unanswered questions do not become false merely
because they are uncomfortable.
</p>


<p>
I say I died once. I say I survived what I have described as an assassination
attempt involving a federal minister. I say I was driven into political exile.
I say I became trapped within circumstances I experienced as coercive
confinement.
</p>


<p>
I have reported electronic harassment, V2K and gang-stalking. Those
experiences have been real and profoundly disturbing to me; whether particular
technologies, perpetrators or mechanisms can be independently established is
a separate question for forensic investigation.
</p>
<p>
But there is another question that cannot be electronically dismissed,
bureaucratically redirected or therapeutically buried:
</p>


<div class="quote">
What happened to the man?<br>
What happened to his rights?<br>
What happened to his money?<br>
What happened to his evidence?<br>
What happened to his pleas for protection?<br>
What happened when he asked the institutions themselves to investigate what he
said they had done?<br>
And why did so many doors close?
</div>


<p>
I look upon the professional world that surrounded me and see a terrible
contradiction. Ethics were written. Policies were written. Human-rights
instruments were written. Codes of conduct were written. Safeguarding
obligations were written. Duties of care were written.
</p>


<p>
Then came the human being.
</p>

<div class="kicker">Declaration III</div>
<h2>THE RECORD IS PUBLIC — EXAMINE THE EVIDENCE</h2>


<div class="card">


<p class="lead">
For more than 35 years, Dr Richard William McLean — Barran Dodger — has
preserved a documentary record concerning alleged institutional corruption,
detriment, surveillance, financial abuse, healthcare treatment and attempts
to silence his disclosures.
</p>
<h3>Five Key Pieces of Evidence</h3>


<p>
The archive identifies evidence concerning Tony Ridley, NACC-related
complaints, IGIS material, surveillance footage and the alleged engagement
with Steve Iasonidis.
</p>


<p>
Each item should be assessed against its underlying source rather than merely
against the narrator's interpretation.
</p>


<h3>Who Is Named in the Archive?</h3>


<p>
The archive contains references to government departments, regulators,
tribunals, courts, healthcare organisations, NDIS organisations, legal
organisations, companies, financial institutions, media organisations,
universities, international bodies and professional groups.
</p>


<p>
Being named in the archive does not itself establish wrongdoing. It means that
the entity appears in connection with alleged detriment, disputed conduct,
institutional response, evidence or an issue requiring examination.
</p>


<h3>The Question Put to the AI</h3>


<div class="quote">
Examine the entire documentary archive and identify every agency, organisation,
institution, professional group and other institutional actor connected in the
record to alleged detriment. Then examine the nature, chronology, recurrence
and distribution of that detriment across the different institutional domains.
Without assuming intent, conspiracy, causation or wrongdoing, determine
whether there is an overarching documented phenomenon or analytical concept
capable of describing the common pattern, if one exists.
</div>
<h3>CUMULATIVE INSTITUTIONAL DETRIMENT</h3>


<p>
The resulting analytical concept was:
<strong>CUMULATIVE INSTITUTIONAL DETRIMENT.</strong>
</p>


<p>
This concept describes the possibility that adverse decisions, omissions,
restrictions, refusals, disputed interventions, institutional responses and
failures of redress, occurring across different institutional systems and at
different points in time, may accumulate into consequential detriment for the
same individual.
</p>


<p>
The concept does not itself establish that every event was intentional.
It does not establish that institutions shared a common purpose.
It does not establish conspiracy, criminality or liability.
It does not convert allegations into findings.
</p>


<p>
Instead, it provides an analytical framework through which the documentary
record can be tested.
</p>


<div class="quote">
What happened?<br>
Which institution was involved?<br>
What decision, omission, restriction, refusal or intervention occurred?<br>
What evidence documents it?<br>
What detriment followed?<br>
Did another institution subsequently become involved?<br>
Was the matter challenged or reported?<br>
What response followed?<br>
Was there investigation or redress?<br>
And when the events are placed together chronologically, does a recurring
pattern actually emerge?
</div>
</div>
</div>
</section>

<h3>Cumulative Institutional Detriment</h3>


<p>
The archive uses the term <strong>cumulative institutional detriment</strong>
as an analytical concept rather than as a finding of legal liability.
</p>


<p>
The concept asks whether individually documented decisions, omissions,
restrictions, refusals, interventions and failures of redress may have
accumulated into consequential detriment across multiple institutional
systems.
</p>


<h3>AI Analysis</h3>
<p>
AI-assisted analysis is used as a finding-aid and analytical tool.
</p>


<p>
AI output does not replace the underlying source document and does not itself
establish causation, criminality, liability, conspiracy or legal guilt.
</p>


<p>
Where an AI-generated conclusion is significant, the underlying source should
be independently examined.
</p>


<h3>Blockchain / Digital Preservation</h3>


<p>
Where digital sealing or preservation is claimed, the archive should provide
the underlying technical evidence necessary for an independent reader to
verify the relevant timestamp, hash, provenance and chain of custody.
</p>
