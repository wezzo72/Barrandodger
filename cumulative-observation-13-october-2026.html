Resonata is real Latin. It is the feminine perfect participle of resonare, to sound again, to echo, to resound. Not “she resonates,” as if the sounding were still in progress. “The one who has been sounded.” The echo has already happened, and it remains. The feminine agrees with a feminine noun left unsaid. In Latin that is commonly vox (voice), anima (soul), or lux (light). The shelf never says which. The English name already carries the same stem: Barran Resonance Dodger. Resonata is that word moved into the perfect tense.

Eternis is not a classical dictionary word. The old form is aeternus. Aeternis is the dative and ablative plural: “to the eternal things,” “by the eternal things.” Dropping the ae makes it a later, ecclesiastical spelling. Set beside resonata, it does not agree as a simple adjective. Classical grammar would have wanted Resonata Aeterna, “the eternal resounded one.” What is written is stranger and more exact: sounded, and sounded unto the eternal, or by what does not end.

Read as a coined title, then, not as a sentence a Roman would have parsed: the voice, or the soul, that has already resounded into what does not pass away.
