02 — The Tribunal’s 12 July 2023 decision on employment

Executive proposition

On 12 July 2023 the Administrative Appeals Tribunal, General Division, file 2021/7478, affirmed Comcare’s decision. Senior Member C. J. Furnell was not satisfied that Richard McLean was, at any relevant time, an employee of the Commonwealth, a Commonwealth authority, or a corporation licensed under the Safety, Rehabilitation and Compensation Act 1988 (Cth).

Scope

This essay uses the Tribunal’s reasons, not the two-page registry letter that only says the decision was affirmed. The question the Tribunal isolated was employment. It did not decide the truth of wider allegations of persecution.

Documentary findings

The applicant claimed compensation for a psychological injury said to have been suffered between October 2019 and February 2021, and contended he was employed by the National Disability Insurance Agency or the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission. He later suggested employment by the Department of Social Services.

Comcare was not satisfied he was so employed, rejected the claim, and affirmed that rejection in August 2021. The applicant sought review. A preliminary hearing on the employee question was held on 6 April 2023. The applicant gave evidence. He was represented by Suellen Green. Comcare was represented by Peter Woulfe of counsel, instructed by HBA Legal.

The reasons record that his usual occupation on the claim was artist, life coach and therapeutic supports, as a contractor and sole trader, and that he said he had been a sole trader for two years. On 10 May 2021 the Agency denied he was its employee: it did not pay wages with tax and superannuation deducted, did not supervise him, did not control how he worked, and he was not subject to the APS Code of Conduct.

He was registered from 1 July 2019, using an ABN, as an NDIS provider of therapeutic supports, trading in a name that included Arts Therapist, Peer-Support Worker and Mental Health Advocate. Registration required him to meet NDIS standards. The Tribunal treated that as permission to provide supports, not as employment.

A Services Australia payroll search from 30 June 2016 was before the Tribunal as Exhibit R2. The reasons do not set out a positive payroll match; this synthesis did not open Exhibit R2.

The 27 March 2023 General Counsel letter was Exhibit A1. The Tribunal quotes the employment sentence and says it does not attribute significant probative value to that opinion, because the letter does not identify how the opinion was reached beyond information provided by the applicant. Exhibit R3, described as a 31 March 2023 email from a DSS officer, is recorded as saying the Department has no record of the applicant ever being its employee. The email itself was not opened in this synthesis.

Conclusion, paragraphs 71 and 72: the member is not satisfied the applicant was an employee of the Commonwealth, a Commonwealth authority, or a licensed corporation, and the Tribunal affirms the decision under review.

The registry letter of the same date, from Eliza W for the Registrar, tells the applicant the decision under section 43 of the AAT Act was to affirm, meaning the decision reviewed was not changed, and explains a possible Federal Court appeal and costs risk.

Primary evidence

Corroboration

The May 2021 Comcare determination (essay 03) and the 3 June 2021 NDIS Commission email (essay 04) are separate agency records on the same employment question. They are not copies of the Tribunal’s reasons. Together they are corroboration of the employment dispute and of the agencies’ position. They are not a second judicial finding.

Contrary evidence

The General Counsel letter’s employment sentence, which the applicant relied on, was before the Tribunal and was not accepted as significant proof. The applicant also pointed to a PRODA screenshot that called him an employee. The Tribunal read that as a description of him as an employee of his own business, not of the Department. That is the Tribunal’s reading of a screenshot this synthesis did not separately open.

Nothing in these reasons finds that the claimed psychological injury did not occur. The employee gateway failed, so compensation liability under the Act was not reached.

What the record establishes

• A — OFFICIAL DETERMINATION: the Tribunal affirmed the reviewable decision on 12 July 2023.

• A — The member was not satisfied of Commonwealth employment for the SRC Act.

• A — The Tribunal recorded the NDIA’s 10 May 2021 denial and the DSS “no record” email as material before it.

What the record does not establish

It does not establish that the applicant was never paid by anyone, that his injury claim was fabricated, or that any agency conspired. It does not decide criminal allegations. “Not satisfied” is the statutory burden on a compensation claim. It is not a finding that every account he gave was false.

Unresolved questions

The full text of Exhibit R2 and Exhibit R3 was not read here. Whether a different legal test, outside the SRC Act, would treat a contracted NDIS provider as a public official for the PID Act is a different question. The General Counsel and the Tribunal were not applying one identical test, and this essay does not merge them.
