Documentary findings

The extract is dated 18 May 2026, our ref C-2336089, from Tanya Bruning, Director Client Service and Compliance, to Mr Richard McLean (Barran Dodger) at richarddrawsstuff@gmail.com.

It says that on 12 May 2026 he emailed a Legal Aid NSW solicitor that he had recorded the advice appointment that day and published it on YouTube. It says posting the video without the solicitor’s knowledge or consent breaches Legal Aid standards of courtesy and fairness.

It states that under section 7 of the Surveillance Devices Act 2007 (NSW) it is a criminal offence to record a private conversation without the other party’s consent, with a maximum penalty of up to 5 years. That is the letter stating the offence provision. It is not a court conviction. No charge, plea, or sentence is recorded in the extract.

It says YouTube was notified that the solicitor did not consent, and it asks that the video be removed by 5pm on Monday 25 May 2026, failing which further action is intended. Whether the video was removed was not checked in this scan.

It says emails included statements that the solicitor “should watch her back,” and that because of this a contact limit was imposed: writing only, polite language, responses only if Legal Aid decides a response is necessary, no visits to offices, no approaches to solicitors at court or conferences, calls will be ended. The limit takes effect immediately for 12 months and will then be reviewed. He may still apply for a grant of legal aid. Review is directed to the NSW Ombudsman.
