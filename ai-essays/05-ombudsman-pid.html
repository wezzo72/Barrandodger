05 — The Commonwealth Ombudsman and PID-2021-400008

Executive proposition

On 18 November 2021 an Assistant Director in the Commonwealth Ombudsman’s Public Interest Disclosure Team agreed with an authorised officer that a disclosure given reference PID-2021-400008 did not satisfy the Public Interest Disclosure Act 2013 (Cth). The file in the archive is named as an IBAC rejection. The letterhead in the PDF is the Ombudsman’s, not IBAC’s.

Scope

The letter signed “Kathleen”, Assistant Director. The archive filename 2021-11-18-IBAC-PID-Rejection-400008R.pdf is a label. The label is not the letterhead.

Documentary findings

The letter is dated 18 November 2021, reference PID-2021-400008-R, to Dr Richard McLean at 2 McCubbin Street, Footscray, email richarddrawsstuff@gmail.com. It responds to an email of 12 November 2021.

It says the email included abusive comments toward the officer who handled the disclosure, that this is unacceptable, and that contact may be limited if it continues.

It treats the correspondence as a request to review the authorised officer’s decision. It says the officer assessed that, viewed objectively, belief alone was not reasonable grounds to demonstrate disclosable conduct covered by the Commonwealth scheme. It says the PID Act does not apply to alleged conduct of state agencies and private entities, so those parts could not be progressed under the Commonwealth scheme.

It states the test: the information must tend to show, or the discloser must believe on reasonable grounds that it tends to show, disclosable conduct, and an allegation must be more than an assertion. It says the disclosure did not provide information about disclosable conduct by a Commonwealth agency or Commonwealth public official, and therefore did not satisfy section 26.

The review agrees with that decision, considers the officer applied the legislation reasonably, and says further correspondence on the matter will be kept but not answered unless it raises a new issue.

Primary evidence

Ombudsman review, 18 November 2021, filed under an IBAC filename

Corroboration

APRA’s 5 November 2021 letter and the Federal Court letter of 27 March 2023 are separate agencies declining PID pathways. They are not the same decision and do not corroborate each other’s reasons. They corroborate only a pattern of non-allocation or non-acceptance, which is described as a pattern of outcomes, not as proof of coordination. See essay 10.

Contrary evidence

The letter records that he may genuinely have believed the information tended to show disclosable conduct. The office’s point is that belief was not enough. His disagreement is noted and not accepted. Nothing in the letter investigates the factual truth of every state-level allegation; it says those allegations are outside the Commonwealth PID scheme.

What the record establishes

• A — The Ombudsman’s PID team, on review, decided this disclosure did not meet the PID Act.

• A — The stated limits included: belief alone, lack of Commonwealth agency content, and state or private conduct being outside the scheme.

• A — The archive filename does not match the letterhead.

What the record does not establish

It does not establish that every complaint he has ever made was baseless, or that the office found him to have committed an offence by the comments it called abusive. “Abusive” here is the office’s description of emails. No charge is recorded in the letter.

Unresolved questions

The original authorised-officer decision, as a separate document, was not opened. Only this review was.

Evidence classification

Review outcome: A, official record.

His belief that the information showed disclosable conduct: recorded as his belief; the office found the statutory test unmet. Not upgraded to A.
