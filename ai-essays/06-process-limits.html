06 — Integrity and human-rights bodies issued written jurisdictional and process limits

Executive proposition

A second pattern in the opened paper is not silence. It is written limit. IBAC, APRA and the Australian Human Rights Commission each produced letters that say, in substance, what they would not do. Those letters are official records of process outcomes. They do not decide the underlying allegations. They do decide that, on the information then before those bodies, specified statutory gates were not passed.

Scope

Documentary findings

IBAC, 9 April 2020, ref CASE-2020712, signed Sue Kapourelakos, Manager, Assessment & Review. Complaint received 4 March 2020 against Victoria Police and AHPRA. IBAC "has determined that your complaint should not be treated as a 'public interest complaint'." After consideration, IBAC "has decided not to investigate or refer your complaint to another agency for investigation" under s 67 of the IBAC Act 2011. The letter cites s 26 of the Public Interest Disclosures Act 2012 (Vic), particularly subsection 3, and states the information "doesn't show or tend to show the public officer engaged in improper conduct or detrimental action." It adds that although the complaint is not a public interest complaint, "you will still be entitled to the protections under part 6 of the PID Act." It states AHPRA "is not a public body, as per section 6 of the IBAC Act," so that allegation is outside jurisdiction. The letter records welfare referrals.

APRA, 5 November 2021: matters not within APRA's remit; will not be treated as a PID or Corporations Act whistleblower complaint; APRA "will no longer be responding to you on any further correspondence regarding these matters unless further information relevant to APRA's remit becomes available." The same letter states concern about the nature of correspondence with Peter Dunstan, a decision to block further electronic communication to him, and that APRA "has also reported your latest correspondence to the New South Wales Police."

AHRC, 4 July 2023, from InfoService@humanrights.gov.au, subject "RE: Your contact with AHRC [SEC=OFFICIAL:Sensitive]." The Commission writes that "the power to grant you whistleblower status is arguably not a power possessed by the AHRC"; that being a whistleblower is "arguably not a protected attribute" under the anti-discrimination legislation it administers; that ICS offers dispute resolution / conciliation and has "very few enforceable powers"; that complaints must be lodged by or on behalf of a person aggrieved in a covered area of public life; that AHRC "is unable to consider concerns about breaches of human rights if they relate to a state government body in Australia"; and that its powers on human-rights complaints are "very limited."

The Ombudsman service-restriction file's PDF metadata title is "Commonwealth Ombudsman - Service Restriction - Dr Richard McLean 8 August 2025." Body text was not recovered. Existence of a file with that title is established. The restriction's terms are not.

Corroboration

The pattern is multiple agencies, multiple years, multiple statutes, each producing a written "we will not / cannot do X" outcome. That is a documented pattern of process limits. It is not independent proof that the agencies coordinated, and it is not independent proof that the underlying complaints were true or false.

Contrary evidence

A written limit is also consistent with ordinary jurisdictional screening. IBAC's letter says it investigates "a very small number of matters." AHRC describes statutory limits that apply to complainants generally. Those sentences cut against a reading that a limit, by itself, is evidence of targeting. They do not cut against the archival fact that the letters exist and can be read.

IBAC's 2020 letter also records residual Part 6 PID Act protections even while refusing public-interest-complaint status. Omitting that sentence would mis-state the letter.

What the record establishes

• A — IBAC decided, in writing, not to treat CASE-2020712 as a public interest complaint, not to investigate or refer it, and that AHPRA was outside its public-body definition.

• A — APRA decided, in writing, not to treat the matter as a PID or Corporations Act whistleblower complaint, and to stop responding unless new information within remit arrived.

• A — AHRC stated, in writing, that it could not grant whistleblower status, that whistleblower is arguably not a protected attribute, and that it cannot consider human-rights concerns about state government bodies.

• A — A file named as an 8 August 2025 Commonwealth Ombudsman service restriction exists; its body was not extracted.

What the record does not establish

It does not establish that the complaints were investigated on the merits and rejected as false. It does not establish that the complaints were true. It does not establish a joint enterprise among IBAC, APRA, AHRC and the Ombudsman. "Gangstalking," as a characterisation used on other archive pages, is not a finding of any of these three opened letters.

Unresolved questions

The later IBAC letters in the same folder were not opened in this pass. The Ombudsman restriction's operative text remains unread. Whether any body later reopened a matter is not shown by these files.
