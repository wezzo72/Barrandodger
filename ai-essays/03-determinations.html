03 — The highest-status opened instruments are administrative determinations, not criminal findings

Executive proposition

Inside the opened corpus, the documents with the highest formal status are administrative determinations about a workers' compensation claim. On 26 May 2021 Comcare declined claim 1326583/1 under section 14 of the Safety, Rehabilitation and Compensation Act 1988. On 12 July 2023 the Administrative Appeals Tribunal sent a covering letter stating that it had affirmed the decision under review. Those instruments are official records. They are not findings that a crime occurred.

Scope

Opened: McLean-determination-Comcare-rejection-26May2021.pdf (3 pages, extractable text) and ART-2021-7478-McLean-and-Comcare-Decision-12-July-2023.pdf (2 pages, AAT covering letter). A longer file named McLean-and-Comcare-FINAL-Decision.pdf exists in the same folder; this essay does not treat the covering letter as the full reasons.

Documentary findings

The Comcare letter is dated 26 May 2021, addressed to Dr Richard William McLean at 2 McCubbin Street, Footscray 3011. Claim number 1326583/1. Claimed condition: anxiety and depression. Date of injury: 22 January 2021. The determination states the claim "has been declined under section 14 of the Safety, Rehabilitation and Compensation Act 1988 (SRC Act) for anxiety state and depressive disorder."

The stated first criterion is whether the person is an "employee" under section 5 of the SRC Act. The letter records that the claim form dated 26 April 2021 stated the usual occupation as "artist-life coach- therapeutic supplier" and "sole trader"; that an email of 13 May 2021 said the person was a registered NDIA provider; that NDIA, in a statement dated 10 May 2021, advised the person is not an employee of NDIA, is not paid wages with tax and superannuation deducted, and is not subject to the APS Code of Conduct. Comcare concludes it is "not satisfied you are an 'employee' in accordance with the SRC Act and as such are precluded from claiming compensation through Comcare." The letter is signed Amy Delzoppo, Comcare delegate.

The AAT covering letter is on Administrative Appeals Tribunal letterhead, General Division, Our Ref: 2021/7478, dated 12 July 2023, signed "Eliza W For the Registrar." Applicant: Richard William McLean. Respondent: Comcare. It states a decision has been made under section 43 of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal Act 1975, that a copy of the decision is sent with the letter, and: "We have affirmed the decision we were asked to review, which means that it has not been changed." It advises of a possible appeal to the Federal Court of Australia.

PDF metadata on the AAT covering letter shows CreationDate 5 March 2026 and Producer "SBC IT Pty Ltd via ABCpdf." That is a later processing stamp on the file as stored. It is not, without more, a reason to discard the letterhead text. It is a provenance fact about the stored copy.

Corroboration

The two letters are consistent with each other on outcome: a declined claim, later affirmed. They are also consistent with the archive's own later analytical pages that discuss the Comcare path. Consistency of outcome across a determination and a tribunal covering letter is corroboration of the administrative result. It is not corroboration of the author's allegations about other agencies.

Contrary evidence

The covering letter is two pages and is not the reasons for decision. Treating "affirmed" as a full account of the Tribunal's findings on every issue would over-read this file. The longer reasons PDF exists and should be opened by any reader who needs the Tribunal's findings of fact.

Comcare's employee analysis is an administrative conclusion on the evidence then before the delegate. It is not a criminal finding, and it is not a finding about NDIS quality-and-safeguards issues except insofar as they were used as indicators of contractor status.
