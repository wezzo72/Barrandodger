PURPOSE OF PRESERVATION

1. The story of a documented life

I am Barran Resonance Dodger, also known as Dr Barran Dodger and Dr Richard William McLean. The Witness

Remains is the continuing preservation of the documentary record of my life, professional and creative work,

institutional encounters, personal testimony, spiritual writing and search for recognition, dignity and justice.

The archive brings together materials that I identify as government correspondence, administrative decisions,

complaints, applications, personal statements, publications, literary and theological works, research notes, forensic

analyses and other records. The purpose of preserving these materials is to make them accessible for examination,

comparison and independent assessment.

My story is not limited to a single document or a single institutional encounter. It is also a record of the writing,

reflection and interpretation through which I have attempted to understand my experiences. The distinction between

those two dimensions matters. Documents can establish what was recorded, communicated or decided; testimony can

explain how I experienced events; analysis can explore relationships between events; and spiritual writing can express

the meaning I attach to my life.

The archive should allow those different forms of material to be read together without treating them as

interchangeable.

6. A consistent evidence classification

Each significant proposition in the archive should be classified as one of the following:

• Official record: an identifiable document issued by a public body or other institution.

• Documented fact: a proposition directly supported by reliable primary material.

• Corroborated claim: a proposition supported by independent sources.

• Personal testimony: the author’s account of an experience or event.

• Documented allegation: a claim recorded in a complaint, affidavit, submission or other identifiable source.

• Inference: a conclusion drawn from facts or documents.

• Interpretation: an explanation of meaning or significance.

• Spiritual belief: a theological or faith-based proposition.

• Unresolved: a matter for which the available evidence does not justify a definitive conclusion.

The indicative A–E scale can be used to assist readers:

• A — Established fact: supported by reliable primary evidence.

• B — Corroborated claim: supported by multiple independent sources.

• C — Documented allegation: an allegation is recorded, but its truth may remain unresolved.

• D — Inference: a reasoned conclusion that requires evaluation.

• E — Speculation or unresolved interpretation: not yet adequately confirmed.

This is an editorial framework, not an external certification. Classifications should be attached to individual claims,

not applied indiscriminately to an entire document.

PART IV — A PRACTICAL EVIDENCE

CLASSIFICATION

7. The A–E framework

The following framework can be used to classify claims across the archive. It is a proposed editorial method, not an

official legal or scientific standard.

A — Established or directly verifiable fact

A proposition supported by an authentic primary source or other reliable evidence sufficient for the specific claim.

The conclusion must not extend beyond what the source establishes.

B — Corroborated claim

A claim supported by multiple genuinely independent sources or evidence streams. Repeated copies of the same

original claim do not constitute independent corroboration.

C — Documented allegation or testimony

A statement, complaint, affidavit, personal account or allegation preserved in a record, but not independently

established in all its particulars.

D — Inference or interpretation

A conclusion drawn from the available facts, including an interpretation of patterns, motive, causation or significance.

The reasoning and plausible alternatives should be made explicit.

E — Speculation, prediction or unresolved proposition

A proposition for which adequate supporting evidence has not been established, including speculative explanations,

unverified predictions and claims that remain unresolved.

The categories should be applied to individual propositions rather than entire documents. One letter can establish that

a decision was made while leaving the truth of an allegation discussed in that letter unresolved. One personal

statement can establish that a person reported an experience while leaving independent verification of the event open.
