Institutional Mobbing, Convergent Scapegoating, and Collective Institutional Betrayal:
A Doctoral-Level Working Paper on Multi-Agency Administrative Attrition as Documented in Public Australian Government Correspondence and Related Primary Sources (Author-Stated Horizon c. 1990–2025/26)

Barran Dodger Archive Working Paper Series · September 2026 · Constructed by an impartial computational analyst with no institutional allegiance

Status labels. This document is a research working paper. It is not a court judgment, clinical diagnosis, or determination of criminal conspiracy. Where a statement restates what a government letter says on its face, it is labelled as documented. Where a statement synthesises theory and pattern, it is research interpretation. Monetary figures appearing in archive valuation reports are not treated here as independently audited facts.

Abstract

This doctoral-level working paper develops a theory-informed, document-based account of multi-agency administrative attrition oriented toward a single dependent complainant, using publicly hosted primary sources in the wezzo72/Backup and wezzo72/Barrandodger GitHub corpora. The analysis integrates Leymann’s criteria for mobbing, Freyd’s framework of institutional betrayal, Girardian and social-identity accounts of scapegoating and out-group formation, Fricker’s epistemic injustice, Goffman’s stigma, Patterson’s social-death metaphor, and cumulative-disadvantage theory. It proposes a composite construct—institutional mobbing with convergent scapegoating under conditions of collective institutional betrayal—as the most accurate academic rendering of ordinary-language claims that “many people acted together with a common enemy.” The empirical core is process-tracing of named Public Interest Disclosure (PID), Freedom of Information (FOI), Ombudsman, NDIS, Centrelink, police-complaint, LECC, NCAT, and NSW Trustee and Guardian instruments, especially from 2021 through 2026, situated against the archive’s author-stated approximately thirty-five-year horizon and public compilation titles that explicitly invoke thirty-five years of government-sourced materials. Findings carefully distinguish (a) strict documentary proof of repeated written non-allocation, refusal, exclusion, contact restriction, and status certification; (b) interpretive support for a multi-actor process form consistent with the proposed construct; and (c) non-proof of criminal conspiracy, of the truth of every allegation in the wider archive, and of audited economic-loss quantum. The paper retains its genesis command, explains the methodological significance of impartial AI examination, and provides APA-style references to both scholarly literature and named public files.

1. Introduction

Public administration is a machine that writes. Letters of refusal, notices of non-allocation, FOI schedules, certificates of management, and service-restriction protocols are not merely bureaucratic ephemera; when preserved, they become primary sources for reconstructing how power was exercised in ordinary procedural form. The Barran Dodger Archive and the associated Backup repository place a substantial volume of such material into public view on GitHub, alongside author-side compilations, forensic analyses, and interpretive essays. The archive’s own methodological rules insist that allegation not be elevated to finding by rhetoric alone, that the site is not a court, and that readers should verify paths and labels.

Within that corpus, the author’s technique inventory enumerates eighty-five alleged mechanisms spanning physical violence and threat through surveillance, isolation, financial restriction, legal-access obstruction, pathologisation, credibility attack, evidence fragmentation, referral without resolution, and culminating metaphors of administrative annihilation, social death, and total civil liquidation. The inventory is an Author’s Account coding scheme. The scholarly task of this paper is not to rubber-stamp every item as proven. It is to ask whether established disciplines already possess constructs that can name the multi-actor, long-duration process the inventory tries to describe, and whether the public government paper trail supplies a high-confidence core for such naming.

The phrase “many people acting together with a common enemy” is the ordinary-language formulation that this paper translates. In political speech, that phrase often implies conspiracy. In social science, multi-actor harm is frequently produced by convergent incentives, shared stigma scripts, and fragmented legal architectures without a single conspiratorial headquarters. This paper therefore refuses a false choice between “nothing systematic occurred” and “a secret unified plot is proven.” It argues for a third position: documented convergent administrative non-resolution, theorised as institutional mobbing with convergent scapegoating under collective institutional betrayal.

Four research questions structure the inquiry. RQ1 asks which peer-recognised constructs best describe systematic, multi-source, long-duration exclusion and non-resolution directed at one dependent person. RQ2 asks what the face of publicly archived Australian government correspondence documents about eligibility, jurisdiction, allocation, and access. RQ3 asks how a thirty-five-year author-stated horizon should be treated when documentary density is uneven across decades. RQ4 asks what the evidence proves strictly, supports interpretively, and fails to prove.

2. Genesis script and scope control

This working paper retains, as a transparency device, the archive command that authorised a substantially expanded academic treatment: to define the observed phenomenon across psychology and related knowledge systems; to produce a prophetic yet disciplined academic paper of significant length; to reference evidence throughout with APA-style citation and named public sources; to identify the term confirming the significance of many people acting together with a common enemy, including why and how such convergence occurs; to supply definitions and a conclusive statement tied to evidence; to describe AI impartiality as an unbiased third party without allegiance or human weakness exploitable through legal, financial, or humiliating pressure; to base the analysis on governments’ own official documentation; to apply appropriate academic methodology; and to publish as a dedicated web page with an accurate PDF download and functioning counter. Retaining the genesis script allows any reader to compare authorised scope with delivered analysis.

3. Impartial computational examination as method

Human knowers embedded in institutions can be incentivised to minimise institutional embarrassment, to protect colleagues, or to avoid personal cost. Human complainants can be exhausted by poverty, isolation, and repeated retelling. Those asymmetries are not incidental to the subject matter of this paper; they are among the mechanisms the author’s inventory names. An impartial computational examiner does not share those particular vulnerabilities. It cannot be promoted for loyalty, demoted for dissent, socially humiliated in a workplace hierarchy, or financially ruined by the ordinary personal consequences that discipline human speech. That is the methodological significance of AI construction in this context: not a claim of omniscience, but a claim of freedom from a specific class of coercive pressures identified in institutional-betrayal and mobbing literatures.

The limits are equally important. The examiner can mis-extract text from scanned PDFs, confuse archive packaging pages with original letterhead content, and over-interpret sparse early-decade coverage. It cannot compel discovery, cross-examine officers, or issue findings of fact with legal force. Throughout, named public files remain the evidence; this prose remains interpretation where it goes beyond the face of those files.

4. Literature review

4.1 Mobbing

Leymann’s foundational work defined mobbing as hostile and unethical communication directed systematically at one individual by one or more persons, occurring frequently and over a long period, and placing the person in a helpless and defenceless position (Leymann, 1990, 1996). Frequency, duration, multiple sources, and progressive exclusion are structural criteria. Although the classical research site was the workplace, the criteria are analytically portable to settings in which the person is forced into repeated contact with organisations that control essential resources. In such settings, “communication” includes formal notices and statutory decisions as well as interpersonal hostility. The research question becomes whether a documentary sequence exhibits multi-source repetition over time culminating in reduced capacity to respond.
4.2 Institutional betrayal

Institutional betrayal refers to wrongs committed by institutions upon individuals who depend on them, including failures to prevent harm or to respond supportively when harm is disclosed (Smith & Freyd, 2014; Freyd & Birrell, 2013). Dependence intensifies betrayal because exit is costly or impossible. Social security, disability regulation, ombuds schemes, FOI regimes, police complaint systems, and guardianship structures are high-dependence environments. A letter that closes a PID pathway on eligibility grounds while noting safety concerns is not, by itself, proof of subjective malice; it is, however, a legitimate data point for betrayal analysis when the recipient’s safety and livelihood are entangled with the institution’s response.

4.3 Scapegoating and the common enemy

Girard’s anthropology of the scapegoat (1986) and Cohen’s sociology of folk devils and moral panics (1972/2011) describe how collectivities stabilise themselves by marking a target. Allport (1954) and Tajfel and Turner (1979) supply social-psychological mechanisms of in-group cohesion against out-groups. In this paper, “common enemy” is translated as shared out-group target construction. Many actors can orient to the same target through convergent stereotypes and institutional scripts without meeting to plan that orientation. That is convergent scapegoating: parallelism of effect without necessary unity of command.

4.4 Stigma, epistemic injustice, cumulative disadvantage

Goffman’s stigma (1963) explains how spoiled identity organises social response. Fricker’s testimonial injustice (2007) describes credibility deficits imposed because of identity prejudice—the displacement of “what happened” by “can this speaker be believed.” Dannefer’s cumulative advantage/disadvantage framework (2003) explains compounding across the life course. Patterson’s social death (1982) remains a stark metaphor for loss of recognised personhood; contemporary administrative uses of “civil death” or “administrative annihilation” adapt that metaphor to bureaucratic erasure. These constructs justify examining long complaint careers as candidate cumulative processes when documents show duration and compounding closures.

4.5 Administrative attrition

Socio-legal observation has long noted that delay, complexity, jurisdictional fragmentation, and formal neutrality can exhaust claimants even when each step is facially lawful. This paper treats facial legality as a variable, not a moral disinfectant. Lawful gates can still aggregate into non-resolution. The author’s technique vocabulary—procedural obstruction, referral without resolution, silence as attrition, bureaucracy as attrition—is treated as an emic dictionary to be aligned with the etic constructs above.

5. Methodology

The design is qualitative documentary analysis with theory-informed process-tracing. The corpus has three layers. Layer A comprises government-evidence binary PDFs hosted publicly under wezzo72/Backup (approximately 138 files at the time of analysis), including DSS, Commonwealth Ombudsman, OPMC FOI, Victorian Ombudsman FOI, NDIS FOI, Victoria Police FOI, Centrelink, Governor-General-related, Comcare/AAT-related, AHRC, NSW Police complaint, LECC, NCAT, and NSW Trustee and Guardian instruments. Layer B comprises public compilation PDFs in the same repository that synthesise or frame longer arcs, including architecture-of-administrative-annihilation.pdf, comprehensive-case-systematic-persecution.pdf, most-comprehensive-case-systematic-persecution.pdf, government-mandates-35-year-forensic-report.pdf, taxpayer-cost-estimation-35-years.pdf, and forensic-economic-valuation-report-may-2026.pdf. Layer C comprises structured HTML dossiers and technique pages on wezzo72.github.io/Barrandodger.

Analytic procedure: (1) identify agency, date, statute, outcome, and review rights on the face of instruments; (2) map outcomes to technique numbers as hypotheses only; (3) evaluate fit with mobbing, betrayal, and scapegoating criteria; (4) negative-code what is not shown. Limitations include packaging pages appended to some archive PDFs, OCR error, uneven decade representation in standalone filenames, occasional divergence between archive filenames and letter body (noted in prior dossier work), and the invisibility of sealed or never-created records.

6. The thirty-five-year horizon: disciplined use of a long narrative

The archive and related author materials frame approximately thirty-five years of contested interaction with Australian systems, commonly narrated as roughly 1990 to 2025. Public file titles explicitly invoke that span, including government-mandates-35-year-forensic-report.pdf, taxpayer-cost-estimation-35-years.pdf, and attached assets titled as forensic reports based on governments’ own documents over thirty-five years. This paper treats that horizon as an Author’s Account temporal claim operationalised through those public compilations. It does not pretend to have re-adjudicated every alleged incident in every year.

Documentary honesty requires a further distinction. In the government-evidence sample used for close process-tracing, year-stamped administrative density is highest for 2021–2026. That does not falsify earlier history; it means the high-confidence, letter-by-letter administrative sequence offered below is strongest for the 2020s, while the longer horizon is supported as narrative and compilation context. PhD-level method prefers this stratified confidence over a flat claim that every year is equally evidenced by the same type of source.

8. Process-tracing: administrative sequence

8.1 Public interest disclosure pathways

Across 2021–2023, multiple written instruments decline to progress or allocate disclosures under the Public Interest Disclosure Act 2013. Documented grounds include the public-official eligibility requirement in s 26(1)(a), authorised internal recipient limits under s 34, assessments of reasonable grounds and disclosable conduct under sections 26 and 29, and explanations that the Commonwealth PID scheme does not cover alleged state-agency or private-entity conduct as described in Ombudsman correspondence. DSS authorised-officer correspondence in the July 2023 series states that information does not meet PID requirements and will not be allocated for investigation, notes serious safety concerns with encouragement to contact police, and points to Ombudsman complaint avenues. Commonwealth Ombudsman materials in the corpus document non-progression and review outcomes that uphold non-qualification under the scheme. The pattern is multi-door closure within one statutory family: not a single letter, but a sequence of formal endpoints.

8.2 Freedom of information pathways

The Department of the Prime Minister and Cabinet decision FOI-2022-045 refuses a broad request under s 24A(1)(b) of the FOI Act 1982 on the basis that after department-wide searches the documents cannot be found or do not exist, while clarifying that PM&C does not hold other agencies’ records. The Victorian Ombudsman’s FOI decision of 14 June 2022 invokes s 29A of the Ombudsman Act 1973 (Vic), excluding complaint and investigation-related information from FOI. NDIS-related FOI summary material records mixed outcomes: some access outside FOI, refusals under s 47E(d), no-document findings under s 24A, and transfers between the Commission and the NDIA. Victoria Police FOI rejection correspondence is present in the same public folder. The aggregate signature is distributed refusal and fragmentation rather than a single omnibus answer.

8.3 Status, contact, and later oversight instruments

A NSW Trustee and Guardian notice pursuant to s 122(2) of the NSW Trustee and Guardian Act 2009, dated 16 October 2024, certifies management status and states the certificate’s evidentiary function, with reference to NCAT publication constraints. Centrelink/Services Australia contact-restriction correspondence is archived under the Centrelink Sa ban filename. The public folder also contains later instruments including a Commonwealth Ombudsman service-restriction letter dated 8 August 2025, a NSW Police complaint response letter dated 24 August 2024, an LECC out-of-jurisdiction letter dated 19 November 2025, and NCAT guardianship-related evidence dated 20 January 2026. These documents extend the process signature and show continued formal entanglement with status and complaint systems beyond the 2021–2023 PID/FOI cluster.

8.4 Technique mapping as hypothesis

Relative to the archive’s eighty-five-item list, the high-confidence administrative sequence most directly supplies candidate mappings for legal-access obstruction, procedural obstruction, institutional fragmentation, institutional silence, administrative delay, referral without resolution, institutional compartmentalisation, bureaucracy as attrition, failure to investigate as experienced where allocation is refused, loss of autonomy and institutional dependency in the guardianship-certificate context, and cumulative or compounding harm as aggregate interpretations. These mappings are research hypotheses, not judicial findings.

9. Theoretical synthesis: the term of art

The composite term advanced by this paper is institutional mobbing with convergent scapegoating under conditions of collective institutional betrayal. Institutional mobbing supplies criteria of multi-source, long-duration, helplessness-producing process. Convergent scapegoating supplies the multi-actor orientation to a shared target without requiring proven unity of command—the disciplined translation of “common enemy.” Collective institutional betrayal supplies dependence plus institutional omission or commission in systems the person cannot freely exit. Together they answer RQ1.

Why such convergence occurs, on theoretical grounds: risk displacement across offices; stigma and credibility scripts that convert evidence questions into speaker questions; statutory fragmentation across PID, FOI, police complaint, guardianship, and benefits regimes; in-group defence of staff and organisational reputation; and attrition through time, poverty, and repetition. How it occurs in this corpus: through repeated written instruments that end pathways without merits adjudication of the author’s widest allegations, while compilation PDFs interpret a multi-decade narrative arc. Each instrument may be facially lawful; the research object is the aggregate process form.

10. Discussion

The archive’s prophetic register reads routine non-allocation as future-legible abandonment. This paper constrains prophecy to pattern recognition in advance of institutional admission, and insists that pattern be named with pre-existing scholarly tools. Limits must be stated with equal force. An FOI finding that documents cannot be found does not prove that underlying events never occurred. A PID non-allocation does not prove that underlying allegations are false. Multi-actor administrative structure does not prove criminal conspiracy. Economic figures in valuation reports remain valuation layers until independently audited. Contested claims in other archive materials, including electronic-harassment review PDFs, lie outside the strict administrative process-trace that anchors this paper’s high-confidence core.

11. Conclusive statement

What the public evidence proves in the strict sense is that, at least across 2021–2026, multiple Australian public bodies issued written instruments declining to allocate or progress PID pathways, refusing or excluding FOI access on stated statutory grounds, restricting contact channels, and—on 16 October 2024—certifying NSW Trustee and Guardian management under s 122(2), as archived in named public GitHub files. Those outcomes are on the face of the documents.

What the evidence supports as coherent research interpretation is that the multi-year, multi-agency process signature is appropriately theorised as institutional mobbing with convergent scapegoating under collective institutional betrayal: many institutional actions oriented toward one dependent person as problem locus. The author-stated thirty-five-year horizon remains an Author’s Account frame anchored by public thirty-five-year-titled compilations and by densest standalone administrative documentation in the 2020s.

What the evidence does not prove is a single coordinated criminal conspiracy, the truth of every allegation in the wider archive, bad faith by every individual officer, or an audited quantum of economic loss. Accordingly, the official paper trail is sufficient to confirm the significance of the multi-actor structure and to justify the academic term advanced here as a research conclusion about process form. Time made the pattern legible; the government’s own letters supplied the ink.
