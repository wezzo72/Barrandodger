3. What a gift does not do

  • It does not turn an allegation into a fact.
  • It does not turn a hash into proof of the words inside a file.
  • It does not open a case at the ICC, the High Court, or any other court.
  • It does not appoint this archive as a lawyer or an agent.

4. A pathway, in order

This is a reading and filing pathway. The archive can do the first steps. Only the person, writing to the institution, can do the last step.

  1. Start from the index of 3,215 PDFs, not from a slogan. Note the repository and the path.
  2. Open the file. Keep the filename. Do not rename evidence to make a bundle look cleaner.
  3. Label each item before it is used: primary record, author's account, allegation, analysis, or prophetic writing. A blockchain stamp or a SHA-256 does not change that label.
  4. Set aside anything that is only a mirror of the same bytes. The methodology page explains how identical bytes were counted once.
  5. If a person chooses to write to an institution, they write in their own name, attach the documents they have actually read, and say which sentences are records and which are allegations.
  6. The International Criminal Court publishes its own site at icc-cpi.int. That site was answering on 4 October 2026. Any communication goes there, from the person, not from a donate button on this archive.
