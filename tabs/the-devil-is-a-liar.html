THE DEVIL IS A LIAR

Spiritual interpretation and testimony · Barran Dodger / Dr Richard William McLean · Finding aid · not a court · not a verdict

How to read this page. This is the author’s spiritual interpretation of lived experience. It is testimony, not an independently established finding. Allegations and accusations referred to here are presented for examination against the published documentary record.

The devil is a liar.

He did not attack me only in the present. I believe he attacked my future — what I was destined to become — because he knew, before I fully remembered it myself, the impact I could have if I remembered who I am.

I believe that if I remembered who I am, the kingdom of darkness would shudder.

My evidence is published, evidence-based material that can be independently examined and fact-checked by anyone willing to look. The documents, records, correspondence and chronology are there. They can be tested against the source material rather than simply accepted because I say they are true.

But there is something particularly revealing about the allegations that have surrounded me.

Neither those who have made accusations against me, nor those who have repeated them, have confronted me directly with the evidence and said: “This is what we say you have done. Here is the evidence. Let it be examined in a legitimate forum, where both sides can be heard and the truth can determine the outcome.”

I have not experienced that kind of direct confrontation with evidence.

Instead, much of what has surrounded me has appeared through anonymous voices, indirect accusations, unexplained claims and allegations without an identifiable person standing openly behind them.

That quality matters.

There is something profoundly different between a person looking another person in the eye and saying, “This is what I accuse you of, and I am prepared to substantiate it,” and accusations that circulate without a clear source, identity, accountability or opportunity for proper examination.

In my spiritual interpretation of what I have experienced, that anonymity resembles the nature of the adversary.

The devil is not only a liar. He is the great distorter — twisting truth, manipulating perception and using whatever means he can to influence people.

And I believe he is also a coward.

He hides.

He works through anonymity.

He allows accusations to circulate without the person making them having to stand openly behind them.

I have lived through what felt to me like hell on earth.

I have experienced the blackness of betrayal, rejection and what I experienced as being cast out from human community.

I have studied my own shadow self so thoroughly that I have had to confront parts of myself that were difficult, painful and frightening.

I feel as though I have been to hell, shaken Satan’s hand, and returned.

And yet I am still here.

I have continued to be provoked, challenged and tested, but none of it has ultimately been sufficient to extinguish what I believe I was created to do.

I feel like an angel who can kill and a psychopath who can bless.

I do not mean that as a literal claim about violence or psychopathy. I mean that I have confronted both the capacity for destruction and the capacity for radical compassion within the human shadow, and I have had to learn to hold both realities consciously.

That is why I do not fear the devil.

I can laugh at what I perceive as his cowardly anonymity because I recognise the pattern.

And if my interpretation is correct, then everything intended to obscure, silence or destroy my purpose has ultimately become part of the testimony through which I understand that purpose.
