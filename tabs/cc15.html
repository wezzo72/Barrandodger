Statement to Cass — Formal Legal Objection and Notice of Reported Electronic Surveillance
and Slander
Cass,
I am placing you and Ablepoint on formal notice of my objection to what I am experiencing and reporting within
Ablepoint accommodation, including what I perceive to be electronic surveillance, unexplained or intrusive audio,
and repeated derogatory statements or slander directed at me.
I specifically object to any monitoring, recording, surveillance, harassment, intimidation or other conduct occurring
within my accommodation without lawful authority, proper consent, notification, or an appropriate safeguarding and
investigative process. I also object to the repeated attribution to me of extremely serious allegations, including
allegations involving sexual misconduct or paedophilia, where those allegations are not established by any finding of
a competent court or other properly constituted investigative process.
I have reported experiencing voices or audio that I describe as “V2K” or perceived electronic voice phenomena. I
am not asking you simply to accept my explanation of the technology as established fact. I am asking Ablepoint to
take seriously the underlying safeguarding complaint: I am reporting persistent and distressing audio

experiences which I experience as externally directed, hostile, humiliating and targeted. The appropriate
response is investigation, documentation and safeguarding—not dismissal, ridicule, retaliation or silence.
The messages and recordings I have preserved are contemporaneous evidence of what I reported, when I
reported it, and the effect it was having upon me. They do not, by themselves, establish the technical mechanism
responsible for the audio. That distinction is important. Equally, the absence of an explanation does not justify
simply disregarding the complaint.
I further object to the circulation or repetition of allegations portraying me as a “pedo” , rapist, or other criminal when
those matters have not been established against me by a competent tribunal or court. If Ablepoint, its employees,
contractors or associated persons contend that such allegations are founded upon an actual complaint, report,
finding or other evidence, I request that the existence and provenance of that material be formally identified through
the appropriate lawful process.
I also put on record my position that I do not presently regard myself as freely able to leave my accommodation
merely because I am theoretically permitted to do so. My circumstances involve disability, financial limitations,
dependence upon accommodation and support, and practical barriers to obtaining alternative arrangements.
Consequently, statements that I am “free to leave” do not, without examination of those circumstances, answer my
complaint about whether I am practically able to escape an environment that I experience as threatening and
psychologically harmful.
This is particularly important because the alleged conduct is occurring in the very environment upon which I depend
for accommodation and disability-related support. That creates an inherent safeguarding concern if the person
making the complaint believes that the same environment is the source of, or is permitting, the conduct complained
of.
I therefore request that this statement be treated as a formal safeguarding and legal objection, rather than as an
interpersonal disagreement.
I ask Ablepoint to:
1. preserve all relevant records, communications, incident reports, complaints, staff notes and other material
concerning my allegations;
2. record my complaint accurately and without characterising my report as established fact or dismissing it
without investigation;
3. identify whether any authorised surveillance, recording or monitoring of my accommodation has occurred;
4. investigate any allegations being circulated about me and identify their evidentiary basis through proper
procedures;
5. ensure that I am not subjected to retaliation, intimidation, eviction pressure or adverse treatment because I
have made complaints or sought external review;
6. provide an appropriate independent avenue for me to raise safeguarding concerns where the organisation
itself is implicated in the complaint; and
7. consider practical measures that would allow me to feel safe while the matters are being assessed.
I am also expressly requesting that no inference of guilt be drawn from my reporting of these experiences. I am
documenting what I perceive, what I have heard, what I have reported, and the circumstances in which those
experiences occur. Whether the underlying cause is technological, interpersonal, environmental, psychological, or
something else is a matter requiring proper assessment rather than assumption.
My objection is therefore not dependent upon proving the mechanism I believe may be responsible. My objection
concerns the reported conduct, its effects upon me, the repeated derogatory allegations, the lack of an
adequate response, and the safeguarding implications of those matters occurring while I remain dependent
upon the accommodation in which they are occurring.
I am asking you, Cass, to understand that I am not seeking a confrontation. I am seeking a documented, impartial
and accountable response.
I want the allegations investigated. I want the evidence preserved. I want serious accusations against me
dealt with through lawful processes rather than insinuation, repetition or humiliation. And I want my inability
to readily leave my accommodation to be recognised as a practical safeguarding issue rather than reduced to
the proposition that I am technically “free to leave. ”
This statement is made contemporaneously to preserve my position and to place Ablepoint on notice of the
concerns I am reporting.

THEY COULD NOT ERASE WHAT GOD PRESERVED
A Two-Part Prophetic Testimony of Survival, Divine Purpose, Truth, Justice and Reckoning
Barran Dodger
This two-part work presents a prophetic testimony concerning survival, divine preservation, truth, justice and
ultimate reckoning. It is constructed at the intersection of personal testimony, biblical symbolism, theological
reflection and historical witness, presenting the author’s experience through the interpretive framework of death,
resurrection, persecution, preservation and vindication. At its centre is the proposition that what human power
sought to destroy, silence or erase was nevertheless preserved, and that survival itself became part of the

testimony. The work therefore understands preservation not merely as continued existence, but as the
preservation of memory, evidence, identity, testimony and purpose against forces perceived by the author as
seeking their destruction.
The first part establishes the theological and existential foundation of the testimony. It interprets survival through
recurring biblical paradigms of the persecuted righteous, the tested servant, the rejected witness and the person
who emerges from apparent destruction with a renewed sense of divine purpose. The language of resurrection
functions both literally within the author’s spiritual understanding and symbolically as an account of radical
survival: the movement from apparent ending to continued existence, from attempted erasure to testimony, and
from silence to declaration. Biblical motifs associated with figures such as Job, Joseph and other witnesses
subjected to suffering, abandonment and apparent defeat provide an interpretive vocabulary through which the
author’s experience is situated within a larger theological narrative of trial, endurance and eventual revelation.
The second part develops this testimony into a declaration concerning truth, justice and reckoning. It advances
the theological conviction that concealment cannot permanently defeat truth and that injustice does not
constitute the final judgment upon the person who has endured it. The archive, testimony and surviving record
assume particular significance within this framework because they represent what remained when other forms of
recognition, protection or institutional validation were perceived to have failed. Preservation consequently
becomes an evidentiary and spiritual concept: the record survives, the witness survives, and therefore the
questions raised by the testimony remain capable of being examined rather than permanently extinguished.
Academically, the work is best understood not as an empirical proof of supernatural causation, but as a
theological testimony documenting the author’s interpretation of extraordinary survival and subsequent events. Its
prophetic claims are therefore presented as matters of faith, spiritual discernment and personal testimony, while
its historical claims may be considered separately against whatever documentary, contemporaneous or
independently verifiable evidence accompanies them. This distinction allows the work to retain its prophetic voice
without requiring the reader to accept theological interpretation as equivalent to independently established
historical fact.
The significance of the testimony lies ultimately in its refusal of erasure. The work proposes that a person may be
deprived of status, security, resources, institutional recognition or social belonging without thereby eliminating the
truth of their lived testimony. What survives can itself become a witness. In this sense, the title constitutes the
governing thesis of the work: they could not erase what God preserved. The preservation of the witness becomes,
within the author’s theological understanding, evidence of continuing purpose; the persistence of the record
becomes a challenge to forgetting; and the continued existence of the testimony becomes an anticipation of a
justice that the author believes ultimately transcends human institutions.
Taken as a whole, the two-part testimony is therefore both an account of survival and a declaration of meaning. It
moves from the experience of being thought finished to the conviction of having been preserved; from suffering to
purpose; from silence to testimony; from concealment to revelation; and from apparent defeat toward justice and
reckoning. Its ultimate claim is theological rather than merely autobiographical: that human beings may attempt to
destroy a witness, suppress a testimony or erase a record, but cannot finally determine what God chooses to
preserve.
