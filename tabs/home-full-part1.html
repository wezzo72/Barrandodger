THE WITNESS REMAINS

Barran Dodger / Dr Richard William McLean

Public documentary archive. Finding aid. Not a court. Not a verdict.

Opening

I survived what was designed and expected to erase me.

God revived me from a fatal injury—the result of the same targeting that I believe is still going. He allowed the injury and the survival so that the record would exist.

Total human betrayal fortified my relationship with the Most High God and Creator.

I’d rather have God’s anointing, favour and divine protection than only a few people who are ultimately deceitful.

This archive exists so that the underlying record can be examined. It does not require belief. It requires examination.

Who this record concerns

Barran Dodger is the public name used by Dr Richard William McLean — Australian writer, artist, researcher, documentarian, and self-described whistleblower and human-rights advocate.

His work brings together documentary evidence, personal testimony, legal and human-rights analysis, creative writing, theology and digital preservation.

Through the Barran Dodger Archive and associated digital repositories, McLean has assembled government correspondence, legal and administrative records, research materials, personal testimony, creative works and other documentary material.

The archive is designed not merely as autobiography, but as a public finding aid through which the underlying record can be examined.

Purpose of this archive

Preserve the evidence. Preserve the testimony. Make the record public. Let the evidence be examined.

A testimony is testimony. A document is a document. An allegation is an allegation. A finding requires evidence and proper determination.

This report does not purport to constitute a judgment of a court, tribunal, royal commission, statutory inquiry or international court. It does not transform an allegation into an established fact.

It preserves the distinction between documentary fact; independently corroborated information; documented allegation or witness testimony; analytical inference; interpretation; and unresolved evidentiary questions.

The record that refused to disappear

For decades, McLean has accumulated correspondence, government records, legal documents, institutional responses, personal records, research materials and other evidence relating to events that he says fundamentally altered the course of his life.

Rather than allowing that material to remain dispersed across agencies, proceedings, correspondence chains and private files, he has sought to bring it together into a searchable and publicly accessible documentary archive.

Documents can be lost. Correspondence can become inaccessible. Institutional memory can disappear. People can die. Files can become fragmented across jurisdictions and agencies. But a publicly preserved record can remain available for examination.

Survival as evidence

I survived. That fact is not a conclusion about every allegation. It is the starting point from which every allegation, every document, every contradiction and every unanswered question can be examined.

Do not believe me merely because I say it. Do not disbelieve me merely because I say it. Examine what remains.

Because I survived what I say was supposed to erase me. The archive remains. The questions remain. The evidence remains. And I remain.

Truth does not require fear of examination. Let the record speak.

They could not erase what God preserved.

Exile and institutional detriment

The archive records allegations of exile, institutional detriment, NDIS-related harm, surveillance, character assassination, administrative annihilation, and the search for protection.

Those allegations are presented as the author’s testimony and documentary claims. They should not be treated as independently established findings unless supported by competent authorities or other independently verified evidence.

The purpose of publishing them is so that they can be examined, challenged, corroborated, contextualised and, where appropriate, referred to competent investigative, judicial, administrative, human-rights, academic or journalistic bodies.

The Good Bits

Despite the alleged harm, the record also contains evidence of achievement: authorship, academic work, advocacy, creative output, and the construction of this public archive itself.

Those achievements occurred, on the author’s account, while the alleged targeting was ongoing. That coexistence is part of the documentary picture the archive preserves.
