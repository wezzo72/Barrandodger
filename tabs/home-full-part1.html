THE WITNESS REMAINS

MY ACCOUNT — NOT A POLICE OR COURT FINDING

The hit they ordered to murder me was their attempt to erase the witness. I survived. That survival is not a verdict on every allegation. It is the fact from which examination begins.

God revived me from a fatal injury—the result of the same targeting that I believe is still going. He allowed the injury and the survival so that the record would exist.

Total human betrayal fortified my relationship with the Most High God and Creator.

I’d rather have God’s anointing, favour and divine protection than only a few people who are ultimately deceitful.

Who this record concerns

Barran Dodger is the public name used by Dr Richard William McLean — Australian writer, artist, researcher, documentarian, and self-described whistleblower and human-rights advocate.

His work brings together documentary evidence, personal testimony, legal and human-rights analysis, creative writing, theology and digital preservation. At the centre of his practice is a sustained effort to preserve and publish the documentary record of his experiences with Australian public institutions and the consequences he says followed from those experiences.

Through the Barran Dodger Archive and associated digital repositories, McLean has assembled government correspondence, legal and administrative records, research materials, personal testimony, creative works and other documentary material. The archive is designed not merely as autobiography, but as a public finding aid through which the underlying record can be examined.

Purpose

Preserve the evidence. Preserve the testimony. Make the record public. Let the evidence be examined.

A testimony is testimony. A document is a document. An allegation is an allegation. A finding requires evidence and proper determination.

This report does not purport to constitute a judgment of a court, tribunal, royal commission, statutory inquiry or international court. It does not transform an allegation into an established fact.
