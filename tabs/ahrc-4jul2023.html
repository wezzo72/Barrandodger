Whistleblower protections

I note you have raised concerns about whistleblower protections and wish to raise a claim of said whistleblower protections with the Australian Human Rights Commission (AHRC) due to your reporting of corrupt practices.

For example, you request that the AHRC “initiate the necessary procedures to grant (you) whistleblower status”. Please note the most this Commission’s Investigation and Conciliation Service (ICS) can offer are dispute resolution/conciliation services, and we have very few enforceable powers — the power to grant you whistleblower status is arguably not a power possessed by the AHRC.

Please note further that being a whistleblower is arguably not a protected attribute for the purposes of the anti-discrimination legislation administered by the AHRC such as sex, age, race etc.

Other concerns relating to human rights and systemic issues of discrimination

In the supporting letters you have provided, for example in the letter provided from Tahsinullah Sultani, reference is made to your advocacy work for people with disabilities and the need to expose systemic discrimination against people with mental illnesses. Reference is also made to your experiences of homelessness, starvation and denial of necessary financial support and wider human rights concerns.

Please note that complaints must be lodged on behalf of or by a person aggrieved by the conduct in question, for example, a person with a disability who felt they experienced unfair treatment in a relevant area of public life we cover such as employment, goods and services etc. because of their disability. On this basis it is not clear the ICS can investigate systemic discrimination unless it is lodged by those specifically affected by certain acts of discrimination, and it is not clear you are raising a specific complaint of discriminatory conduct which occurred towards you because of a protected attribute we cover, such as disability, in a specific area of public life covered by this Commission.

Further, it is not clear who you are alleging has engaged in breaches of your human rights, e.g. a specific federal agency — and which human rights in the international instruments scheduled to, or declared under, the Australian Human Rights Commission Act 1986 (Cth) (AHRCA), such as the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights and the Convention on the Rights of the Child, have been breached.

Please keep in mind the AHRC can only consider concerns of breaches of human rights by Commonwealth bodies, not state government agencies or private organisations/individuals.

Please note further, the International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights (ICESCR) is not an instrument scheduled under the AHRCA as outlined above.

Specific concerns, including “gangstalking”:

1. You raise concerns about “gangstalking”, however, “gangstalking” is not a ground of discrimination administered by this Commission such as race, sex etc. It is also not clear it relates to the Commonwealth human rights jurisdiction of this Commission as scheduled to the AHRCA as outlined above.

You may wish to contact the Commonwealth Ombudsman: https://www.ombudsman.gov.au/

3. You refer to the police using the mental health act — please note that this Commission is not the most appropriate body to consider concerns about the police. In Victoria, arguably the most appropriate body to raise concerns with about Victoria Police is IBAC.

You refer to a coverup to “whitewash” the tragedy at numerous state government bodies in Victoria such as IBAC, the MHCC, the Victorian Inspectorate, the Victorian Ombudsman etc. Please note that the AHRC is unable to consider concerns about breaches of human rights if they relate to a state government body in Australia.

5. You refer to a duty of care owed to you by a hospital and infer you have concerns about treatment you received at a hospital including a brain injury. In Victoria, arguably the most appropriate body is the Health Complaints Commission (HCC).

Victimisation

In relation to claims of victimisation, victimisation has a particular meaning under our laws. Victimisation arises where a person is subjected to a detriment because they lodged a claim of discrimination or asserted rights that are covered by the Commission’s federal anti-discrimination laws. From the information you have provided, it is not clear that you are being victimised because you have raised the possibility of making a discrimination complaint but because you may have been considered/regarded to be a ‘whistleblower’.

Limits of our process

Our Commission’s powers in relation to complaints about a breach of human rights are very limited. The Commission can, where appropriate, assist parties to try to resolve a complaint by conciliation. However, if the complaint is not able to be resolved through the Commission’s conciliation process and the President finds that there has been a breach of human rights, the President can prepare a report on the complaint, including recommendations, for the Federal Attorney-General. The Commission cannot order a respondent to take a particular action or provide a remedy to a complainant. The Commission does not have the power to review the decisions of Courts or Tribunals.
