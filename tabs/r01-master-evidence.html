STANDARD EVIDENCE CLASSIFICATION

  • A — DOCUMENTED FACT: Supported directly by a reliable primary record or authoritative determination.
  • B — CORROBORATED CLAIM: Supported by more than one independent or materially corroborating source without necessarily constituting an official finding.
  • C — DOCUMENTED ALLEGATION: Recorded in a complaint, statement, correspondence or other document but not independently established.
  • D — INFERENCE: A conclusion drawn from documented circumstances rather than directly established.
  • E — INTERPRETATION: Analytical, legal, philosophical or theological interpretation.
  • F — UNRESOLVED: Evidence is incomplete, conflicting, unavailable or insufficient for a stronger classification.

PRIMARY FUNCTION

To operate as the central evidentiary register for the archive: what exists, where it exists, what it says, who created it, when it was created, how it is preserved, what evidentiary status it has, what corroborates it, what contradicts it, and what remains unresolved.

The Master Evidence Record is the control layer connecting a proposition to its primary record. An independent reviewer should start with a claim, locate the exact document, inspect provenance, compare related records, and determine whether the proposition is established, corroborated, alleged, inferred, interpreted or unresolved.

CORE QUESTIONS

  1. What evidence exists?
  2. Is the source primary, secondary, analytical or testimonial?
  3. What date does the record establish?
  4. Who created it and who received it?
  5. What institution is involved?
  6. Is there a competent finding?
  7. Is there corroboration?
  8. Is there contradiction?
  9. Can the file be independently retrieved?
  10. Has its integrity been preserved?

RECOMMENDED RECORD FIELDS

Evidence ID; Title; Original filename; Repository; Path; File type; Document date; Acquisition date; Publication date; Author/creator; Recipient; Institution; Event/issue; Classification A–F; Source URL; Archive URL; SHA-256; Git commit; Git blob SHA; OpenTimestamps; Blockchain record; Related Evidence IDs; Related Report IDs; Verification status; Privacy classification; Authenticity notes; Contradictory/limiting evidence; Finding status.

SOURCE HIERARCHY

Authoritative judicial or statutory determination → official government record → contemporaneous primary record → independent corroborating material → author account → documented allegation → inference → interpretation → speculation. Lower hierarchy is not automatically false; it requires more care.

PRESERVATION RULES

  1. Preserve the original file and original filename where possible.
  2. Preserve repository path and commit history.
  3. Record duplicates rather than treating them as independent corroboration.
  4. Never silently overwrite a superseded version.
  5. Record hashes for important files; separate processed copies from originals.
  6. Preserve source context, acquisition date, redaction status.
  7. Preserve links to mirrors without confusing mirrors with independent sources.

CRYPTOGRAPHIC VERIFICATION

SHA-256 can show a retrieved file matches a later copy. Git history assists with repository provenance. OpenTimestamps/blockchain can assist with hash chronology. None establishes the truth of substantive statements in the file.

REGISTERS

Contradiction register: proposition A/source A/date A; proposition B/source B/date B; same question?; explanations; independent evidence; official response; status.

Institutional knowledge register: date contacted; recipient; method; document; acknowledgement; response; action; referral; follow-up; receipt evidence; unresolved issue. Silence is not automatic admission.

Financial register: label each amount as actual documented loss, calculated, estimate, projection, opportunity cost, alleged, or unresolved; identify source, calculation, assumption, causation, double-counting risk, mitigation, recovery.

Privacy register: PUBLIC; PUBLIC WITH REDACTIONS; RESTRICTED; SENSITIVE; DO NOT PUBLISH.

DOCUMENTARY WARNING. Existence of a document establishes that the document exists — not the truth of every statement inside it. A complaint establishes that a complaint was made. A court filing establishes that a filing occurred. A government record establishes what was recorded or communicated. A blockchain timestamp assists integrity/chronology — not substantive truth.

READER TEST

FULL-CAPACITY EXPANSION

1. What “conclusive” means

A proposition is conclusive only to the extent the underlying evidence establishes it. Documentary existence, recorded complaint, official determination, and author interpretation are distinct layers. The archive describes itself as a finding aid, not a court or verdict.

2. Fact-aligned conclusion register

For every major proposition: (A) PROPOSITION · (B) PRIMARY SOURCE · (C) WHAT THE SOURCE ACTUALLY ESTABLISHES · (D) WHAT IT DOES NOT ESTABLISH · (E) STATUS A–F.

3. Negative control

Deliberately search for disconfirming material: contradictory institutional statements, adverse decisions, alternative explanations, missing records, inconsistent dates, different causal sequences, jurisdiction gaps. Failure to respond is documented as non-response — not automatic admission.

4. Primary-record chain

Claim → earliest primary document → institution/person → date/context → corroboration → contradiction → institutional response → competent finding → documented consequence → unresolved question.

5. Repository separation

Wezzo72 (repo), DrBarranDodger (repo), and Pages/mirrors are distinct stores. Copies across repositories are not automatic independent corroboration. Official-drive binaries are party copies, not court findings.

6. Preservation ≠ proof

Git history, hashes, timestamps establish properties of digital records — not the truth of every sentence inside affidavits, complaints, statements, or AI reports.

7. Independent reproduction test

Start with the named link, open the archive/repository, locate the document, check date/title/path, compare description to the underlying record. If unreproducible, say so.

READER INDEPENDENCE. A reader should be able to disagree with the archive’s interpretation while still having direct access to the underlying material. Final determination of contested facts belongs to the appropriate evidence-review, regulatory, judicial or other competent process.
