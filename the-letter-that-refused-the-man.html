The book

The significance is that the testimony was already on a public shelf. It was not a secret they discovered. It was a record I had already made. After the book was published, my former employer the Herald Sun weaponised words from it. A weekend double-page spread carried the bold heading “MY DESCENT INTO MADNESS”. The spread, as preserved in this archive, is headed “My descent into madness. How schizophrenia stole Richard McLean’s mind.” What followed was a barely veiled public character assassination. I was then fired from The Age, where I worked as a news graphics artist and cartoonist. I did not resign, and I did not leave. I was denied a lawyer and a pathway to have it tested at the time. The later Legal Aid restriction is the continuation of that refusal, not the 2003 letter itself. The madness was not any real or perceived illness I had. It was the deceitful way an honest and courageous testimony was weaponised against me, systemically, politically and financially, from that point on. The targeting lasted decades. Its goal was my ruin, and the avoidance of accountability by those who participated in it.
