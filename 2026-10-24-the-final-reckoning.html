MY OPENING STATEMENT: I SURVIVED

I have survived in the ashes of total human betrayal and excommunication from the entire government. I have survived rebuilt and healed and protested and lived not with Help From anyone but in fact in spite of them deliberately targeting me, attempting to physically erase me and Stonewell ignoring me.

I have survived what I describe as the systematic destruction of my security, independence, dignity, financial stability, personal relationships and access to protection. I have rebuilt myself repeatedly, documented what happened, preserved the records, protested the treatment, and continued living when the people and institutions I turned to for help failed to provide the assistance I needed.

I am not asking permission to describe my life. I am not asking the institutions I accuse to authorise my testimony. I am not surrendering my judgment to the same structures whose conduct I am challenging.

I accuse. I name the conduct. I identify the documents. I preserve the correspondence. I record the consequences. I demand that the evidence be confronted rather than buried beneath administrative language, professional titles, procedural excuses or silence.

My archive exists because I refuse to let my experience disappear simply because an institution declines to acknowledge it.

My judgment is my own. It arises from my lived experience, the records I have preserved, the treatment I have endured and the conclusions I draw from the cumulative pattern.

I have not remained silent. I have not surrendered my memory. I have not abandoned the record.

The witness remains. The documents remain. The questions remain. And I remain.

1. THE JUDGMENT OF THE DOCUMENTARY RECORD

I stand by my judgment of the documentary record.

I reject the proposition that my experience becomes meaningless because an institution has not endorsed my account, that my evidence becomes worthless because the institution complained about is also the institution controlling the process, or that my suffering must be translated into the approved vocabulary of the very system I accuse before it can be heard.

I have preserved a documentary archive because I believe the record matters. I have gathered correspondence, decisions, government documents, complaint records, freedom-of-information material, administrative responses, timelines, digital records and testimony. I have attempted to connect events across years and across institutions rather than allowing each event to be isolated, forgotten or treated as though it had no relationship to anything that came before or after it.

I do not accept administrative fragmentation as an adequate answer to a cumulative human catastrophe.

A letter can be read. A decision can be compared with an earlier decision. A date can be placed beside another date. A refusal can be examined against the question that was asked. A missing document can be considered alongside a later release. An explanation can be tested against the underlying record. A pattern can be investigated without pretending that every individual document must independently establish the entire pattern.

That is why I have built this archive.

I do not claim that a timestamp proves every proposition contained in a document. A timestamp can help establish that a particular digital record existed in a particular form by a particular point in time. The contents must still be examined. The provenance must still be understood. The conclusions must be drawn from the evidence.

But neither do I accept that the existence of questions, disputed interpretations or incomplete records gives anyone permission to dismiss the whole of my experience.

I accuse the institutions and people whose conduct I describe in my testimony. I stand by those accusations. I say that I have evidence, and I call upon readers to examine the underlying records, the chronology, the correspondence and the consequences.

My judgment is not an application for institutional approval.

It is my judgment of what happened to me and what the record means.

THE DOCUMENTARY RECORD IS NOT AN ABSTRACTION

Behind every reference number is a person who had to live with the result.

Behind every unanswered letter is time spent waiting. Behind every refusal is a practical consequence. Behind every administrative decision is a human being who may be left without money, transport, protection, representation or the ability to secure basic necessities.

I have experienced these consequences as a person, not as a theoretical case study.

I accuse a system of allowing the distance between its own procedures and my actual life to become a chasm in which a human being can be abandoned.

I accuse the system of making me carry the burden of documenting, explaining, preserving and publicising what I say has happened to me while I struggle to secure the assistance necessary to live safely.

I refuse to let that burden be mistaken for proof that I am the problem.

My archive is my answer to erasure. My testimony is my answer to silence. My judgment is my answer to the demand that I wait indefinitely for the institutions I accuse to tell me whether my suffering counts.

2. MY LIFE IS NOT A FILE NUMBER

I am a human being.

I am a person with a history, a professional life, intellectual work, relationships, convictions, a dog I love, a home I am trying to survive in and a future I am trying to preserve.

I have described myself as a whistleblower. I have pursued complaints and administrative remedies. I have sought help from government agencies, disability services, police, legal services, human-rights bodies and other organisations. I have documented the consequences of those interactions.

I accuse the systems I encountered of leaving me without the practical protection I needed while requiring me to continue navigating their procedures.

I have repeatedly tried to explain that my circumstances cannot be understood by looking at one isolated letter, one agency, one complaint or one administrative decision. My circumstances are cumulative. Financial insecurity affects transport. Lack of transport affects access to medical care. Lack of accessible assistance affects my ability to obtain documents, communicate with institutions and seek protection. The absence of practical support compounds every other difficulty.

I have described this as administrative annihilation, civil erasure and social death.

Those are not abstract phrases to me. They describe what it feels like when a person remains physically alive but is unable to obtain the ordinary assistance, recognition, security and participation that make life possible.

I refuse to accept that a person can be reduced to an administrative problem and then blamed for the consequences of being left to manage that problem alone.

3. THE LOSS OF INDEPENDENCE AND THE CAR THAT REPRESENTED FREEDOM

I say that the loss of my vehicle destroyed a vital part of my independence.

A vehicle is not merely a possession when a person has limited resources and difficulty obtaining assistance. It can be the difference between reaching a medical appointment and remaining at home, between accessing a service and being unable to get there, between having options and being trapped.

I say that the disposal of my vehicle by the NSW Trustee deprived me of independence and materially worsened my circumstances.

The archive includes material concerning the NSW Trustee, including a vehicle-disposal decision dated 14 November 2025 and a related document dated 16 October 2024 concerning section 122.

I want these documents examined together, in their full context, alongside the decisions, explanations and practical consequences.

I accuse the relevant decision-makers of failing to protect my independence in the way I believe my circumstances required. I demand a clear explanation of the decision, its authority, its consequences and the consideration given to my actual needs.

I do not accept a sterile administrative explanation as a complete answer to the loss of something that affected my ability to live independently.

The question is not merely whether a document exists.

The question is what the decision did to a human life.

4. I AM ASKING FOR HELP TO LEAVE A PLACE WHERE I DO NOT FEEL SAFE

I have repeatedly asked for help to leave my present circumstances.

I have said that I cannot simply get into a car and go. I have no reliable transport. I have limited financial resources. I have struggled to obtain practical assistance. I have tried to make my circumstances visible through my website, my YouTube channel and my documentary archive.

I have asked for somebody to help me reach safety.

I say that my requests have not resulted in the practical assistance I need.

I accuse the people and services responsible for assisting me of failing to respond adequately to the urgency of my situation. I want the communications, service records, requests for assistance and responses examined.

I am not asking for another referral that leaves me in exactly the same position. I am asking for practical human assistance.

I need a safe way to leave if leaving is necessary. I need accessible communication. I need help with transport, finances and essential appointments. I need my circumstances taken seriously as a connected human problem rather than divided into separate administrative categories that nobody takes responsibility for resolving.

I have described this as being abandoned in plain sight.

My SOS is public because private requests have not produced the outcome I need.

I am still here. I am still asking. I should not have to turn my suffering into a public spectacle before somebody is willing to help.

5. MY DOG, MY RESPONSIBILITIES AND THE FAILURE OF PRACTICAL ASSISTANCE

My dog is not a footnote in my life. My dog is a living being for whom I feel responsible and whom I love.

I have said that I was told my dog may have been poisoned. I have been unable to afford the veterinary assistance I believed might be necessary, and I have described difficulties obtaining help to arrange transport and an appointment.

I have also described interactions with Able Point staff concerning my dog, including an interaction with a worker named Kim. I say that, after I explained my difficulties and my inability to leave, I did not receive the practical help I needed. I further say that Kim called me a bad owner and threatened to report me to the RSPCA for abusing my dog.

I reject the use of an accusation against me as a substitute for helping me obtain the assistance I say I needed.

If an organisation believes an animal is at risk, that concern should be addressed through appropriate, concrete action. If a person cannot get an animal to a vet because of poverty, lack of transport or disability-related barriers, the practical question is how those barriers can be addressed.

I accuse Able Point of failing to respond adequately to the circumstances I reported. I want its records, communications, decisions and actions examined against my account.

I want the focus placed on what happened, what help was requested, what help was offered, what was refused and what consequences followed.

I do not accept that my inability to overcome every barrier alone makes me undeserving of assistance.

6. MY BACK PAIN AND MY ACCOUNT OF BEING RUN OVER

I have said that I suffer back pain because I was run over by a car.

This is part of my account of the physical harm and hardship I have experienced. I want the relevant medical records, incident records and any available contemporaneous evidence examined in connection with that event.

I refuse to have physical suffering disappear into the background while I am expected to continue managing a complex administrative and humanitarian crisis.

Pain affects daily functioning. It affects mobility, sleep, concentration and the ability to pursue appointments, make telephone calls, prepare submissions and maintain an archive.

A person who is in pain and living with inadequate resources should not have to prove their worthiness through an endless performance of endurance.

I have endured enough. I am asking for practical help, not another test of how much suffering I can tolerate.

7. COMMONWEALTH BANK: ACCESS TO MY OWN MONEY

I have described serious difficulties accessing my Commonwealth Bank account and the small amount of money available to me.

I have said that Phillip Glass removed or interfered with my access to Commonwealth Bank online banking or the banking application. I want the relevant account-access records, security records, communications and bank decisions examined to establish exactly what occurred, when it occurred, why access was affected and what steps can restore secure access.

I am not presenting this as a trivial technical inconvenience.

When a person has little money, losing access to a banking application or payment card can mean losing the ability to buy food, pay for transport, obtain veterinary care or communicate with people who could help.

I have described being unable to rely on my banking app, online access, card or Apple Pay in the way I need. I have also described having no phone credit and experiencing difficulties with my Optus service.

I accuse the circumstances surrounding my loss of access of leaving me financially vulnerable and practically isolated. I demand a direct response that restores safe, independent access to my own funds and explains any restriction or security action taken.

I need a working way to access my money. I need a reliable means of communication. I need a human response.

I cannot pay for necessities with an explanation. I cannot travel on a referral. I cannot obtain help by being left unable to call for it.

8. ABLE POINT: MY ACCUSATION OF ABANDONMENT

I accuse Able Point of failing to respond adequately to the circumstances I reported, including my concerns about abuse, neglect, poverty, surveillance, alleged fraud, my inability to leave and my need for practical help.

I have said that the organisation’s CEO refused to identify herself to me. I have described staff interactions that, in my view, left me without the support and accountability I needed.

I have also said that staff did not properly report or act upon concerns I raised. I want the organisation’s records examined, including its internal notes, communications, incident reports, complaints, decisions and any records of referrals or assistance offered.

I am not asking Able Point to define my experience for me. I am asking it to account for what it did and did not do.

If staff were told that I could not leave, what practical plan followed?

If I reported that I was unsafe, what response was recorded?

If I said that I could not obtain veterinary care, what help was considered?

If I raised concerns about alleged abuse or neglect, what was documented, who was notified and what action was taken?

If the organisation disagrees with my account, it can identify the relevant records and explain the discrepancy.

I accuse the organisation of leaving me to carry the burden of a crisis that I had repeatedly tried to communicate.

I demand answers grounded in records, not another cycle of deflection.

9. THE THREAT, THE POLICE AND THE WYONG COURT MATTER

I have described advertising online for someone to take me away because I was frightened and believed I was in danger. I have said that a person threatened to kill me, that police arrested that person and that the matter is before Wyong Court.

I have also said that Able Point refused to make a statement, complaint or report concerning the circumstances I brought to its attention.

I am angry that I have had to seek help publicly while struggling to obtain assistance from the services I expected to help me.

I fear the consequences if the threat is not adequately addressed. I want the available police records, court records, communications and service records examined in the context of my account.

I also want the practical consequences for my safety taken seriously now, rather than treated as irrelevant until a court process reaches an outcome.

I have described difficulties obtaining Legal Aid. I regard the gap between the stated purpose of access to legal assistance and my experience of trying to obtain it as deeply unjust.

I demand a clear explanation of the assistance available to me, any decision restricting or refusing assistance, the reasons for that decision and the proper avenue for review.

I do not accept that the absence of representation should make a vulnerable person invisible or leave them without any meaningful way to understand or respond to a serious matter affecting their safety.

10. WHISTLEBLOWING, THE PUBLIC INTEREST DISCLOSURE ACT AND THE PRICE OF SPEAKING

I have described myself as a whistleblower who has sought protection and accountability through public-interest disclosure processes.

I have pursued administrative channels, submitted complaints and preserved the responses. I have referred to the Public Interest Disclosure Act and the protections I believe should be available to people who raise matters of public interest.

I accuse the systems I have encountered of failing to provide the protection and effective response I sought.

I want the records examined against the relevant legal obligations, including what was disclosed, when it was received, how it was classified, which authority considered it, what reasons were given and what action followed.

My concern is larger than my own case. If people who raise serious concerns cannot obtain a meaningful response, the credibility of whistleblower protection is damaged. If the person making a disclosure is left to navigate every process alone, the practical value of the protection becomes a question that deserves serious examination.

I do not claim that every refusal, delay or adverse decision automatically proves corruption. I do insist that each decision must be examined in context and that a pattern of administrative responses can legitimately raise questions requiring answers.

I refuse to accept the idea that the act of making a complaint should itself become a reason to disregard the person making it.

A protection that cannot be reached when it is needed is a protection whose practical operation must be questioned.

11. POLICE RESPONSE, HARASSMENT AND MY EXPERIENCE INSIDE MY HOME

I have described going to hospital after I believed my safety was at risk and after, in my account, police did not come when I reported threats to kill me.

I have described hearing people around my house yelling abuse and experiencing communications and sounds that I believed were connected to interference with electronic devices or Wi-Fi.

I have also described experiences that I call V2K torture, including recurring hostile phrases such as:

“You’re a faggot.”

“You’re a paedophile.”

“No one will help.”

“Kill yourself.”

“We hate you.”

“Give up.”

I am putting these experiences into the record because they have affected my sense of safety, my ability to rest and my experience of being alone in my home.

I have researched patents and technologies that I believe may be relevant to my interpretation of these experiences. I want any specific evidence concerning communications, devices, incidents, witnesses or identifiable perpetrators examined on its merits.

I am not asking readers to ignore my experience because they do not immediately share my explanation for it. I am asking them to take seriously the distress and fear I have described, to examine any available evidence and to help me establish safety.

I accuse those responsible for any actual threats, harassment or unlawful interference established by the evidence. I also accuse the institutions I approached of failing to respond adequately where I reported that I was afraid and needed help.

I refuse to be shamed into silence about what I have experienced.

I want the record examined, and I want the immediate human need addressed: safety, practical assistance, appropriate investigation and access to support.

12. SEXUAL SHAMING, EXPLOITATION AND THE DESTRUCTION OF DIGNITY

I have described experiences in which I believe people approached me when I was desperate for help, exploited my vulnerability, supplied drugs or encouraged drug use, left me financially worse off and then used my circumstances to shame or humiliate me.

I have also described sexual encounters and subsequent derogatory comments about my sexuality.

I am angry about the humiliation I say I experienced. I am angry about what I believe was the exploitation of my vulnerability. I am angry that the search for help became entangled with experiences I describe as degrading and harmful.

I refuse to accept the proposition that a person’s vulnerability gives other people permission to exploit them.

I refuse to accept that sexuality can legitimately be used as a weapon to humiliate or silence someone.

I want specific incidents, communications, witnesses and records examined where they exist. I stand by my account of my experiences and by my demand that conduct involving exploitation, coercion, threats or abuse be taken seriously.

My dignity is not conditional on whether other people approve of my private life.

My need for assistance does not make me available for exploitation.

My poverty does not make me disposable.

13. DANNY, ZACH AND THE QUESTIONS I RAISED ABOUT SAFETY

I have described Danny, whom I understood to be a church elder and an Able Point employee, as having been violently attacked by a client named Zach.

I have said that I warned Able Point for months that the situation was escalating, that I wanted an apprehended violence order pursued and that I believe my concerns were not acted upon as I expected.

I understand that Danny subsequently went on WorkCover. I believe the circumstances raise serious questions about what warnings were received, what risk assessments were undertaken, what protections were considered and what action was taken.

I also believe the circumstances warrant scrutiny of whether Able Point met its responsibilities toward staff and clients.

I am not asking anyone to reach a conclusion without examining the underlying records. I am asking that the records be examined rather than allowing the matter to disappear into a sequence of separate events.

What warnings were given?

Who received them?

What was recorded?

What steps were taken?

What further steps were considered?

What happened after the attack?

These are concrete questions. They deserve concrete answers.

My anger arises from the belief that risks I identified were not adequately addressed. I want the documentary record tested against that belief.

14. MY FEAR FOR MY LIFE AND MY FAMILY

I have described fears that people close to me were preparing to benefit financially from my death. I have also described believing that I was forced into exile and left without the protection I needed.

I have referred to Bill Sheldon and to circumstances that I understand as having contributed to my displacement, fear and isolation.

I have described living in a car with my therapy dog, without the assistance I believed I needed, and fearing that I might be murdered or might not survive my circumstances.

I stand by my account of what I experienced, what I feared and the reasons I sought help.

I want any specific documentary evidence relevant to threats, financial interests, communications, incidents and decisions examined directly.

I do not accept that a person’s fear should be mocked or dismissed simply because the full explanation remains disputed. Equally, I want the underlying evidence to be identified and examined so that concrete events can be distinguished from conclusions about motive.

My central point is not difficult to understand: I have repeatedly said that I felt unsafe, that I needed help and that I could not manage my circumstances alone.

I wanted protection. I wanted someone to take my concerns seriously. I wanted a way to live without feeling that my survival depended entirely on my own ability to withstand the next crisis.

I accuse the people and systems that failed to provide the assistance I sought of contributing to my isolation and distress.

I will not pretend that those consequences were insignificant.

15. THE ASSASSINATION ATTEMPT I REPORTED AND THE QUESTIONS THAT REMAIN

I have described reporting what I believed was an assassination attempt and fleeing because I feared for my life. I have attributed responsibility to Bill Shordon and have said that Ben and an NDIS provider told me that federal police or other agencies had confirmed aspects of the event, including that two alleged assassins had been caught.

I have also described being told that police had confirmed that a girl was paid to fabricate a report about an event in my youth, which I believe was intended to destroy my life.

I stand by my account of what I reported, what I was told and what I understood those communications to mean.

I want the underlying records identified and examined: the original reports, any police incident or investigation numbers, communications from the people who conveyed this information, and any official records that can establish what was actually confirmed.

I accuse the people I believe were responsible for the conduct I reported. I demand that any available evidence be examined rather than allowing the seriousness of the matter to be used as a reason to avoid addressing it.

I have described feeling unable to report a crime effectively and believing that the police system itself was implicated in the failure to help me. I want my reports and the responses to them examined in detail.

I am not asking for a reader to substitute speculation for evidence. I am asking that the specific records that could clarify these events be located and assessed.

The seriousness of what I reported is precisely why the underlying records matter.

16. HUMAN RIGHTS, INTERNATIONAL COMPLAINTS AND THE UNANSWERED APPEAL

I have sought assistance and accountability beyond the agencies with which I have been in conflict.

I have referred to human-rights organisations, international bodies and submissions concerning my treatment, including communications associated with the reference UR/UST/23/AUS/17.

I have described my circumstances as involving political exile and have sought contact with the UNHCR. I have also said that I submitted material to the Office of the United Nations High Commissioner for Human Rights and the International Criminal Court, and that I did not receive the action I hoped for.

I want the receipts, correspondence and responses associated with those submissions preserved and examined.

A submission receipt is evidence that a communication or submission was received through the relevant process; it is not, by itself, proof that the receiving body has accepted jurisdiction, substantiated the allegations or opened an investigation. Those distinctions matter because I want the documentary record to be accurate and the actual response to my appeal to be visible.

But I refuse to treat the existence of an international complaints process as a substitute for practical protection in my daily life.

I have sought help because I believe my circumstances raise serious questions about human rights, disability support, personal safety, access to justice and the treatment of a person who says he has been repeatedly failed by institutions.

I demand that the evidence be examined and that my need for practical assistance not be lost behind jurisdictional boundaries or procedural language.

17. THE MEDIA, PUBLIC SILENCE AND THE RIGHT TO BE HEARD

I have described what I believe is a media silence surrounding my case. I have referred to this as a media ban and have expressed the belief that my attempts to publicise my circumstances have been suppressed.

I stand by my account of my efforts to obtain public attention and by my criticism of the lack of meaningful response I have experienced.

I want any specific evidence of a direction, restriction, communication or decision affecting publication to be identified and examined. I do not want the phrase “media ban” used as a substitute for identifying the records that would establish such a restriction. I want the actual evidence, if available, put before readers.

My broader accusation is that public silence has compounded my isolation. I have built websites, published documents, made videos and asked for help because I have not felt that ordinary channels were producing the response I needed.

I have expressed my belief that professional self-protection, institutional interests and fear of consequences can cause people to remain silent when they should speak.

I cannot establish the private motives of every person who has not responded. I can describe the silence I have experienced, the requests I have made and the consequences of not receiving assistance.

I demand that the public consider the record rather than treating the absence of widespread coverage as proof that there is nothing worth examining.

A person should not need a major media organisation to pronounce their suffering newsworthy before they can receive basic humanitarian assistance.

18. MY SPIRITUAL CONVICTION AND THE WITNESS WHO REMAINS

My understanding of this struggle is not solely administrative or political. It is also spiritual.

I believe in the Most High God. I understand my life, my survival and my testimony through my faith. I believe I have a responsibility to speak about what I have experienced and to expose what I see as corruption, cruelty, hypocrisy and the betrayal of human dignity.

I have described myself as a chosen witness and have drawn upon the image of Christ, sacrifice, truth and divine reckoning.

That is my spiritual conviction. I do not need an institution to authorise it.

I believe that truth requires more than polished declarations. It requires the willingness to face the consequences of what has been done, to confront suffering rather than hide it and to refuse the convenient lie that nobody is responsible because every person can point to someone else.

I believe the witness has a duty to speak.

I believe that those who exercise power have a duty to answer.

I believe that a system that claims to protect vulnerable people must be judged against what happens to vulnerable people in practice.

I have been angry because I believe that the difference between public principles and lived reality has become unbearable.

My faith gives meaning to my refusal to disappear. It gives me a language for endurance, justice and moral reckoning.

I am not asking every reader to share my theology. I am asking them to understand that it is central to how I interpret my life and why I continue to speak.

I have not survived everything I describe merely to become silent at the end.
