1. PURPOSE AND URGENCY
I submit this formal complaint because my circumstances have become intolerable. I am asking each recipient
to recognise the seriousness of my situation, investigate the matters within its jurisdiction, preserve relevant
records, and take appropriate action without further unnecessary delay.
I am living with persistent distress, isolation, physical illness, financial insecurity and a lack of meaningful
assistance. I report crying daily, experiencing pain and feeling trapped in accommodation from which I cannot
practically leave because I have no car, no available money and inadequate practical assistance.
I dispute any characterisation of my circumstances that reduces these practical difficulties to a psychiatric
explanation while leaving the underlying problems unaddressed. Whatever clinical opinions may exist, my lack
of transport, restricted access to money, medical needs and need for effective disability support are matters
capable of independent examination.

I am asking for practical intervention, not another cycle of referrals, dismissals, conflicting explanations or
assessments that leave my circumstances unchanged.
2. COMPLAINT AGAINST ABLEPOINT AND ITS MANAGEMENT AND STAFF
I formally complain about the adequacy, continuity, responsiveness and accountability of the support I report
receiving from AblePoint.
I request an independent examination of the conduct of the organisation, its management, its rostering and
complaints processes, and every worker involved in my support.
My concerns include:
1. Inadequate practical assistance. I report that essential household tasks and daily support needs have
not been met adequately. In particular, I describe an incident involving a support worker identified to me
as John, who was expected to provide assistance but refused to wash my dishes, reportedly because
they were too dirty. I request that this incident, the applicable support obligations and the relevant
service records be examined.
2. Failure to respond adequately to distress and vulnerability. I report that I am frequently alone,
crying and experiencing significant distress, without meaningful assistance that improves my
circumstances.
3. Continuity and quality of care. I request a review of staffing arrangements, attendance records,
support plans, missed or incomplete tasks, risk assessments, incident reports and the actual services
delivered.
4. Safety and incident management. I request an audit of whether reported safety concerns and any
relevant threats or serious incidents were documented, escalated and reported to the appropriate
authorities where required.
5. Accountability of management. I request the full legal name of AblePoint’s chief executive, the
identity of the responsible provider entity, its relevant registration details, and the names and positions
of the people responsible for decisions affecting my care.
I have identified Rachael as the chief executive. I request confirmation of her full professional identity and role
through the appropriate corporate and provider records. I ask that this be verified rather than assuming,
without evidence, that any identification issue constitutes a criminal offence.
I am particularly concerned that AblePoint may continue receiving payments for services while I remain in
circumstances in which my practical needs are not being adequately met. I request an audit of the services
claimed, the services actually delivered, the applicable service agreements and the records supporting those
claims.
I request that AblePoint provide a written response addressing each concern individually and identifying the
corrective action taken or proposed.
3. COMPLAINT TO THE NSW PUBLIC GUARDIAN — ACCESS TO MY MONEY AND PERSONAL
AUTONOMY
I formally complain about the restrictions affecting my access to my Commonwealth Bank accounts and the
resulting impact on my independence, welfare and ability to obtain essential services.
I understand that my access to Commonwealth Bank internet and mobile banking was blocked in connection
with arrangements involving the Public Guardian. I have previously identified Philip Glass as a person involved in
communications about my financial access. I request verification of the precise decisions, authorities and
individuals involved.
I report that I have no practical access to sufficient funds to meet my immediate needs, including transport,
medical appointments and other necessities.
Restricting access to money can have consequences extending well beyond financial administration. Without a
workable way to access funds, I can lose the practical ability to obtain transport, arrange medical care,
purchase necessities and exercise personal autonomy.
I therefore request:
1. Written identification of the legal instrument, order, appointment or other authority relied upon to restrict
my banking access.

2. A copy of the relevant decision and an explanation of its scope, duration and review arrangements,
subject to any lawful limitations.
3. An itemised account of transactions, withdrawals, payments, fees and transfers made on my behalf,
together with supporting records and the identity of the decision-maker for each disputed transaction.
4. An explanation of any proposed or completed further withdrawals from my funds.
5. An urgent assessment of my immediate living expenses and access to money for food, transport, medical
care and personal necessities.
6. Consideration of a lawful, practical arrangement that gives me appropriate access to funds while
addressing any substantiated risks identified by the responsible authority.
7. Written information about review, complaint and appeal rights, including any available independent
review mechanism.
8. Confirmation of how my wishes, preferences, decision-making capacity and right to participate in
decisions have been considered.
I ask the Public Guardian to distinguish any genuine protective purpose from the practical consequences of its
decisions. A financial arrangement that leaves a person unable to meet essential needs requires urgent scrutiny
and an effective response.

means to direct my own life.

consequences involved.

PUBLIC STATEMENT FROM BARRAN DODGER
OF THE BARRAN DODGER ARCHIVE
THE WITNESS REMAINS: A WHISTLEBLOWERS TESTIMONY OF SURVIVAL TESTIMONY & FAITH
The Barran Dodger Archive is a public documentary and spiritual record established to preserve, organise and
make accessible a decades-long body of evidence concerning alleged institutional, administrative, financial and
human-rights harms.
It brings together official records, correspondence, documentary evidence, personal testimony, forensic
analysis, research, philosophy, spiritual gospels and prophetic declarations, including writings understood by
the author to have been anointed by the Most High God.
The Archive deliberately distinguishes between documented facts, official records, corroborated evidence,
allegations, personal testimony, inference, interpretation and matters of faith. This distinction is fundamental to
its purpose: readers are invited to examine the underlying evidence for themselves, test the claims, consider
the sources and reach their own conclusions.
At the heart of the Archive is a testimony of survival and preservation.
What the author believes was subjected to attempts at silencing, erasure, exclusion or institutional
disappearance has nevertheless survived in records, documents, testimony, memory, faith and public
disclosure.
The central declaration is simple:
THE WITNESS REMAINS.
The Archive therefore represents both an evidentiary record and a spiritual testimony. The author understands
his survival through a faith-based conviction that the Most High God protected, preserved and gave purpose to
what human institutions could not erase.
The spiritual writings and prophetic declarations within the Archive are presented as matters of faith and
personal interpretation. They are preserved alongside the documentary record so that the complete testimony
— evidentiary, personal, philosophical and spiritual — can be understood in its own context.
The purpose is not to demand belief.
It is to preserve the record.
It is to make the evidence accessible.
It is to ensure that the surviving witness can be examined.
And it is to leave the historical record open to independent scrutiny.
THE ARCHIVE IS 100% FREE TO ACCESS
https://barrandodgerarchive.base44.app
KNOWLEDGE IS FREE
The Barran Dodger Archive is founded on the principle that knowledge, evidence and truth should be freely
accessible.

There is no paywall for the public documentary record. Research, testimony, philosophy, spiritual writings,
prophetic declarations and public-interest material are made available for people to examine for themselves.
Access to knowledge should not depend upon a person’s ability to pay.
The Archive is therefore freely accessible, while its preservation and continued publication require practical
resources.

financial hardship and vulnerability, and as an internally displaced asylum seeker within the country of his

Those circumstances create practical needs extending beyond the preservation of documents.
Voluntary donations are therefore accepted from anyone who freely wishes to assist.
Support may contribute towards essential needs including food, safe accommodation, communication,

A donation does not purchase access to the Archive.

If you are blessed with sufficient prosperity to help preserve this work, your voluntary support can help ensure
that financial hardship does not become another mechanism through which a public record disappears.
