BARRAN DODGER ARCHIVE — FULL-CAPACITY FORENSIC REPORT SERIES

DOCUMENT STATUS: PUBLIC ARCHIVE ANALYTICAL REPORT
METHOD: FULL DETAIL / NO COMPRESSION / PRESERVE ITEMISATION
EVIDENCE PRINCIPLE: DOCUMENT THE RECORD; DISTINGUISH RECORD FROM TESTIMONY, ALLEGATION, INFERENCE, INTERPRETATION AND UNRESOLVED MATTERS.

IMPORTANT LIMITATION
This report is a structured analytical reconstruction of material exposed through the public Barran Dodger archive and the public repository structure. It does not convert archive assertions into judicial findings. Where a proposition is not independently established, it is labelled accordingly. Counts appearing on different archive pages may represent different inventories, dates, repositories or verification stages and are not silently reconciled.

STANDARD EVIDENCE CLASSIFICATION
A — DOCUMENTED FACT: Supported directly by a reliable primary record or authoritative determination.
B — CORROBORATED CLAIM: Supported by more than one independent or materially corroborating source without necessarily constituting an official finding.
C — DOCUMENTED ALLEGATION: Recorded in a complaint, statement, correspondence or other document but not independently established.
D — INFERENCE: A conclusion drawn from documented circumstances rather than directly established.
E — INTERPRETATION: Analytical, legal, philosophical or theological interpretation.
F — UNRESOLVED: Evidence is incomplete, conflicting, unavailable or insufficient for a stronger classification.

CORE EVIDENCE COMMAND
CLAIM → IDENTIFY → SOURCE → DATE → ACTOR / INSTITUTION → PRIMARY RECORD → CORROBORATION → CONTRADICTORY / LIMITING EVIDENCE → OFFICIAL RESPONSE → FINDING STATUS → LEGAL / ANALYTICAL FRAMEWORK → CONSEQUENCE → VERIFICATION → PRESERVATION.

PROVENANCE COMMAND
SOURCE → ORIGINAL FILE → ORIGINAL FILENAME → REPOSITORY → PATH → ACQUISITION DATE → PUBLICATION DATE → HASH → GIT COMMIT → GIT BLOB → TIMESTAMP / SEAL → VERSION HISTORY → RELATED RECORDS → PUBLIC LINK.

REPORT 2 — THE 35-YEAR CHRONOLOGY

PRIMARY FUNCTION
To reconstruct the chronological sequence from approximately 1990 through 2026 while keeping each event separate from later interpretation.

CHRONOLOGY RULE
The fact that events appear in a sequence does not by itself establish that they share a cause. The chronology therefore records events first and tests relationships second.

STANDARD EVENT FIELDS
DATE; LOCATION; PERSON / INSTITUTION; EVENT; PRIMARY DOCUMENT; SOURCE; WHAT THE RECORD ESTABLISHES; AUTHOR ACCOUNT; OFFICIAL RESPONSE; CONSEQUENCE; CORROBORATION; CONTRADICTION; FINDING; UNRESOLVED QUESTION.

1. 1990–2000 — EARLIEST LONGITUDINAL MATERIAL
The archive identifies approximately 1990 as the beginning of its broader 35-year chronology. The period should be reconstructed event by event rather than retrospectively treated as a single campaign. Relevant records include education, employment, family or personal history where publishable, early advocacy, journalism, creative work and contemporaneous correspondence.

2. 2000–2010 — PROFESSIONAL, PUBLICATION AND ADVOCACY HISTORY
This period should document professional work, published writing, mental-health advocacy, creative work, education and institutional recognition where independently documented. The publication history should distinguish bibliographic fact from later claims concerning the significance of the work.

3. 2010–2019 — EDUCATION, ADVOCACY AND DEVELOPING DISPUTES
Each material event should be separated into date, institution, document and consequence. Later accounts should not be back-projected into earlier periods without contemporaneous support.

4. 2020 — PhD / ACADEMIC MILESTONE
The archive identifies a Victoria University PhD completed in 2020 and connects it to research involving AI and posthumanism. The authoritative university record should be treated as the primary source. The significance of the degree for later disputes is an analytical question, not an automatic evidentiary consequence.

5. 2021 — MAJOR HEALTH EVENT
The archive describes a major 2021 health event, resuscitation and acquired brain injury. The chronology must distinguish hospital admission; clinical observations; discharge diagnosis; patient report; later interpretation; and any independent neurological or psychiatric assessment. Medical conclusions should be quoted from records rather than reconstructed from later summaries.

6. 2021–2022 — PSYCHIATRIC AND ADMINISTRATIVE INTERVENTIONS
The archive reports multiple involuntary psychiatric interventions and at least one page gives a specific count. Each episode requires its own legal authority, clinical basis, duration, outcome and review trail. Claims of unlawfulness, retaliation or coordination remain allegations unless supported by an appropriate finding.

7. 2022 — CORPORATE / IDENTITY RECORDS
The archive reports numerous alleged unauthorised or fraudulent business registrations. The existence of each registration can be checked against ASIC records; the proposition that a registration was fraudulent requires evidence concerning authorisation, filing, identity use, objections and response.

8. 2022 — TRUST / CORPORATE RECORD
The archive identifies The Trustee for barrandodger.com and reports ABN 78 833 496 164 and an active date of 7 August 2022. This should be checked against the authoritative ABR/ASIC record.

9. 2023 — PUBLIC INTEREST DISCLOSURE
The archive identifies PID 2023/Krypton and describes a disclosure involving more than 21 allegations. The receipt and processing of a PID are distinct from proof of the allegations contained within it. The primary PID documents should be indexed separately from the later analytical conclusions.

10. 2023 — NDIA RESPONSE
The archive identifies an institutional response and an authorised officer, and describes acknowledgement, framework information, response to allegations and requests for further information. The exact document should be linked and its wording preserved.

11. 27 MARCH 2023 — FEDERAL COURT GENERAL COUNSEL CORRESPONDENCE
The public archive preserves a letter attributed to Federal Court General Counsel Scott Tredwell stating that, on the information provided, he was satisfied that Dr McLean was or had been an employee with the Department of Social Services. The evidentiary proposition is the existence and content of the letter; broader implications require separate analysis.

12. 2023–2024 — MULTI-AGENCY CONTACT
The archive identifies correspondence with ASIC, ATO, Commonwealth Ombudsman, NDIA, NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission, Australian Human Rights Commission, police, courts and other bodies. Each interaction must be treated as its own evidence chain.

13. 2024–2025 — DIGITAL ARCHIVE DEVELOPMENT
The material develops from individual records into public indexes, finding aids, evidence graphs, official-document catalogues, forensic analyses and repository mirrors. The event itself is the creation and expansion of the archive; the conclusions drawn from the archive remain separate.

14. 2025 — FORENSIC ANALYSIS EXPANSION
The archive reports AI-assisted analysis of documents, claims, relationships and financial consequences. These analyses should be treated as analytical outputs and checked back against their cited primary documents.

15. 2025–2026 — EVIDENCE PRESERVATION
GitHub repositories, mirrors, hashes, blockchain timestamps and public indexes are used to preserve records. These are preservation mechanisms rather than proof of substantive truth.

16. 2026 — FORENSIC REPORT PUBLICATION
The public forensic page currently exposes a particular project inventory and forensic framing. Because the page is a project publication, its claims should be cited as project-reported unless the underlying source has been independently verified.

17. 2026 — INTERNATIONAL SUBMISSIONS / CLAIMS
The archive reports material relating to ICC, UNHCR and UN human-rights mechanisms. The chronology must distinguish preparation, submission, acknowledgement, registration, admissibility, investigation and finding.

18. 2026 — FINANCIAL FORENSIC RECORD
The archive publishes large financial-loss calculations. These belong in the chronology as publication and analytical milestones; the amount itself requires reconstruction under Report 8.

19. 2026 — HOUSING / SUPPORT / FINANCIAL CRISIS MATERIAL
Public pages describe severe financial and support circumstances. Each allegation concerning threats, coercion, provider conduct or institutional responsibility must be tied to a dated primary record and official response.

20. 2026 — PERMANENT PUBLIC RECORD
The archive presents itself as a long-term public documentary record. This creates a final chronological phase: preservation, publication, verification and future independent review.

CHRONOLOGY QUALITY CONTROL
For every event ask: is the date contemporaneous; is the source primary; is the event independently corroborated; is the later narrative consistent with the contemporaneous record; did an institution make a finding; is there contradictory evidence; and what remains unresolved?

THE CENTRAL DISTINCTION
A long chronology can demonstrate that many events occurred across time. It cannot by chronology alone prove that all events were coordinated, unlawful or caused by a single actor. Those propositions require connecting evidence.


DOCUMENTARY WARNING
The existence of a document establishes that the document exists. It does not automatically establish the truth of every statement inside it. A complaint establishes that a complaint was made; it does not itself establish the complained-of conduct. A court filing establishes that a filing occurred; it does not establish that the court accepted every proposition. A government record establishes what the government recorded or communicated; it does not necessarily establish that every interpretation of that record is correct. A blockchain timestamp can assist with integrity and chronology; it does not establish the substantive truth of a document.

READER TEST
For every significant proposition ask: Who says this? When did they say it? What did they rely upon? Is the original record available? Was it contemporaneous? Is there independent corroboration? Is there contradictory evidence? Did a competent authority make a finding? What remains unresolved?

EVIDENCE AUDIT CHECKLIST

1. Every major proposition has a unique Evidence ID.
    2. The original document or authoritative source is identified.
    3. The public-facing source link is named and retained.
    4. The document date is distinguished from upload/publication date.
    5. The institution and individual role are recorded precisely.
    6. The evidence classification does not exceed what the source supports.
    7. Allegations are not silently converted into findings.
    8. Later interpretations are not substituted for contemporaneous records.
    9. Independent corroboration is identified separately from repository duplicates.
    10. Contradictory or limiting evidence is disclosed.
    11. Financial figures are reproducible from source records.
    12. Causation is distinguished from temporal sequence.
    13. Institutional knowledge is supported by receipt/acknowledgement evidence.
    14. Legal frameworks are identified without asserting a legal conclusion beyond the source.
    15. Privacy status is recorded before publication.
    16. Repository provenance and version history are preserved.
    17. The reader can return to the primary document without relying on AI summary.
    18. Unresolved questions remain visible rather than being rhetorically closed.

READER INDEPENDENCE STATEMENT

The purpose of this report is to increase the inspectability of the record. A reader should be able to disagree with the archive’s interpretation while still having direct access to the underlying material. The report therefore separates documentary existence, substantive content, corroboration, official findings and interpretation. The final determination of contested facts belongs to the appropriate evidence-review, regulatory, judicial or other competent process.
