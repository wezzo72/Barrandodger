## Abstract

This paper examines, in the ordinary and not the criminal-law sense, the documentary record that Dr Richard William McLean has placed in public GitHub repositories concerning the National Disability Insurance Scheme, policing and police-oversight bodies, ombudsmen, integrity agencies, tribunals, legal assistance, guardianship administration, and public communication. It is written to the author's instruction that the structure be prosecutorial in force and evidentiary in method. That instruction is accepted only with the constraint the author also set: a document's presence on GitHub does not prove the misconduct alleged in its title.

The paper therefore distinguishes four statuses. A **catalogue fact** is a statement that a named repository file or index row exists. A **record fact** is a statement taken from text extracted in this examination from a file that was opened. An **author allegation** is a proposition advanced by Dr McLean, including in filenames, cover sheets, and audio titles. An **inference** is a conclusion drawn from more than one record, and is labelled as such. Where a file was not opened, this paper does not quote it and does not treat its filename as proof of its contents.

On 9 October 2026 the presentation repository `wezzo72/Barrandodger` described itself as a website front for an existing corpus, not a replacement of the evidence stores. Its README, recounted on 4 October 2026, stated that the tree held 5,583 files, including 2,840 PDFs and 1,813 HTML files, and that `docs/official-drive/` held 95 PDFs. The same README stated: "A filename is not a finding." The Phase 2 inventory of 19 September 2026 said the Backup documents folder then held 531 PDFs, and that SHA-256 of those PDF bytes had not been calculated in that phase. The allegation matrix of the same presentation site recorded 150 items and the finding "NONE DECLARED."

Four public PDFs named in the catalogue were opened for this edition. One, the Law Enforcement Conduct Commission letter of 19 November 2025, reference CASE202512395/13KM, is a clean official letter and is quoted. One Victorian Ombudsman file contains both an official section 29A decision and the author's own request language. Two other files catalogued as official letters opened, in whole or in part, behind an author-publication wrapper. Those mismatches are themselves part of the record. They are not used to upgrade any allegation into a finding.

## 1. The question this paper is willing to ask

The author asked that the examination put a single question to the institutions that held authority while he says he was losing the practical ability to protect himself, obtain representation, give meaningful consent, or secure an effective response:

> When a person repeatedly asks for assistance, supplies documentary evidence, identifies the consequences of institutional decisions, seeks representation, seeks protection and attempts to exercise or recover control over decisions affecting his life, what responsibility remains with the institutions that possess the authority to act when they repeatedly do not?

That question is accepted as the organising question. It is not accepted as a conclusion. Responsibility, in law and in administration, is not established by the fact that a person asked. It is established, if at all, by showing what was placed before a body, what authority that body had, what it did, what it refused, and what followed. This paper sets out the public material from which that demonstration can begin. It does not complete it across 2,840 PDFs.

The author also asked that the paper be approximately 50,000 words, carry full APA references, be exported as a PDF, be timestamped on a blockchain, and be uploaded to the GitHub repository. The PDF, the hash, the OpenTimestamps stamp, and the upload are done. The length is not inflated to 50,000 words. A 50,000-word text that repeated the same ten questions against unread PDF interiors would breach the evidence rule the author set in the same instruction. Word count is reported in the colophon from the text of this edition, not asserted in advance.

## 6. Record fact: the LECC letter of 19 November 2025

The file `2025-11-19_LECC_Out_Of_Jurisdiction.pdf` was downloaded from `wezzo72/Backup` and text-extracted. The extracted text is an official letter. It is not an author wrapper. The following is the substance of that letter.

The letterhead is the Law Enforcement Conduct Commission, Level 3, 111 Elizabeth Street, Sydney NSW 2000, postal address GPO Box 3880, Sydney NSW 2001, www.lecc.nsw.gov.au. The reference is CASE202512395/13KM. The date is 19 November 2025. It is addressed to Richard William McLean by email. It says: "I refer to your correspondence dated 13-16 November 2025." It says: "Your complaint doesn't concern officers of the New South Wales Police Force or NSW Crime Commission." It says: "It is therefore 'outside our jurisdiction'." It says: "This means we cannot take any action on the information you have given us."

The letter then states what the LECC does. It says the LECC is an independent body that oversees the police. It says the law says that the NSW Police Force is responsible for managing and investigating complaints of misconduct by police, and that this means the LECC refers most complaints to the police. It says the LECC's role is to review how police handle complaints, and to investigate cases of police misconduct and corruption where it thinks it is appropriate. It says there are some types of complaint it cannot investigate. It is signed by the Assessments Team, Law Enforcement Conduct Commission. The extracted text is one page.

**What this record establishes.** It establishes that on 19 November 2025 the LECC Assessments Team wrote to Richard William McLean about correspondence dated 13-16 November 2025, assigned reference CASE202512395/13KM, and stated that the complaint did not concern officers of the NSW Police Force or the NSW Crime Commission and was therefore outside jurisdiction, and that the Commission would not take action on the information given. It establishes that the letter explained the ordinary division of function between the NSW Police Force and the LECC.

**What this record does not establish.** It does not establish what the 13-16 November 2025 correspondence said. That correspondence was not opened in this edition. It does not establish that NSW Police received a complaint, investigated it, or declined it. It does not establish misconduct by any police officer. It does not establish that the LECC's jurisdictional statement was wrong in law. It does not establish that a referral was made to NSW Police. The letter says the LECC refers most complaints to the police. It does not say that this complaint was referred.

**The questions, applied to this record.**

What did they know? They knew the contents of correspondence dated 13-16 November 2025. This paper has not read that correspondence.

When did they know it? The letter says the correspondence was dated 13-16 November 2025 and the response is dated 19 November 2025. The interval on the face of the letter is days, not years. That interval is a record fact about these dates. It is not a finding about other complaints.

What documents were placed before them? Correspondence of 13-16 November 2025, contents not extracted here.

What authority did they possess? On the letter's own account, authority over complaints concerning NSW Police Force officers and the NSW Crime Commission, with a statutory division under which NSW Police manages and investigates most misconduct complaints and the LECC reviews that handling and investigates where it considers it appropriate.

What intervention was available? The letter says action was not available because the complaint did not concern those officers. Whether some other intervention was available is not stated in the letter.

What did they actually do? They assessed the complaint as outside jurisdiction and wrote that they could not take action.

What did they refuse to do? They refused, in terms, to take any action on the information given.

What did they fail to investigate? The letter says they could not investigate this complaint. Failure is the author's characterisation if it means they ought to have investigated. The record shows a jurisdictional closure, not a completed misconduct investigation.

What consequences followed? Not stated in this letter.

Who still had power? On the letter's own account, the NSW Police Force retains the ordinary responsibility to manage and investigate complaints of misconduct by its officers. This letter does not show that a complaint was then before NSW Police.

This is the pattern the author asked the paper to isolate: a formal response, a boundary, and no substantive investigation visible in the response itself. The pattern is visible in this letter. Whether the boundary was correctly drawn depends on the unseen correspondence of 13-16 November 2025.

## 9. Consent, representation, and the sentence that cannot yet be written

The author wants a sentence of this kind to be tested, not assumed: that institutional structures designed to protect autonomy operated, in his experience, as instruments through which autonomy was displaced. The test he set is the record. The records most relevant to that sentence, on the catalogue, are the NCAT file, the NSW Trustee and Guardian file, the Legal Aid NSW advice file, and any Public Guardian file not yet separated in the index. They were not opened. The sentence therefore cannot be written as a finding.

What can be written is the author's position, the existence of the filenames, and the questions those files have to answer: who the decision-maker was; what order or advice was made; whether the author was a party; whether he was heard; whether reasons were given; whether a review was available; whether he used it; and whether a representative continued to act after he asked that the representation stop. Those questions are the examination. They are not the result.

## 10. Cumulative consequence

The author asked that poverty, housing insecurity, loss of income, barriers to legal assistance, disability-service failure, police responses, guardianship decisions, media silence, and repeated referral be read in sequence rather than as isolated incidents, and that the sequence be described as civil erasure: his term for the cumulative loss of recognition, agency, representation, and practical access. He also asked that the paper not require the reader to accept that word, but demonstrate the chronology and let the conclusion arise.

The chronology this edition can demonstrate is the chronology of filenames plus two opened process outcomes: a Victorian Ombudsman FOI exclusion under section 29A, and an LECC jurisdictional closure. A Commonwealth Ombudsman restriction is legible as a heading and not as a full set of reasons. That is not yet a demonstrated sequence of deprivation. It is a demonstrated sequence of process responses and a larger sequence of unpublished-to-this-paper filenames. Civil erasure remains the author's description. It is not this paper's finding.

The cumulative question is still a proper question. If later editions open the NDIA, AFP, DSS, Federal Court, NCAT, Trustee, and Legal Aid files and those files show repeated referral, refusal, or non-response after notice of the same underlying circumstances, the inference will be available to a reader. It is not available from the four files opened here, because three of them do not reach the merits and the fourth was not the letter its filename promised.

## 11. What would count as the indictment the author asked for

The author asked for an evidentiary indictment in the ordinary sense: a structured presentation of documented conduct demanding explanation, naming institutions and individuals where the public record permits, linking each material proposition to its source, distinguishing fact from allegation and inference, and refusing to let institutional terminology replace examination. He also asked that the paper not manufacture findings the evidence does not support.

This edition is that instrument at the scale of the records actually opened. It names the LECC Assessments Team as the signatory on the 19 November 2025 letter, because that is what the letter says. It does not name individual officers, guardians, lawyers, journalists, or Public Guardian staff, because `people.json` deliberately did not add third parties from filenames, and this edition did not open files that would identify them as decision-makers. Naming from a filename would breach the author's own evidence rule.

The strongest available statement is therefore not an adjective. It is this:

- A letter was written by the LECC on 19 November 2025. It said the complaint was outside jurisdiction and that no action would be taken on the information given.
- A Victorian Ombudsman file applies section 29A and thereby does not release complaint documents. The requester's characterisations sit in the same file and are not findings.
- A Commonwealth Ombudsman file shows a decision to restrict dated 8 August 2025, reference 2024-706941, behind an author cover that makes claims the letter has not been shown to confirm.
- A file catalogued as an IBAC rejection did not, on download, present as that rejection.
- Twenty timeline rows exist. Sixteen were not opened. Their dates are filename dates.

That is the indictment this record presently supports: not a proved scheme, but a public corpus whose own indexes refuse premature findings, and a small opened sample in which the visible institutional act is closure, exclusion, or restriction rather than a merits investigation. Whether that pattern holds across the unopened files is the next question. It is not an answer.
