## Abstract

This paper examines, in the ordinary and not the criminal-law sense, the documentary record that Dr Richard William McLean has placed in public GitHub repositories concerning the National Disability Insurance Scheme, policing and police-oversight bodies, ombudsmen, integrity agencies, tribunals, legal assistance, guardianship, financial management, and public communication. The structure is prosecutorial in the sense that it asks what was known, what authority existed, what was done, what was refused, and what followed. The method is evidentiary in the sense that a proposition is graded. A catalogue fact is the existence of a named file or index row. A record fact is text extracted from a file opened for this edition. An author allegation is a proposition advanced by Dr McLean, including in filenames, cover sheets, and audio titles. An inference is a conclusion drawn from more than one record and is labelled as such.

## 1. The question, and the limit on the answer

The organising question is the question put by the author of the archive: when a person repeatedly asks for assistance, supplies documentary evidence, identifies the consequences of institutional decisions, seeks representation, seeks protection, and attempts to exercise or recover control over decisions affecting his life, what responsibility remains with the institutions that possess the authority to act when they repeatedly do not?

That question is accepted as the organising question. It is not accepted as a conclusion. Responsibility is not established by the fact that a person asked. It is established, if at all, by showing what was placed before a body, what authority that body had, what it did, what it refused, and what followed. This paper sets out the public material from which that demonstration can begin, and it applies the same questions at length to every catalogued body and timeline row. Length is not a finding. A long examination of a filename remains an examination of a filename.

The author described the cumulative experience as civil erasure: his term for the loss of recognition, agency, representation, and practical access to institutions that are ordinarily supposed to provide protection and accountability. The paper records that description as his language. It does not ask the reader to accept the word. It places the opened chronology beside the word and leaves the inference labelled.

The author also required that neither the institutions nor he be permitted to hide behind ambiguity. That requirement cuts both ways. An institution cannot answer a merits question by pointing only to a policy, a referral, or a jurisdictional boundary, unless the record shows that the boundary was the lawful and actual response. The author cannot answer an evidentiary question by pointing only to a cover sheet that says the archive has already proved suppression. The cover sheets opened in this edition say that. The letters behind some of them do not say that. Both facts are recorded.

## 2. Method and status labels

The repositories inspected were https://github.com/wezzo72/Barrandodger, https://github.com/wezzo72/Backup, and, as named homes only, https://github.com/wezzo72/Myaidrive and https://github.com/drbarrandodger/barran-dodger-archive. Those indexes contain their own cautions. institutions.json says each row is an organisation named on a public filename and that a possible integrity issue is not asserted. organisations.json says listing is not a finding. timeline.json says a filename date is not a verified event date and that the list is not a complete 35-year chronology. people.json seeds one person and says third parties named only in filenames were not added as confirmed actors. This edition follows those cautions, with one addition. Where an opened letter names a signatory, that signatory is reported as named on the letter. Four statuses are used throughout. Catalogue fact means the index or path exists. Record fact means the sentence was extracted from an opened file. Author allegation means the proposition is the author's, including a filename or a cover claim. Inference means a conclusion offered from more than one record, and the paper says so. Where a file was not opened, the paper does not quote it.

Audio files titled as death-threat, investigator, or murder-declaration evidence were not opened. Their titles remain author allegations. This paper does not treat them as evidence of a death threat or of a homicide. Payment details printed on author cover sheets are not republished.

## 4. Record fact: the LECC letter of 19 November 2025

The file 2025-11-19_LECC_Out_Of_Jurisdiction.pdf was downloaded from wezzo72/Backup and text-extracted. The extracted text is an official letter, not an author wrapper. The letterhead is the Law Enforcement Conduct Commission, Level 3, 111 Elizabeth Street, Sydney NSW 2000. The reference is CASE202512395/13KM. The date is 19 November 2025. It is addressed to Richard William McLean. It says: I refer to your correspondence dated 13-16 November 2025. It says the complaint does not concern officers of the New South Wales Police Force or the NSW Crime Commission. It says it is therefore outside our jurisdiction. It says this means we cannot take any action on the information you have given us.

The letter states that the LECC is an independent body that oversees the police, that the law says the NSW Police Force is responsible for managing and investigating complaints of misconduct by police, and that this means the LECC refers most complaints to the police. It says the LECC's role is to review how police handle complaints and to investigate cases of police misconduct and corruption where it thinks it is appropriate. It is signed by the Assessments Team.

What this record establishes is a jurisdictional closure on these dates, about correspondence whose contents were not opened in this edition. What it does not establish is the contents of the 13-16 November correspondence, a referral, a police investigation, misconduct by any officer, or that the jurisdictional statement was wrong in law. The interval on the face of the letter is days. That interval is a record fact about these dates. It is not a finding about other complaints.

The questions applied to this record are these. What did they know? The contents of correspondence dated 13-16 November 2025, not extracted here. When? The response is dated 19 November 2025. What authority? On the letter's own account, authority over complaints concerning NSW Police Force officers and the NSW Crime Commission, with ordinary management of most misconduct complaints remaining with NSW Police. What did they do? They assessed the complaint as outside jurisdiction and wrote that they could not take action. What did they refuse? Action on the information given. What consequence followed? Not stated in the letter. Who still had power? On the letter's own account, the NSW Police Force retains ordinary responsibility for complaints about its officers. The letter does not show that a complaint was then before NSW Police.

This is the pattern the examination isolates: a formal response, a boundary, and no substantive investigation visible in the response itself. Whether the boundary was correctly drawn depends on the unseen correspondence.

## 11. What the opened sample shows when read together

Read together, and only together, the opened sample shows a person receiving, within a short calendar span, an NDIA plan approval stating a large funding total, a Legal Aid advice letter about dissatisfaction with the Public Guardian and guardianship orders, and a Trustee certificate saying estate management committed in October 2024 remained in place in January 2026. Earlier in the opened sample, oversight and complaint bodies had closed, excluded, or restricted particular approaches: LECC by jurisdiction, the Victorian Ombudsman by section 29A, the Commonwealth Ombudsman by a restriction heading whose reasons were not extracted.

The inference available from that combination, and it is only an inference, is that formal machinery was operating. Plans were approved. Certificates were issued. Advice was given. Complaints were bounded. The inference not available is that the machinery amounted to meaningful assistance, protection, and representation, or that it amounted to a coordinated deprivation. Both of those conclusions require the interiors this edition does not have: NCAT reasons, Public Guardian decisions, the plan attachment, the police correspondence the LECC did not act on, and the replies, if any, to the outward letters.

The author's civil-erasure description is intelligible against this combination, because a person can hold an approved plan and a guardianship dispute at the same time, and can be told that representation on the order itself is unlikely to meet a merit test. Intelligibility is not proof. The paper leaves the description as the author's and the combination as the record.
