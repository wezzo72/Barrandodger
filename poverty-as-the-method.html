Silence, and what the law does with it

Silence is not a legal admission. An Australian court does not treat a failure to answer a website as a confession. Jones v Dunkel allows a limited inference where a party in a hearing fails to call a witness who could have spoken. It does not apply to a family member who has not been summoned. A person named on a public page can sue in defamation, demand a correction, or say nothing. The third choice is lawful. The significance is practical, not doctrinal. A denial would have been evidence. A proceeding would have been evidence. The file contains neither. The reader is left with the accusation and the absence of the contradiction.
