To: Philip Glass, NSW Trustee & Guardian

Philip,

FORMAL OBJECTION AND DEMAND FOR ACTION — ACCOMMODATION, FINANCIAL HARDSHIP, ABLE POINT, NDIS FUNDING, LOSS OF INDEPENDENCE AND INSTITUTIONAL ACCOUNTABILITY

I am writing to place my formal objection, my personal testimony, my documentary record and my demands for accountability directly before you. This is not another polite request to be passed between departments, reduced to a case note, answered with a formulaic paragraph or buried beneath the language of administrative procedure. I am demanding a substantive response to the circumstances of my life, the decisions affecting my independence and welfare, and the cumulative consequences of the treatment I have experienced.

I have spent decades contributing to Australian society through art, illustration, journalism, writing, music, advocacy, scholarship and independent thought. I completed a PhD in 2020. I have devoted a substantial part of my life to advocating for vulnerable people and their carers. I have created, researched, written, documented and preserved evidence because I believed that knowledge, compassion, truth and public accountability mattered.

I gave and gave and gave of my life and creativity in good faith. I contributed to the cultural, intellectual and democratic life of this country because I believed that a democracy was capable of protecting the dignity of its citizens, particularly those experiencing vulnerability.

Yet I now find myself struggling to secure the basic conditions of dignity, independence, accommodation, financial stability and meaningful assistance within the very democracy to which I have devoted so much of my life.

That is the contradiction at the heart of this letter.

I regard the treatment I have experienced as profoundly unjust. I believe I have suffered institutional betrayal, serious detriment, marginalisation and the erosion of my independence. I have described the cumulative effect as political exile within the democracy of my own citizenship. I am not suggesting that my citizenship has legally disappeared. I am describing the extraordinary reality of feeling driven to seek protection, security and recognition within the very society whose institutions I believe have contributed to my circumstances.

I should not have to feel like an exile in my own country. I should not have to fight for basic dignity after spending decades trying to contribute something worthwhile to the society around me. And I should not have to watch my life reduced to a budget, a file number, an accommodation arrangement or a series of disconnected administrative decisions while the cumulative consequences remain inadequately addressed.

Public office is a responsibility to the public, not a sanctuary from accountability.

You may believe that your position provides protection from the consequences of decisions made within the system you serve. It does not remove your moral responsibility to consider what those decisions do to a human being. An official title does not make a person infallible. An administrative procedure does not automatically make an outcome just. Institutional loyalty cannot be permitted to replace independent judgment, compassion or the obligation to give reasons for decisions that affect another person’s life.

I expect you to read this letter in its entirety, respond to its substance and address the matters raised below individually rather than dismissing them through a general statement that fails to answer the questions being asked.

1. Accommodation, Able Point and my right to independence

I object to the circumstances in which I have been placed and to any assumption that my current accommodation arrangement should simply be treated as an acceptable or permanent solution without proper examination of my wishes, circumstances, rights and available alternatives.

I have made clear that I do not wish to remain at Able Point. I have raised concerns about my independence, my treatment and my ability to leave. I require a transparent explanation of the arrangements under which I am staying, the authority relied upon, the decisions made on my behalf and the practical process available to me if I wish to leave.

I require copies of every relevant agreement, occupancy document, service agreement, consent record, assessment, decision and communication concerning my placement.

If you contend that I am legally or contractually obliged to remain, identify the precise document and legal basis on which that assertion rests. If you contend that I am free to leave, explain the practical arrangements available to facilitate my departure, including how accommodation, personal belongings, finances, transport and continuity of support will be managed.

Do not confuse the provision of a bed with the provision of a life. Accommodation that leaves a person feeling powerless, trapped or deprived of meaningful choice cannot be assessed solely by whether a roof exists above their head. Independence, security, privacy, dignity and the ability to exercise genuine choice matter too.

I want a written response identifying the person responsible for coordinating my accommodation concerns, the available alternatives and the steps that can be taken to address my stated wish to leave.

2. Your position, your responsibilities and your accountability

Philip, I want a clear account of your role in the decisions affecting my life.

Identify the decisions you personally made, the decisions you authorised, the decisions you reviewed and the decisions made by others for which you had responsibility. Identify the information available to you when those decisions were made and the reasoning recorded at the time.

If you believe your actions were appropriate, explain why by reference to the records. If you believe another person or organisation was responsible, identify that person or organisation and explain the division of responsibility. If errors occurred, acknowledge them and identify the corrective action available.

I will not accept the endless administrative manoeuvre in which every institution points somewhere else until the person affected is exhausted and the original problem remains unresolved.

I am not asking you to agree automatically with every conclusion I have drawn. I am demanding that you engage honestly with the evidence, explain your decisions and take responsibility for matters that fall within your authority.

A public servant’s duty is not merely to protect an institution from criticism. It is to discharge public responsibilities lawfully, fairly and humanely.

Social erasure: the destruction of a person’s place in society

What I describe as social erasure is the progressive stripping away of the conditions that allow a person to participate in society as a recognised human being: their independence, financial means, credibility, ideas, creative work, relationships, rights, opportunities and capacity to make meaningful choices.

A person cannot participate fully in a democracy through abstract rights alone. They also need practical means to live, communicate, travel, obtain assistance, pursue their work and have their concerns heard. When a person is left without adequate financial resources or a realistic route to independence, formal recognition of their rights can become hollow in practice.

I have spent decades contributing through art, journalism, writing, music, advocacy and doctoral research. I have created work, developed ideas and tried to help vulnerable people. Yet I now find myself having to fight for the very conditions that make participation in society possible. I experience that contradiction as a profound betrayal of the democratic principles to which I have devoted my life.

I allege that the cumulative effects of institutional decisions and failures have contributed to this erosion. My academic and forensic writings examine the concepts of administrative annihilation, institutional mobbing, social erosion and the psychology of erasure. They provide a framework for examining the pattern I describe; the specific claims must be assessed against the records, chronology and evidence relevant to each event.

I ask you to consider what happens when the person affected is repeatedly directed from one office to another, when decisions are made without an intelligible explanation, when financial support becomes insufficient to meet ordinary needs, and when no one appears willing to take responsibility for the cumulative outcome. Each event may be described as a separate administrative matter. Together, their effects may be devastating.

14. Faith, conscience and the moral limits of human power

There is another dimension to this matter that cannot be reduced to a financial assessment or a case-management plan: my faith.

I have survived circumstances that I understand through the language of betrayal, suffering, preservation and grace. In my own Christian understanding, I have risen, like a new Christ from the ashes of human betrayal. That is my personal spiritual testimony: an expression of survival, renewal and the conviction that suffering will not have the final word.

I believe that truth does not need permission to exist, while falsehood requires continual maintenance. I believe that a person’s dignity does not depend on the approval of a government department, a trustee, a service provider or a public official.

I also believe that ultimate authority over life belongs to God. No individual, government institution, police service, non-government organisation, trustee or other body has an unlimited moral licence to damage another person, strip away their dignity or treat their continued existence as an administrative inconvenience.

Human authority has limits. A public office does not confer ownership over another human being. Responsibility for managing another person’s affairs does not extinguish that person’s humanity, autonomy, conscience or right to be heard.

I am asking for a reckoning in the moral sense: a serious accounting of decisions, actions, omissions and consequences. In my faith, no comfortable title, institutional reputation or carefully maintained public image can substitute for truth, conscience or accountability before God.

I am not asking you to pronounce a theological judgment. I am telling you the moral framework through which I understand my life and the responsibilities of those who exercise power over it.

If people have made mistakes, those mistakes should be acknowledged and corrected. If decisions have caused avoidable harm, that harm should be examined and addressed. If an institution has failed to listen, it should reconsider its response. If records reveal a different account, those records should be identified and explained.

That is what accountability requires.
