THE METHODOLOGY IS ITSELF SIGNIFICANT

The archive's methodology is itself significant. Rather than relying upon a single narrative or isolated document, The Witness Remains seeks to construct a longitudinal evidentiary environment in which records can be compared across time, institutions, correspondence, chronology, provenance and subsequent events. Where applicable, SHA-256 hashes, Git history, OpenTimestamps material and blockchain-related preservation are used to assist with establishing the integrity and continuity of particular digital records. These mechanisms do not prove the truth of an allegation. They provide an additional means by which the existence, version, provenance and continuity of particular digital material can be examined.

The methodology therefore places PRESERVATION FIRST. EXAMINATION SECOND. CONCLUSION LAST. A document is treated as evidence of what was recorded. Correspondence is treated as evidence of what was communicated. A contemporaneous record may provide material capable of supporting or contradicting an account. Cryptographic preservation may assist with establishing the integrity or continuity of a particular digital version. The evidentiary significance of each item must nevertheless be assessed on its own merits. The archive does not ask the reader to accept a conclusion merely because a document exists. It asks the reader to examine what the document actually establishes.

THE EVIDENTIARY DISTINCTIONS

The archive deliberately distinguishes between documented fact, official record, corroborated material, first-person account, allegation, inference, interpretation, spiritual belief and unresolved matter. This distinction is fundamental to the archive's purpose. A documented fact is not the same thing as an allegation. An official record is not automatically proof that everything stated within it is true. A first-person account records what the author says occurred or was experienced. An inference is a conclusion drawn from available material. An interpretation gives meaning to evidence or events. A spiritual belief belongs to a different category from empirical or documentary proof. An unresolved matter remains unresolved unless and until the evidence permits a stronger conclusion.

The archive is therefore not presented as a court or verdict. The record is preserved so that it can be examined.
