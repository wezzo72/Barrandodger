Evidence Architecture

Follow the Evidence

The archive contains different classes of material. The reader should consider the provenance, context and actual contents of each source.

A

Documented Fact

A proposition directly established by an identifiable source or record.

B

Corroborated Claim

A claim supported by additional relevant material, without necessarily constituting an official determination.

C

Documented Allegation

An allegation recorded in a source. Recording an allegation does not establish that the allegation is true.

D

Inference

An interpretation or conclusion drawn from available material rather than directly established by the source.

E

Speculation

A possibility that remains insufficiently established and should not be presented as established fact.

Faith

Spiritual Belief

Religious belief, prophecy and theological interpretation are identified as faith material rather than documentary evidence.

Impartial Reading

What Does a Source Actually Establish?

The purpose of this archive is not to predetermine the reader's conclusion. It is to preserve material and make it accessible for examination.

Ask of every important proposition:

What is the underlying source?

Who created it?

When was it created?

What does it actually establish?

Is it an official determination, a personal account, an allegation, an inference or an interpretation?

Is there independent corroboration?

What remains disputed or unresolved?

The inclusion of a document, allegation, interpretation or testimony within this archive does not by itself establish the truth of every proposition contained within it.

The archive is therefore intended as a finding aid and preservation project, not as a court, tribunal or verdict.
