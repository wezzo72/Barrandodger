About the Archive

Barran Dodger Archive — public documentary record

Through the Barran Dodger Archive and associated digital repositories, McLean has assembled government correspondence, legal and administrative records, research materials, personal testimony, creative works and other documentary material. The archive is designed not merely as autobiography, but as a public finding aid through which the underlying record can be examined.

The defining feature of the work is not simply what is written. It is what is preserved.

For decades, McLean has accumulated correspondence, government records, legal documents, institutional responses, personal records, research materials and other evidence relating to events that he says fundamentally altered the course of his life. Rather than allowing that material to remain dispersed across agencies, proceedings, correspondence chains and private files, he has sought to bring it together into a searchable and publicly accessible documentary archive.

Purpose

The Barran Dodger Archive is intended to provide readers with access to the underlying record.

Its purpose is not to require belief.

It is to make examination possible.

The archive brings together documentary material alongside McLean’s own testimony, analysis, essays, theological writing and creative work. Where appropriate, the distinction between these categories is made explicit so that readers can determine for themselves what a document actually establishes, what is alleged, what is interpreted and what remains unresolved.

This distinction is fundamental to the project.

A testimony is testimony.
A document is a document.
An allegation is an allegation.
A finding requires evidence and proper determination.

The archive exists so that those distinctions can be examined rather than obscured.

How to read this site

A defining principle is that serious claims should be confronted through the underlying evidence and documentary record. The archive therefore seeks to distinguish between documentary material, personal testimony, interpretation and matters requiring independent determination.

Published material on this site contains serious allegations concerning public institutions and individuals. Those allegations and interpretations are presented as part of testimony and documentary archive and should not be treated as independently established findings unless supported by competent authorities or other independently verified evidence.

Theological and prophetic writings on this site are the author’s spiritual interpretation and testimony, not independently established factual or institutional findings.

Preservation as accountability

Digital preservation here is treated as a form of public accountability.

Documents can be lost.

Correspondence can become inaccessible.

Institutional memory can disappear.

People can die.

Files can become fragmented across jurisdictions and agencies.

But a publicly preserved record can remain available for examination.

That principle underlies the archive’s use of digital repositories, public document catalogues, source links and archival copies.

The objective is simple: keep the record available.

The central question

The archive asks the reader to do something more demanding than simply believe or disbelieve its author.

It asks the reader to look.

Read the correspondence.

Examine the dates.

Open the documents.

Follow the chronology.

Compare the claims with the primary records.

Identify what is established.

Identify what is disputed.

Identify what remains unanswered.

And where the record contradicts the author’s interpretation, say so.

That is the standard this archive seeks to apply to itself.

Philosophy

The record should survive the people who created it.
Preserve the evidence. Preserve the testimony. Make the record public. Let the evidence be examined.

That is the purpose of the Barran Dodger Archive.

Scott Tredwell — 27 March 2023 — acknowledgement and non-protection in the same letter

Scott Tredwell (also written Treadwell), General Counsel, Federal Court of Australia, wrote the final assessment dated 27 March 2023. It is his letter. File: 2023-03-27-federal-court-final-assessment-dr-rich-mclean.pdf. The sentences below are the words preserved from that letter in the primary archive (CaseStudies.tsx).

DSS employee — his sentence

On the information you have provided me, I am satisfied that you are, or were, an employee with the Department of Social Services, providing services under the trading name Rich McLean, Arts Life Coach, Peer-Support Worker & Mental Health Advocate.

Three observations in that same response

  1. Corruption — perverting the course of justice. He recorded that the disclosed conduct “perverts, or is engaged in for the purpose of perverting, the course of justice.”
  2. Maladministration. He recorded that the conduct “constitutes maladministration.”
  3. Harm. He recorded that the conduct “unreasonably results in danger to health or safety.”

WorkCover refused — no legal aid at Comcare or the AAT

I asked for help because corruption was harming me. He answered as a DSS employee. WorkCover did not. Comcare rejected the workers’ compensation claim: determination 26 May 2021, claim 1326583. The AAT / ART decided McLean and Comcare 2021/7478 on 12 July 2023. I had no legal aid at Comcare and no legal aid at the AAT. The compensation path refused me without a lawyer, after the Court’s own General Counsel had written that I was, or had been, a Department of Social Services employee.

The significance of that letter

I asked him for help with corruption that was harming me. In the same letter he acknowledged the employment, and he acknowledged the three things in front of him: perversion of the course of justice, maladministration, and danger to health or safety. Protection was not given in that letter. The acknowledgement and the non-protection are the same document.
