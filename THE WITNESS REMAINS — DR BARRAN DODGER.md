## THE METHODOLOGY IS ITSELF SIGNIFICANT

The archive’s methodology is itself significant. Rather than relying upon a single narrative or isolated document, The Witness Remains seeks to construct a longitudinal evidentiary environment in which records can be compared across time, institutions, correspondence, chronology, provenance and subsequent events. Where applicable, [SHA-256 hashes](https://barrandodgerarchive.base44.app/), [Git history](https://github.com/drbarrandodger), [OpenTimestamps material](https://barrandodgerarchive.base44.app/) and [blockchain-related preservation](https://barrandodgerarchive.base44.app/) are used to assist with establishing the integrity and continuity of particular digital records. These mechanisms do not prove the truth of an allegation. They provide an additional means by which the existence, version, provenance and continuity of particular digital material can be examined.

The methodology therefore places **PRESERVATION FIRST. EXAMINATION SECOND. CONCLUSION LAST.** A document is treated as evidence of what was recorded. Correspondence is treated as evidence of what was communicated. A contemporaneous record may provide material capable of supporting or contradicting an account. Cryptographic preservation may assist with establishing the integrity or continuity of a particular digital version. The evidentiary significance of each item must nevertheless be assessed on its own merits. The archive does not ask the reader to accept a conclusion merely because a document exists. It asks the reader to examine what the document actually establishes.

## THE EVIDENTIARY DISTINCTIONS

The archive deliberately distinguishes between [documented fact](https://barrandodgerarchive.base44.app/), [official record](https://barrandodgerarchive.base44.app/), [corroborated material](https://barrandodgerarchive.base44.app/), [first-person account](https://barrandodgerarchive.base44.app/), [allegation](https://barrandodgerarchive.base44.app/), [inference](https://barrandodgerarchive.base44.app/), [interpretation](https://barrandodgerarchive.base44.app/), [spiritual belief](https://barrandodgerarchive.base44.app/) and [unresolved matter](https://barrandodgerarchive.base44.app/). This distinction is fundamental to the archive’s purpose. A documented fact is not the same thing as an allegation. An official record is not automatically proof that everything stated within it is true. A first-person account records what the author says occurred or was experienced. An inference is a conclusion drawn from available material. An interpretation gives meaning to evidence or events. A spiritual belief belongs to a different category from empirical or documentary proof. An unresolved matter remains unresolved unless and until the evidence permits a stronger conclusion.

The archive is therefore not presented as a [court](https://barrandodgerarchive.base44.app/) or [verdict](https://barrandodgerarchive.base44.app/). The record is preserved so that it can be examined.

Its central proposition is not that publication itself proves every allegation. Rather, the archive preserves the surviving material so that claims can be tested against contemporaneous evidence, with corroboration, contradiction, uncertainty and gaps capable of being identified.

The archive therefore distinguishes between [documented fact](https://barrandodgerarchive.base44.app/), [official record](https://barrandodgerarchive.base44.app/), [corroborated material](https://barrandodgerarchive.base44.app/), [first-person account](https://barrandodgerarchive.base44.app/), [allegation](https://barrandodgerarchive.base44.app/), [inference](https://barrandodgerarchive.base44.app/), [interpretation](https://barrandodgerarchive.base44.app/), [spiritual belief](https://barrandodgerarchive.base44.app/) and matters that remain [unresolved](https://barrandodgerarchive.base44.app/). It does not purport to be a [court](https://barrandodgerarchive.base44.app/) or a [verdict](https://barrandodgerarchive.base44.app/). A document can establish what was recorded. Correspondence can establish what was communicated. A contemporaneous record can provide evidence capable of supporting or contradicting an account. None of these, by themselves, automatically proves the truth of an allegation or a theological proposition.

The broader purpose is preservation: to prevent documentary disappearance through fragmentation, institutional closure, inaccessible records, disputed histories and the passage of time. The archive preserves both the documentary record and the author’s first-person testimony so that they can be examined alongside one another. Alongside its documentary purpose, the archive contains a Christian and spiritual interpretation of survival, providence, justice and testimony. The archive ultimately asks the reader neither simply to believe nor disbelieve, but to examine the record: open the documents, follow the chronology, compare accounts, identify corroboration, identify contradiction, distinguish evidence from interpretation, identify what remains unresolved, and allow the surviving evidence to speak for itself.
