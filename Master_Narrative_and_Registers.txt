5. The inversion of power

Institutions have staff, files, procedures, resources and established credibility. I describe being left to retrieve correspondence, follow reference numbers, prepare submissions, preserve evidence and explain the cumulative history while struggling with illness and limited means. That is the inversion I want examined: a person asking for help carries the administrative burden of proving why help is needed.

Authority creates responsibility. A referral is not the same as a substantive assessment; an acknowledgment is not the same as practical protection; a form is not the same as assistance completing it. Each has a role, but none should be mistaken for another. The question is what happened to the person after each procedural step.
