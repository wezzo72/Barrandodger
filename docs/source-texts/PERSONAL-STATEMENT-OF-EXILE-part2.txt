THE EVIDENCE HIERARCHY

Where possible, I want readers to distinguish between different levels of evidentiary status.

A — Established fact

A court, tribunal or other authoritative body has made a finding or determination.

B — Corroborated claim

The claim is supported by multiple independent records or sources.

C — Documented allegation

The allegation is recorded in correspondence, a complaint, statement, transcript or other documentary material.

D — Inference

The proposition is an interpretation drawn from documented circumstances rather than a direct finding.

E — Speculation

The proposition requires further evidence before it can reasonably be established.

I also distinguish between documentary evidence, corroboration, narrative accounts and analytical interpretation.

My objective is therefore not to ask readers simply to believe me.

It is to ask them to test the documentary record.

PURPOSE OF THE ARCHIVE

I publish these materials because I believe that allegations of serious institutional wrongdoing should be capable of independent examination.

I have attempted to preserve the documentary record so that the chronology can be reconstructed and individual allegations can be assessed against the available evidence.

Where the evidence establishes something, it should be identified as established.

Where it merely records my allegation, it should be identified as an allegation.

Where the evidence supports a claim but does not independently establish it, it should be identified as corroboration rather than proof.

Where the conclusion is an inference, it should be identified as an inference.

Where the evidence remains unresolved, it should remain unresolved.
