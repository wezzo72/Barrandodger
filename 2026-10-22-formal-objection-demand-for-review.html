I reject any suggestion that my willingness to challenge public institutions, preserve evidence or speak forcefully about my experiences is itself a sufficient reason to discount my testimony or restrict my independence.

I have earned the right to have my words read on their merits. My academic work, creative contributions, advocacy and documentary record are part of my life. They do not make me infallible, but neither do the difficulties I have experienced entitle anyone to disregard me.

I will not accept an arrangement in which unsubstantiated assumptions about me carry more weight than the documentary record, while the people making decisions remain unwilling to explain their reasoning.

If you believe my allegations are mistaken, then engage with them. If the evidence contradicts my account, show me the relevant evidence. If your decisions were lawful, reasonable and consistent with your responsibilities, explain the basis for them.

That is the challenge I place before you.

I invite you to prove me wrong—not through silence, not through another formulaic paragraph, and not by referring me endlessly to another department, but through a substantive response grounded in records, reasons and accountable decision-making.

Your professional integrity is demonstrated not by your capacity to defend the system automatically, but by your willingness to examine whether the system has acted properly when its conduct is challenged.

I believe I have been failed by people and institutions from which I was entitled to expect assistance. I have been left fighting for financial security, accommodation, access to support and the ability to live with dignity.

I refuse to accept that this is an inevitable or acceptable outcome.

I have a choice too. I can continue to preserve the evidence, speak about my experience, pursue the available review mechanisms and insist that those exercising authority over my life explain themselves.

That is what I intend to do.

My faith does not teach me to submit to injustice in silence. It gives me the strength to confront it without surrendering my humanity.

I believe God’s grace has sustained me through circumstances that I regard as betrayal. I believe truth deserves to be brought into the light. And I believe that those entrusted with public responsibilities should be willing to account for what they have done, what they have failed to do and what they are prepared to do now.

You now have the opportunity to answer the evidence, explain your decisions and demonstrate that your integrity remains independent of institutional self-protection.

I expect you to take that opportunity seriously.

15. Faith, conscience and the moral limits of human power

There is another dimension to this matter that cannot be reduced to a financial assessment or a case-management plan: my faith.

I have survived circumstances that I understand through the language of betrayal, suffering, preservation and grace. In my own Christian understanding, I have risen, like a new Christ from the ashes of human betrayal. That is my personal spiritual testimony: an expression of survival, renewal and the conviction that suffering will not have the final word.

I believe that truth does not need permission to exist, while falsehood requires continual maintenance. I believe that a person’s dignity does not depend on the approval of a government department, a trustee, a service provider or a public official.

My God does not harm me. His grace protects me. His favour is upon my life.

I also believe that ultimate authority over life belongs to God. No individual, government institution, police service, non-government organisation, trustee or other body has an unlimited moral licence to damage another person, strip away their dignity or treat their continued existence as an administrative inconvenience.

Human authority has limits. A public office does not confer ownership over another human being. Responsibility for managing another person’s affairs does not extinguish that person’s humanity, autonomy, conscience or right to be heard.

I am asking for a reckoning in the moral sense: a serious accounting of decisions, actions, omissions and consequences. In my faith, no comfortable title, institutional reputation or carefully maintained public image can substitute for truth, conscience or accountability before God.

I am not asking you to pronounce a theological judgment. I am telling you the moral framework through which I understand my life and the responsibilities of those who exercise power over it.

If people have made mistakes, those mistakes should be acknowledged and corrected. If decisions have caused avoidable harm, that harm should be examined and addressed. If an institution has failed to listen, it should reconsider its response. If records reveal a different account, those records should be identified and explained.

That is what accountability requires.

16. The contradiction of political exile within my own democracy

I now find myself describing my circumstances as political exile within my own citizenship: a citizen seeking safety, independence and recognition in the country to which he belongs.

That is an absurd and profound injustice as I experience it.

I allege that the cumulative treatment I have experienced has marginalised me, damaged my independence and left me fighting for basic security. I believe that institutional responses have contributed to this outcome. I want the underlying decisions and records examined rather than having my concerns dismissed merely because the overall pattern is complicated or because responsibility is distributed across different organisations.

I have contributed to this society. I have not forfeited my dignity because I have become difficult to accommodate administratively, because I have challenged decisions, or because I insist upon preserving a record of my experience.

I will not accept the idea that my contributions can be taken, my independence restricted and my testimony buried without a meaningful explanation.

18. The final word: truth, accountability and the record

Philip, this letter is an objection to more than a single decision. It is an objection to the possibility that an individual’s life can be fragmented into administrative categories until no one feels responsible for the whole person.

I refuse to accept that outcome as inevitable.

I have spent years preserving documents, reconstructing timelines, examining correspondence and creating an archive because I believe that records matter. I have sought to turn an overwhelming personal history into something that can be read, checked, questioned and understood. My archive is an attempt to preserve my testimony and the documentary trail surrounding it. It is not a court judgment, and I do not present every interpretation as an established finding. It is a record that deserves to be examined on its contents.

I ask you to consider the human being behind that record: a person who has contributed through art, writing, music, advocacy and scholarship; a person who has experienced profound distress and financial hardship; a person who wants independence, a safe place to live and a meaningful response to the issues raised; and a person whose faith has helped sustain him through circumstances he regards as betrayal.

I will not be silenced by the assumption that a public institution is entitled to the last word simply because it controls the file. I will not confuse institutional power with moral authority. And I will not accept administrative indifference as an adequate answer to human suffering.

I have a policy too. I do not give you permission to harm me, cause my detriment and then pretend that your actions are justified simply because they can be described in bureaucratic language.

I believe that public money should support human welfare, not insulate public institutions from accountability. I believe that a person entrusted with responsibility for another person’s affairs has an obligation to explain how that responsibility has been exercised.

I invite you to prove me wrong. Examine the evidence. Explain your decisions. Identify the records. Correct any errors. Address any substantiated failures. Demonstrate through your actions that your responsibilities mean something more than compliance with a process.

In my Christian understanding, there is a reckoning greater than any internal review, management meeting or government response. I believe that every person is ultimately accountable for how they exercise power, how they treat the vulnerable and whether they choose truth over self-protection.

That is the divine reckoning to which I refer—not a threat against any individual, but a statement of conscience and faith.

My God does not harm me. His grace protects me. His favour is upon my life.

I survived humanity’s betrayal as I understand and have experienced it. I survived without the assistance I believe I needed, and in spite of the circumstances I have repeatedly challenged. My survival does not excuse anyone from examining those circumstances. It does not turn deprivation into care, silence into an answer or institutional power into moral authority.

I am still here. I am still speaking. I am still preserving the record.

I have given this democracy my creativity, my intellect, my labour and decades of my life. I did so in good faith. I now ask whether its institutions are prepared to extend to me the dignity, fairness, transparency and humanity that their public responsibilities demand.

My life is not your spreadsheet.
