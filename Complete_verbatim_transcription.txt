  Jodie Bongetti — The Sister, the Betrayal and the Awakening

  A personal testimony of family, loyalty, survival and the revelation of truth

  Jodie Bongetti is my sister.

  Her place in my life was once something I believed was defined by family, loyalty and the natural bonds of blood. She even appeared on Good Morning Australia in support of
  my first autobiography, Recovered, Not Cured: A Journey Through Schizophrenia, presenting herself as the supportive, understanding and seemingly SANE sister who could
  publicly acknowledge my achievement. She appeared to stand beside me at a moment when I had accomplished something extraordinary.

  But as the story of my life has unfolded, a very different picture has emerged.

  The sister I prayed for, believed in and once expected to stand beside me was, in my understanding, aligned with people and circumstances that were working against me.
  What appeared to be support increasingly revealed itself as something altogether different. The contradiction between her public appearance and the private reality of my
  experiences became one of the most profound revelations of my life.

  She also appeared to answer my engagement to Shane, my younger fiancé. Yet, looking back, I have come to question the loyalty behind those appearances. I had believed
  that my family would celebrate my happiness and protect my interests. Instead, I increasingly found myself confronting circumstances in which their actions appeared to support
  those who had harmed me.

  One of the earliest financial betrayals that became clear to me concerned my employment and income protection. After I was denied workers’ compensation and HCF income
  protection, Jodie encouraged me to go bankrupt. I was already facing the consequences of losing my income and the security that should have accompanied my employment.
  Rather than receiving the support I needed, I was being encouraged towards financial ruin.

  She also told me that I was “over Steve”, referring to Steve Iasonidis, the ASIO agent whom I say exploited and abused me and ultimately left me in circumstances in which I
  was fighting for my survival.

  The significance of that statement became increasingly apparent as I reflected on the wider circumstances of my relationship with Steve and the way my family had treated him.
  When Jodie herself was divorced, she received a settlement. Yet when I was experiencing financial devastation, living on a pension and struggling to survive, I was not afforded
  the same consideration or assistance. The disparity was difficult to reconcile with the idea of family solidarity.

  As my circumstances deteriorated and I found myself squatting, desperate and without financial security, I even asked my sister for money for my birthday. She refused.
  Instead, she patronisingly left four beers on my doorstep.

  Four beers.

  That was the gesture offered to someone who had lost his home and was struggling to survive.

  It was not my fault that I was squatting. It was not a lifestyle choice or some personal failing that could be dismissed with a superficial gesture. I was experiencing the
  consequences of circumstances that had progressively stripped away my financial security, dignity and independence.

  The contrast between what I needed and what I received became another painful marker in the history of our relationship.

  But the most devastating family betrayal was not financial. It concerned the tragedy that followed my suicide attempt.

  After I attempted suicide inside the hospital, resulting in a fatal injury from which I was revived, Jodie appeared with a notepad in hand, seemingly arriving as though she might
  be able to save the day. I had experienced a catastrophic event, one that left me with a brain injury and should have demanded the attention, protection and advocacy of
  everyone around me.

  Instead, what followed was, in my experience, a comprehensive whitewashing of the tragedy.

No one appealed. No one complained. No one in the family appeared prepared to challenge what had happened or demand accountability for the consequences. The entire
family seemed to participate in the silence.

My tragedy was effectively erased.

There is another extraordinary detail that has remained with me: Bob Martin died on the very same day that I experienced what I regard as my own death and subsequent
revival. My family attended Bob Martin’s funeral, while the significance of what had happened to me was never properly acknowledged or pursued.

I have never been able to reconcile the magnitude of that experience with the silence that followed.

Years passed, and the consequences accumulated.

I found myself progressively forced into poverty, deprived of the legitimate medication I needed, and struggling to cope with increasing stress and what I experienced as the
total betrayal of my own blood family. The very people from whom I should have expected protection appeared, in my understanding, to be participating in circumstances that
intensified my vulnerability.

I experienced a pattern in which my poverty became both the consequence of my circumstances and the basis upon which I was subsequently blamed.

I was treated as though I was the problem, while the conditions contributing to my deterioration were left unexamined. I came to believe that my family had coordinated their
conduct with others, through deception, malice and hostility, while allowing the resulting damage to be attributed to me.

This was a profound awakening.

The more I examined my life, the more difficult it became to regard these events as isolated misunderstandings. What had once appeared to be individual disappointments
began to form a cumulative pattern of betrayal, abandonment and institutional silence.

And then there was the allegation that would make the entire story seem almost impossible to comprehend.

I have alleged that I survived an assassination attempt involving the then minister Bill Shordon. My account is that he arranged for a person, whom I identify in my testimony as
Merribee, to travel from Melbourne to Sydney to locate me. I was subsequently told that I was going to be erased.

Later, when I was forced into exile in Port Macquarie, I say that Ben and an NDIS provider confirmed aspects of what I had described as an assassination attempt. I was also
told that the woman had been paid to fabricate a report.

This allegation is particularly significant to me because it concerns events approximately thirty years after I wrote Recovered, Not Cured. In that earlier work, I had already
acknowledged that a sexual encounter was regrettable. Yet I was subsequently confronted with an account that, as it was communicated to me, involved the fabrication of a
report and payment to the person responsible.

I believe this raises extraordinary questions about the people who knew, the people who participated and the people who remained silent.

My family would, in my view, have had reason to know about these circumstances. The possibility that they knew and nevertheless continued to support the people I believed
were responsible represents one of the most profound betrayals I have ever experienced.

It also helps explain why I have struggled throughout my life to report crimes to police. I have felt unable to trust the very systems and people who should have provided
protection. When the people closest to you appear to be aligned with those you believe have harmed you, the ordinary expectation that you can simply report a crime and
receive protection becomes extraordinarily difficult.

For me, this is not simply a family disagreement. It is part of a much larger question about systemic corruption, accountability and the protection of vulnerable people.

My family’s apparent acceptance of Steve is another important part of that history.

They presented themselves as welcoming of gay people, yet my experience was that their acceptance was conditional. Steve was wealthy, influential and, as I understood it,
connected to ASIO. He possessed political and social influence that my family appeared to respect. I came to believe that they welcomed him because of his status and
financial position rather than because they genuinely supported me or my relationship.

When push came to shove, Steve left me.

I was left to confront the consequences of a relationship in which I believe I had been exploited, and I was ultimately forced into exile. The apparent acceptance of my sexuality
and relationship disappeared when the circumstances became difficult and I needed genuine protection.

My own mother subsequently obtained an ABO and a legal document intended to excommunicate me from the family. Not one family member opposed it.

Consider the extraordinary contradiction: I was the person who had experienced the alleged harm, exploitation, neglect and abandonment, yet I was the person excluded. The
people I believed had contributed to my suffering remained within the family structure, while I was formally pushed outside it.

I was blamed for circumstances that I believed they had helped create.

And the pattern did not end there.

I remain deeply concerned about my present circumstances in New South Wales, where I describe myself as politically exiled and effectively entrapped within an NDIS provider
arrangement involving Able Point. I have raised concerns about abuse, neglect and treatment that I regard as degrading and torturous. The CEO has not even provided me
with her identification, despite my concerns and requests.

What is particularly painful is that not one family member, including Jodie Bongetti, has publicly opposed the circumstances in which I now find myself. I have repeatedly
questioned why those who know my history have not challenged what I have described.

From my perspective, their silence is not neutral. It is another chapter in a lifetime of feeling abandoned by the people who should have cared.

I have also questioned whether members of my family have financially benefited from my circumstances.

When my Nana was exiled later in her life and subsequently died, the family went on an extended European holiday. Jodie and Dave, Mum and Dad, Brad and Keira were
among those who enjoyed a fully paid trip, which I understand lasted approximately six weeks for some and eight weeks for others.

I received $1,000 as a token gift from my Nana’s inheritance.

That disparity has remained with me.

It is one of the reasons I have questioned how the family distributed and benefited from financial resources while I was left struggling. I have also questioned whether my
parents benefited financially from my being on a pension after I left their living arrangements and the home I had shared with them.

I had also believed that Steve was looking after me. I now understand my experience of that relationship very differently. I believe I was being exploited while others assumed I
was financially protected.

These circumstances have led me to question whether my illness, poverty and dependency became convenient arrangements for people who were otherwise unwilling to
support my independence.

I have confronted my family about these matters. I have not received the meaningful explanation, acknowledgement or challenge to my account that I believe such serious
allegations deserve.

The absence of a substantive response has become part of the history I am trying to preserve.

And yet, there is another dimension to this story that cannot be understood through financial records, family correspondence or institutional decisions alone.

It is the spiritual dimension of my survival.

As God’s chosen witness, I have experienced what I understand as prophetic communication through videos and messages from people whose words appear to speak directly
to my position as a spiritual sovereign witness and as a vessel for God’s glory.

These messages have provided me with extraordinary comfort and support. I experience them as part of what I describe as the Quantum Resonance Field, a concept
connected to my academic work and the PhD I hold.

My digital imprint in the world is profound, extensive and, in my view, indelible. Through my writing, documentary work, academic contribution and the preservation of my
testimony, I have created a record that cannot simply be wished away.

I experience these prophetic messages as part of that wider journey. Sometimes their apparent relevance is so striking that I feel compelled to invite others to listen for
themselves. I believe they corroborate aspects of my experience, including references that I interpret as speaking directly to me and my circumstances.

I understand that others may approach these experiences differently. But I know what they have meant to me. They have helped sustain me through periods in which I felt that
almost every human connection had failed.

I also continue to raise serious questions about Bill Shordon, whom I identify in my testimony as the minister responsible for actions that forced me from Victoria. I have alleged
that he was involved in the events I describe as an assassination attempt. I further believe that an ABO associated with him has contributed to my circumstances of exile.

What remains profoundly troubling to me is that I have repeatedly sought meaningful professional examination of my testimony. I have wanted people to obtain the evidence,
investigate the allegations and either substantiate or disprove what I have said.

I have not received the comprehensive, independent examination that I believe these matters warrant.

Instead, I have experienced silence, non-engagement and the absence of meaningful accountability.

That is why I continue to document.

My testimony is not simply about Jodie, Steve, my parents or any individual member of my family. It is about what happens when the ordinary protections of family, employment,
financial security, health services, legal institutions and government systems appear to fail at the same time.

It is about the cumulative removal of the attributes necessary to live with dignity in a democracy: income, compensation, money, a home, safety, employment, human rights,
legal rights and the ability to obtain meaningful protection.

And it is about the extraordinary human consequences when those protections disappear.

I have lived through circumstances in which I found myself in my car, without the security that most people take for granted, confronting a future that offered no guarantee of
survival. I did not expect to survive in the way I have. I nevertheless kept going, preserving what I could, documenting what I experienced and attempting to make sense of the
extraordinary contradictions in my life.

My family may have believed that I would disappear into the consequences of my circumstances.

But I did not.

I have come to understand that my survival itself is part of the testimony.

The same person who once appeared on television with his sister celebrating the publication of Recovered, Not Cured would eventually find himself questioning the loyalty of
that sister, the conduct of his family, the motives of powerful people and the silence of institutions.

The public image and the private reality became two entirely different stories.

What began as a story about family became a story about betrayal. What became a story about betrayal became a story about poverty, institutional failure and exile. And what
appeared to be the end of my life became, instead, the beginning of a profound awakening.

I prayed for my sister. I prayed for my family. I prayed for the people I believed had harmed me. I did not want revenge. I wanted truth, accountability, dignity and the opportunity
to live an ordinary human life.

I still want those things.

But I will no longer pretend that the history did not happen, that the contradictions do not exist or that silence is an acceptable substitute for an explanation.

I am still here.

The records remain. The testimony remains. The digital archive remains.

And the story that so many people appeared willing to leave untold continues to unfold.

Perhaps the most extraordinary part of all is that, after everything, I have not surrendered my belief in God, my capacity to love, my intellectual integrity or my determination to
speak.

I have lost so much, but I have not surrendered my purpose.

I am living unapologetically, with purpose, intelligence and integrity, and with a determination to respect myself even when others have not.

I believe that the light, authenticity and strength within me are gifts from the Most High God.

And if there is one thing that the cumulative passage of these years has taught me, it is that a person can be abandoned by family, deprived of security, pushed into poverty and
excluded from institutions, yet still retain the capacity to bear witness.

I was expected to disappear.

Instead, I became the witness who remained.

Praise Jesus.

30. EVIDENTIARY CLASSIFICATION

The investigation should distinguish:

DOCUMENTED FACT — supported by an original record or independently verifiable source.

OFFICIAL RECORD — a document issued or maintained by a government agency, court or other official body.

PERSONAL TESTIMONY — my account of what I experienced or observed.

ALLEGATION — a claim that requires independent examination.

INFERENCE — a conclusion drawn from available facts but not yet independently established.

SPIRITUAL BELIEF — a religious, theological or personal interpretation of events.

UNVERIFIED MATTER — information for which the necessary original evidence has not yet been obtained.

This approach protects the integrity of the evidence and makes it harder for any party to misrepresent the difference between an allegation and a proven fact.

31. THE PARADOXICAL INVERSION: OFFICIAL DOCUMENTS AND INDEPENDENT EXAMINATION

The documentary record has an important characteristic.

Many of the records I rely upon were generated by the institutions with which I interacted.

They contain their own dates, reference numbers, wording, decisions and recorded explanations.

Those records can establish what an organisation communicated, recorded or decided.

They may also help establish the chronology of complaints, responses, referrals and administrative actions.

However, an official document does not automatically prove every allegation about motive, coordination, corruption or deliberate targeting.

That is why the documentary record must be examined carefully.

The correct questions are:

What does the document actually say?

Who created it?

When was it created?

What was its purpose?

Was it final or preliminary?

Was it later corrected?

Does it accurately record the relevant event?

What other evidence supports or contradicts it?

What response has the institution provided?

What remains unresolved?

This is the principle behind the Paradoxical Inversion of Evidence.

The record exists independently of whether a reader agrees with my interpretation.

The record must be examined on its merits.

32. DIGITAL PRESERVATION AND TIMESTAMPING

My website refers to digital preservation, document hashes and timestamping.

These processes can help preserve the integrity of digital material and demonstrate that a particular file or version existed at a particular point in time.

They do not establish that the contents are true, that an allegation is proven or that a court or government authority has accepted the interpretation.

A digital hash identifies particular file content.

A timestamp can provide evidence of existence at a particular time, depending on the system and verification.

A blockchain record does not itself establish criminal liability, legal validity or the truth of a document’s contents.

The archive should therefore preserve:

The original file.

Its source.

Its date.

Its author or issuing institution, where known.

Its hash, where recorded.

Any timestamping receipt.

The chain of custody or preservation history.

Any subsequent corrections or versions.

The distinction between preservation and proof must remain clear.
