1. The letter that contains the inversion

Federal Court of Australia, Principal Registry, 27 March 2023, Scott Tredwell, General Counsel, to Dr Rich McLean. Open the PDF.

On the information you have provided me, I am satisfied that you are, or were, an employee with the Department of Social Services, providing services under the trading name Rich McLean, Arts Life Coach, Peer-Support Worker & Mental Health Advocate.

The same letter then finds that the conduct disclosed “does not relate in any way to the Federal Court”, and that the disclosure was not provided to an authorised recipient. The decision paragraph is wider than that finding:

As I am not satisfied that the disclosure made in your correspondence and other information which was received by the Federal Court and FCFCOA was made by a public official or provided to an authorised recipient under the PID Act, there is no reasonable basis on which I can decide that the disclosure is an internal disclosure… I must decide… not to allocate the handling of that disclosure to any agency. This means that no further action under the PID Act will be taken by the Federal Court or FCFCOA, or any other Commonwealth agency on the correspondence and other information you provided.

Read both sentences. Page 2 says satisfied of DSS employment. The decision says not satisfied the disclosure was made by a public official, or not satisfied it was given to an authorised recipient. The letter does not explain how both can stand. A complaint path to the Commonwealth Ombudsman is stated. No other agency is told. 8. What is quotation, and what is the office

The PDF filed as the DSS PID rejection is, on the pages read, Dr McLean’s own email of 22 July 2023 and 1 August 2023, in which he pastes words he attributes to an authorised officer of the Department of Social Services: no record he had been a current or former employee, not satisfied he is a public official, matter not allocated. Those words are in his email. The separate DSS letter was not the file opened. His email also says the Federal Court affirmed that he was employed by DSS. Tredwell’s letter does say “satisfied that you are, or were, an employee” of DSS. It also decides not to allocate. The two sentences are both on the Court letter. They are not a finding that DSS agreed.

FOI/2022/045IC. The page that is an office email, 8 October 2024, from FOI@pmc.gov.au, says a revised decision under the Freedom of Information Act 1982 is attached, and that the matter is under review by the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner, OAIC ref MR22/00677. The later pages of the same PDF are a press release and an annex written in his voice about what those documents mean. That commentary is his. It is not the Department’s decision. The Department’s email is the evidence that a revised decision was sent and that an IC review number exists.

9. What this weight is, and what it is not

Counted from pages read, not from a list of filenames: Legal Aid NSW, the NDIS Commission (twice), Comcare, the Administrative Appeals Tribunal (twice), the Law Enforcement Conduct Commission, the Commonwealth Ombudsman (PID review, and a later contact restriction), the Victorian Ombudsman, the Federal Court, AFCA, the Australian Federal Police (an acknowledgement only), DXC for WorkSafe, NSW Trustee and Guardian, and the Department of the Prime Minister and Cabinet’s FOI section. Fifteen letterheads. They wrote. Several closed the door they had opened. None of the decision paragraphs read in this pass cites his public archive, a video, or a book, or answers the archive as one case.

That is the antithesis the papers support. The Trustee certificate puts the estate under management. Comcare and the NDIS Commission say he is not their employee. The Court says it is satisfied of an employment and then does not allocate. The Ombudsman counts 229 emails and blocks the addresses. Low control of his own estate, and high traffic of official paper, are both on documents. The denial that has evidentiary weight is not a slogan. It is the operative sentence: not an employee, outside jurisdiction, not allocated, affirmed, declined, restricted.

It is not a finding that those offices met and agreed. No minute of that kind was in the letters. It is not a wage, and it is not the prior model of billions. data/costs.json remains null. A signed assessment that he is an institutional threat was not in these letters. Tredwell records that the correspondent wanted struggles “acknowledged and compensated”, and then did not allocate.

Still not in the files searched under these names in Backup: a document titled Moorabbin, a Cassie text, a signed lease, a service agreement, or a court order titled media ban. Absence from that search is absence from that search. It is not proof those pages exist nowhere under another name.
