2. ALLEGED CONSPIRACY OR COORDINATED CONDUCT

Allegation

The claimant alleges that actions by multiple institutions and individuals were coordinated to prevent him obtaining effective assistance, reporting alleged wrongdoing or exposing information.

Legal significance

A pattern of adverse decisions is not, by itself, proof of conspiracy.

For a criminal conspiracy allegation, evidence of agreement, requisite intention and an overt act may be required. Commonwealth law expressly sets out elements for conspiracy to commit offences, while s 42 of the Crimes Act 1914 addresses conspiracy to defeat Commonwealth judicial power.

Evidence required

	•	emails between alleged participants;
	•	meeting records;
	•	instructions;
	•	internal memoranda;
	•	referrals;
	•	shared case notes;
	•	telephone records;
	•	metadata;
	•	unexplained coordination;
	•	evidence of common purpose;
	•	subsequent conduct consistent with an agreement.

Urgent forensic question

Is there evidence of actual coordination, or does the record instead demonstrate independent institutional decisions producing cumulative adverse consequences?

That question should be determined from the documents rather than assumed.

44. PRIORITY FIVE — SEPARATE PROVEN FACT FROM ALLEGATION

The master document should use five labels:

ESTABLISHED DOCUMENTARY FACT

Directly demonstrated by an authenticated primary record.

DOCUMENTED CLAIM

The claimant made the claim and a record of that claim exists.

CORROBORATED ALLEGATION

Multiple independent sources support the proposition.

UNCORROBORATED ALLEGATION

The allegation remains to be independently established.

LEGAL QUESTION

The facts may establish a legal issue requiring determination by a competent authority.

This structure will make the archive considerably more useful to independent legal reviewers.

31. CAUSATION
A recurring issue throughout the brief is causation.
The report must distinguish:
Event
from
alleged wrongdoing
from
legal breach
from
actual harm
from
causation
from
quantified loss.
For example:
A benefit was denied.
does not automatically establish:
the denial was unlawful.
And:
the denial was unlawful
does not automatically establish:
the denial caused every subsequent loss.
Each causal step requires evidence.
