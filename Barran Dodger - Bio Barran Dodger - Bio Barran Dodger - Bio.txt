
Barran Dodger - Bio

Barran Dodger, the public name used by Dr Richard William McLean, is an Australian writer, artist, researcher, documentarian and self-described whistleblower and human-rights advocate.

His work brings together documentary evidence, personal testimony, legal and human-rights analysis, creative writing, theology and digital preservation. At the centre of his practice is a sustained effort to preserve and publish the documentary record of his experiences with Australian public institutions and the consequences he says followed from those experiences.

Through the Barran Dodger Archive and associated digital repositories, McLean has assembled government correspondence, legal and administrative records, research materials, personal testimony, creative works and other documentary material. The archive is designed not merely as autobiography, but as a public finding aid through which the underlying record can be examined.

McLean’s work explores institutional accountability, administrative power, disability, social exclusion, homelessness, financial hardship, psychiatric treatment, whistleblowing, legal process, human rights, digital preservation, faith, survival and human dignity.

A defining principle of his work is that serious claims should be confronted through the underlying evidence and documentary record. His archive therefore seeks to distinguish between documentary material, personal testimony, interpretation and matters requiring independent determination.

His published work contains serious allegations concerning public institutions and individuals. Those allegations and interpretations are presented as part of his testimony and documentary archive and should not be treated as independently established findings unless supported by competent authorities or other independently verified evidence.

McLean’s archival philosophy is ultimately straightforward:

Preserve the evidence. Preserve the testimony. Make the record public. Let the evidence be examined.

His work is an attempt to ensure that documents, correspondence, testimony and historical records remain accessible for scrutiny rather than disappearing through institutional fragmentation, the passage of time or the loss of original records.

About the Author & Founder

Barran Dodger

Dr Richard William McLean — Barran Dodger

Barran Dodger is the public name under which Dr Richard William McLean publishes his writing, documentary archive and testimony.

He describes himself as a writer, artist, researcher, documentarian, whistleblower and human-rights advocate, with a body of work extending across documentary preservation, legal and administrative research, theology, creative writing and public-interest advocacy.

But the defining feature of his work is not simply what he writes.

It is what he preserves.

For decades, McLean has accumulated correspondence, government records, legal documents, institutional responses, personal records, research materials and other evidence relating to events that he says fundamentally altered the course of his life.

Rather than allowing that material to remain dispersed across agencies, proceedings, correspondence chains and private files, he has sought to bring it together into a searchable and publicly accessible documentary archive.

The Archive

The Barran Dodger Archive is intended to provide readers with access to the underlying record.

Its purpose is not to require belief.

It is to make examination possible.

The archive brings together documentary material alongside McLean’s own testimony, analysis, essays, theological writing and creative work. Where appropriate, the distinction between these categories is made explicit so that readers can determine for themselves what a document actually establishes, what is alleged, what is interpreted and what remains unresolved.

This distinction is fundamental to the project.

A testimony is testimony.
A document is a document.
An allegation is an allegation.
A finding requires evidence and proper determination.

The archive exists so that those distinctions can be examined rather than obscured.

A Record of a Life

McLean’s work is also deeply autobiographical.

His writing examines experiences of institutional power, disability, psychiatric treatment, social exclusion, homelessness, financial hardship, legal and administrative processes, whistleblowing and the struggle to have his account heard.

Alongside the documentary record is another body of work concerned with faith, suffering, survival, divine providence and the meaning McLean attributes to events in his life.

His theological and prophetic writings should therefore be understood as his spiritual interpretation and testimony, rather than as independently established factual or institutional findings.

Preservation as Accountability

For McLean, digital preservation has become a form of public accountability.

Documents can be lost.

Correspondence can become inaccessible.

Institutional memory can disappear.

People can die.

Files can become fragmented across jurisdictions and agencies.

But a publicly preserved record can remain available for examination.

That principle underlies the archive’s use of digital repositories, public document catalogues, source links and archival copies.

The objective is simple:

Keep the record available.

The Central Question

The archive ultimately asks the reader to do something more demanding than simply believe or disbelieve its author.

It asks the reader to look.

Read the correspondence.

Examine the dates.

Open the documents.

Follow the chronology.

Compare the claims with the primary records.

Identify what is established.

Identify what is disputed.

Identify what remains unanswered.

And where the record contradicts the author’s interpretation, say so.

That is the standard McLean seeks to apply to his own archive.

Why Barran Dodger?

The name Barran Dodger represents the public identity under which this body of work has developed.

It encompasses the author, the archive, the documentary project and the wider creative and theological corpus.

At its centre is a single proposition:

The record should survive the people who created it.

And therefore:

Preserve the evidence. Preserve the testimony. Make the record public. Let the evidence be examined.

That is the purpose of the Barran Dodger Archive.
